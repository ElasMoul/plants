Minimap

The world view is the small map of your whole garden that never leaves the corner of the screen. This page settles what it draws, what it deliberately refuses to react to, and how it keeps answering where am I at every zoom and in words as well as in light.

What this is

Your garden is bigger than the screen. When the camera is close in on one fig, you can see three or four of its neighbours and nothing else — and if that were all there was, you would have no idea whether the rest of your plants were up, down, near or far. The world view is the answer: the whole garden shrunk into one small frame, one small mark per body, a dashed rectangle showing how much of it you are currently looking at, and a lit dot on the one you are standing in front of. Glance at it and you know where you are, without hovering and without reading.

It is a member of the chrome register, so it is fixed to the corner of the viewport and takes no shadow — the register itself is settled on chrome, and this page does not restate it. What is settled here is the map's own behaviour: it is drawn from the same records the plane is drawn from, and it deliberately ignores the camera.

The plane and its camera belong to world; the cards belong to node; the lines belong to edge; the lattice the cells are counted on belongs to spacing. The map only mirrors them. Its Arrange button opens the mode settled on arrange mode.

The laws

Law 1 · C5

“Where the user is, is answerable at every zoom level without hovering or reading — by a position marker in the persistent world view, not by a breadcrumb alone.” The map is always present, and the you-are-here mark is always inside its frame.

Zoom the specimen all the way out and all the way in. At both clamps, read back document.querySelector("#rz-map-you")’s cx/cy and assert both fall inside 0…208 and 0…104, and that they equal RZ.mapPt(RZ.extent(), f.x + f.dx, f.y + f.dy) for the focused record. The mark does not drift and does not leave.

Law 2 · C5, C16

The map's scale comes from the garden, never from the camera. Panning and zooming move the dashed viewport rectangle and nothing else on the map, so the ground never rescales under the eye that is using it to navigate.

Record every .rz-map__dot’s x/y, then drag the board and zoom twice. Diff: every dot is unchanged, and only #rz-map-view’s x/y/width/height moved. RZ.extent() returns the same object shape before and after.

Law 3 · C9

“No non-structural data change (status, count, new attachment) moves anything.” A plant going thirsty, a recap gaining a line, a treatment step ticked off — the map does not twitch.

Change a record's recap and a card's health, call RZ.drawMap(), and diff every .rz-map__dot position and the four numbers on #rz-map-view. All equal. The extent is unchanged.

Law 4 · C7, C9

The map's extent is the union of the lattice and every body's live position, the persisted drag offset included — so a body arranged past the edge of the lattice is on the map, in the place the user put it, and marks itself as moved.

Enter Arrange, drag a card well past the right of the board, leave Arrange. Assert RZ.extent().w has grown, that the dragged body's dot carries data-moved="true", and that its mapped point still equals RZ.mapPt(ex, r.x + r.dx, r.y + r.dy). Reload: the same extent comes back.

Law 5 · C13, C18

The map's accessible name is rewritten on every hop, in lockstep with the visible you-are-here, and it never speaks a cell, a row or a column — it names the body you are at, its kind, how many veins lead out, and the path you took.

Read the map's aria-label, hop, and read it again: it names the new body and the new degree, and its body name matches the text of the aria-current entry in you-are-here. Set data-motion="reduced" and hop again — the label still changes. Assert it matches no /\d+\s*,\s*\d+/.

The map, running

This is the same garden, at the same cells, as the one on world. Travel by clicking a card and watch the lit mark move while the dots stay exactly where they are. Drag the bare board to pan, hold Alt and use the wheel to zoom, and watch the dashed rectangle be the only thing on the map that responds. Press Arrange, drag a card out past the others, and come back — the map grew to hold it, and that dot now marks itself as moved.

The map is drawn by the runtime and never by hand. One call redraws it from the records, and a second call re-fits only the dashed rectangle after the camera has moved:

RZ.extent();        /* the union of the lattice and every live position */
RZ.mapPt(ex, x, y); /* one garden point, mapped into the 208×104 frame */
RZ.drawMap();       /* edges, dots, you-are-here, and the accessible label */
RZ.drawViewport();  /* the dashed rectangle alone — the camera moved */

What a theme fills

The map has five marks and one frame. A theme may re-key every colour under it and change nothing about what is drawn.

Marks on the map
ClassMeans
.rz-map__edge One vein, from the same edge list the board draws from. If a vein is on the board it is on the map, and the other way round.
.rz-map__dot One body in your garden, at its live position.
.rz-map__dot[data-rank="near"] A body one hop from where you stand. Brighter, so the shape of what is immediately reachable reads without counting.
.rz-map__dot[data-moved="true"] A body you arranged by hand. It is honest about which positions are yours rather than the lattice's.
.rz-map__view The dashed rectangle: how much of the garden is on screen right now. The only mark the camera is allowed to move.
.rz-map__you / .rz-map__you-halo You are here. Exactly one per map, because there is exactly one focal body.

#rz-map-dotsg Where the bodies are drawn, on the same redraw.

Accessibility

The map speaks. The frame is role="img" and its name is rewritten on every hop: “World view. You are at Office Fig, plant. 2 veins out. Path: My garden, then Office Fig.” That sentence carries the same three facts the picture carries — where you are, how far you can go from here, and how you got here — so the answer to where am I does not depend on seeing a lit dot.

Never a coordinate. The cell, the row and the column are the system's private vocabulary. The label names bodies and counts, in your garden's own words, and never a pair of numbers.

Never colour alone. You-are-here is a larger mark with a halo around it where every body is a small square; the viewport is the only dashed mark; an arranged body is recoloured and is the only dot that ever changes colour after load. Blur the map or drop it to greyscale and the three still separate.

No tab stop, and nothing lost. The map holds no controls, so it does not sit in the keyboard path — every body it draws is reachable in the world itself, by the arrow keys, following the veins rather than document order (accessibility). The one control in this corner, Arrange, is an ordinary button with an ordinary name.

Reduced motion. The mark's move is the shortest motion in the system and it still happens; what changes is that the camera's trail is drawn and held rather than animated (traversal). The label is rewritten either way, so a reduced-motion reader loses no fact.

If you are unsure whether a map is pulling its weight on a given surface, say so and test it the honest way: turn the label off and navigate by the picture, then turn the picture off and navigate by the label. Both have to work.

Using it

One map per surface, in the same corner every time. It is worth its space only once the reader stops looking for it.

Do

Let the map answer where you are and let the world do the travelling. Panning and zooming change the dashed rectangle only — the garden keeps its scale in the frame, so the shape you learned a minute ago is the shape you are still reading (C5, C16).

Don’t

Rescale the map to the camera, so every zoom redraws the garden's shape under the reader. The one instrument that exists to make zooming safe becomes the thing that makes it disorienting, and nothing on screen is stable enough to navigate by.

Do

Draw every dot and every line from the records and the edge list, and redraw the whole map rather than appending to it. A dot then cannot outlive the body behind it, and a body cannot go missing.

Don’t

Hand-place a dot, or hide a body from the map to keep it tidy. A body that is on the board and not on the map is a body your reader cannot find; a dot with nothing behind it is a promise you cannot keep (C3).
