Focus panel

The focus panel is not a panel you add — it is the focused card's own body together with the two chrome panels beside it. This page settles the four answers that arrangement owes you, where each one lives, and what happens to them when an answer is not available.

What this is

When you travel to your Office Fig, the fig has four things to tell you: what it is, how it is doing, what you can do about it, and where you can go from here. In Rhizome those four answers are not gathered into one box. They are given by three surfaces that are always together — the focused card in the middle of the world, the Navigate to rail on the end edge, and Actions at the bottom start corner. Read together, that is the focus panel.

It is worth saying plainly what this page is not introducing: there is no .rz-focus class, no drawer, no detail sheet and no second scrolling region. The focused card is an ordinary .rz-n with data-focus="true", and the two panels beside it are ordinary .rz-chrome. What this page owns is the arrangement: which answer lives where, and the rule that all four are present at once.

The card itself — its contract, its ranks, its graduation — is node. The chrome material the two side panels are cut from is chrome; the rail's own behaviour is navigate to and the stakes are action rail. The instruments that answer “how is it doing” are vitals and treatment course.

The laws

Law 1 · C19

“The focused node answers all four questions without further navigation.” What is this, what state is it in, what can I do here, where can I go from here — all four are answerable from where the camera has landed, with no hop and no hover.

With a focus settled, locate on screen a block answering each of the four. Then instrument RZ.go and read all four aloud: the counter is still at zero. If any answer needs a hop to reach, this law is broken, not deferred.

Law 2 · C19, C14

Each answer has exactly one home. Identity and state are inside the card; what you can do is in Actions; where you can go is in Navigate to. No answer is given twice on the surface, and none is split across two of the three.

For each of the four answers, count the elements on the surface that give it: exactly one. Then assert no .rz-stake and no .rz-chip exists inside .rz-chrome, and that the focused card contains no standing list of its own neighbours — the rail is the only place that list appears.

Law 3 · C16

There is one focus panel because “there is exactly one focal point, it owns the camera, and the camera never moves without a user-attributable reason.” The two side panels are re-addressed to the new focus on every hop; they are never duplicated, stacked or opened for a second node.

Assert exactly one element carries data-focus="true", and that #rz-actions-scope names that node and no other. Hop, and assert both panels re-address in the same frame as the rank change. Idle the page 60 seconds: the camera transform is unchanged.

Law 4 · C19, C24

An answer the system does not have is still given, in its own place, in that node's own words. A missing answer is written as missing — never omitted, never faded, never left for the user to infer from a gap.

Set data-net="offline" and hop. All four answers are still present: the state answer now states its own staleness in words, and every stake in Actions is visibly refused or announces itself queued. Count the blocks again — still four, none removed.

Specimens

The assembled thing, running. The Office Fig has the camera; its card gives the first two answers, the rail on the end edge gives the fourth, and Actions at the start corner gives the third. Travel to another card and watch all three re-address together — nothing opens, nothing is added, and the fig stays where it is.

Nothing above is re-implemented. The Navigate-to rows and the degree beside them come from the same edge list the veins are drawn from, and the Actions rows come from the actions register keyed by node — which is why the rail can never offer a hop the world cannot draw, and why Actions can never be scoped to a node that is not the focus.

RZ.mount({
  world: "#rz-world",
  plane: "#rz-plane",
  focus: "n-office",
  path: ["n-garden", "n-office"],
  edges: [
    ["n-garden", "n-office"],
    ["n-office", "n-rot"],
    ["n-office", "n-journal"],
    ["n-garden", "n-journal"]
  ],
  actions: {
    "n-office": ["Log a watering", "Start a treatment", "Move to another room"],
    "n-rot":    ["Start a treatment course"],
    "n-garden": ["Add a plant"]
  }
});

And the state answer on its own, out of the world — the block that carries “how is it doing”. Under an honest reading it says the health and the last watering; under Law 4 it also says what it could not check.

The four answers, and where each lives

This is the table to check a surface against. If you cannot fill a row, the panel is incomplete — and the honest fix is to write the missing answer, not to move another one to cover the gap.

The four answers (Law 1, Law 2)
The questionWhere it is answeredWhat that looks like
What is this? The focused card's head — .rz-n__kind, .rz-n__name, .rz-n__grad The kind in words, the name you gave it at the top rung, and the binomial in .rz-binomial beneath — never above, and never the only name shown.
What state is it in? The card's first .rz-n__section — .rz-vitals, and .rz-n__stale when a reading is old Health as a word beside its dot, the facts a user would ask for, and confidence stated rather than implied. The instruments are vitals.
What can I do here? The Actions panel — .rz-chrome--actions, scoped by #rz-actions-scope One row per mutation, each a .rz-chrome__item with data-means="mutate". Scoped to the focus by name, so it is never ambiguous whose garden is about to change.
Where can I go from here? The Navigate-to panel — .rz-chrome--rail, with its degree in #rz-navto-degree One row per real neighbour, each with data-means="travel" and that neighbour's own recap underneath. Generated from adjacency, so it cannot drift.

Behaviour

Arriving

The card expands where it stands and the two side panels re-address in the same pass. While the camera is still crossing, the destination shows a skeleton at the real row heights and the rail already lists the neighbours you are arriving among — so by the time the camera lands, the fourth answer has been readable for the whole 300ms. The hop itself is traversal.

Reading

The card's body is the one scrolling region, under a bare wheel. The two chrome panels do not scroll with it and do not move with the camera: they are welded to their edges. So the answers stay in the same two places on the screen no matter where in the world you have wandered, which is what makes them findable without looking for them.

Acting

A stake in Actions changes your data and moves the camera not at all; a row in Navigate to travels and changes no data. That separation is interaction and cursors' law, and this page's job is only to keep the two on different edges so a hand reaching for one cannot land on the other.

Changing in place

A watering logged, a health turning, a photo attached, a neighbour arriving: the answer updates where it stands. Nothing re-renders, no answer reflows, and no card moves — a non-structural change is not a structural one. If a genuinely new neighbour appears, the rail gains a row and the degree beside it goes up by one, because both are read from the same list.

When an answer is missing

Say so, in the answer's own place. A reading you could not refresh states its age in the node's own words; an identification you are unsure of states its confidence; a mutation you cannot perform right now says it is queued. What the panel must never do is quietly show yesterday's number as today's — the user is deciding whether to water a plant on it. The materials for each state are degradation.

Accessibility

The four answers must be four answers to a screen-reader user too. The focused card is a group whose label the runtime rewrites on every hop — its kind, its name, its recap, how many veins lead out, and “you are here” — so the first and fourth answers arrive on arrival, before the user has walked anything. That label never speaks a cell, a row or a column.

The number in the rail's heading and the number in the card's label come from the same adjacency, so a screen-reader user and a sighted user are never told two different degrees. If you find yourself typing a count into the markup, that is the bug.

Both side panels are reachable without a pointer, and the arrow keys propose the neighbour that actually lies that way rather than the next one in the document. A direction with no neighbour is spoken, not silently ignored. That behaviour as a whole is owned by accessibility.

Colour is never the only carrier of an answer. Health is a word beside its dot; confidence is a word as well as a bar; staleness is a sentence rather than a dimming; a refused stake says it is refused rather than only looking quieter. The whole arrangement uses logical properties, so the rail and Actions swap edges in RTL and nothing points the wrong way.

One thing to watch for yourself: because the card's body scrolls and nothing else does, an answer parked below the fold with nothing in the tab order leading to it is an answer the user does not have. Read the panel with the keyboard alone before you call it finished.

Usage guidance

Nearly every mistake here is the same one: gathering the answers into one box because they feel like they belong together. They do belong together — that is why they are all on screen at once — but the moment the destinations move inside the card, the reader loses the one reliable reading of the surface: the middle is here, the edges are elsewhere and what you can do.

Do

Let the rail list the neighbours and the card describe the plant. If the fig's own prose wants to point at Root rot, write that as a chip inside the sentence — an in-prose hop, on the same timing as every other (Law 2).

Don’t

Build a “Related” list inside the card. It duplicates the rail, which means it can disagree with it, and a second list of destinations is a second answer to a question that already has one (C19, C14).

Do

Give the state answer the room it needs. “Wants water · last watered 12 days ago, by you” is two facts a person can act on, and they earn their place beside the name.

Don’t

Fold the state behind a control the user has to open. An answer one activation away is an answer the user has to know to ask for, which is exactly what C19 refuses.

Do

Say what you could not check, in the place the answer would have been, in the node's own words — “read before you went offline, about an hour ago” (Law 4).

Don’t

Drop the block, or grey it out and leave the old number showing. A missing answer that looks like a present one is worse than no panel at all (C24).

Two smaller habits. Keep the head honest: the binomial goes under the name you gave the plant, always, and if there is no nickname yet, say so plainly rather than promoting the Latin. And resist letting the state block grow into everything you know about the plant — it is a short set of answers to questions a person actually asks, and a dozen rows of it is a dashboard, which is the one thing PlantPal is not.

Where else this appears

The card at the centre of it is node, standing on world and joined by edge. The material both side panels are cut from — and the rule that they take no shadow and never move with the camera — is chrome. The rail's own rows are navigate to; the stakes are action rail; the “+N more” card the rail may point at is aggregate; and the marker that says where this focus stands in the whole world is minimap.

The instruments inside the state answer are vitals and treatment course. The hop that re-addresses all three surfaces is traversal, and an arrival from a search or a notification is arrivals. The states an answer can wear are degradation; the mode where every card opens at once and this arrangement goes inert is arrange mode; the card the whole application becomes is settings exit; and the first focus in an empty garden is day zero.

The rung ladder the four answers are set on is typography, the two registers' colours are colors, the halo the card carries and chrome's lack of one is elevation, the one navigation timing is motion, the lattice the card stands on is spacing, and the drawn grammar of the two forms is iconography. How each answer is worded is voice and copy, and how to add to any of it is extending this system.
