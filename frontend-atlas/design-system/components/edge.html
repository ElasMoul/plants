Edge

A vein is the line between two cards, and it is the only way from one to the other. This page settles that every vein you can see is a vein you can take, and that both come from one declaration — the edge list — which nothing on the plane is allowed to contradict.

What this is

Your garden is joined up. The fig on your desk belongs to a species, that species has a care guide, the guide is what the treatment course for root rot points back to, and the photo you took on Jul 18 is about that plant and nothing else. Each of those is a vein: a real relationship in your garden, drawn on the plane as a line between two cards, and travelled by the camera when you take it.

A vein is never decoration. If you can see it, you can take it; if you can take it, you could already see it. Both halves of that come from the same place — the edge list, declared once as RZ.EDGES, from which the drawn veins, the keyboard directions, the Navigate to entries, the world view's lines, every degree count and the route of every hop are generated. There is no second list, and there is nowhere to hand-draw a line.

This page owns the vein and its list. The plane it is drawn on and the ownership of its layer are world; the cards at its ends are node; the journey along it is traversal, which owns the one navigation timing. The rule deciding which members of a collection get a vein at all is the density rule, owned by aggregate and spacing.

The laws

Law 1 · C1, C3

The edge list is the single source of truth. Everything that draws a connection, counts a connection or travels a connection reads that one array — so a drawn vein that cannot be travelled, and a travelable vein that was never drawn, both cannot exist. “On a focus change the surrounding nodes change to that node's own neighbours, every drawn edge is actually traversable, and the neighbour count tracks real degree.”

Iterate every .rz-vein in the document and assert its data-a/data-b pair is present in RZ.EDGES; then iterate RZ.EDGES and assert each pair has a drawn .rz-vein. For every node assert RZ.adj[id].length equals the number of veins touching it and equals the number Navigate to prints in its header.

Law 2 · C1

A vein is drawn before it is travelled. “Every navigation follows an edge that was visible before the click.” Nothing appears because you hovered, and no destination is reachable that was not already standing on the plane, joined to where you are.

Take the set of .rz-vein elements at first paint, before any pointer or key event. Then hop anywhere and assert every consecutive pair of RZ.route(from, to) was already in that first-paint set. Assert RZ.go(id) for an unconnected id returns with the camera and the focus unchanged — no path is ever fabricated.

Law 3 · C3

The vein layer is redrawn from the list, never appended to. Any event that changes a position — a drag in arrange mode, a resize, a new record — clears the layer and regenerates all of it in one pass.

Drag one card ten times in arrange mode and count .rz-vein after each release: the count equals RZ.EDGES.length every time, and no two veins share a data-a/data-b pair. Appending on release would show as duplicates stacked on the same line.

Law 4 · C11

A vein's geometry comes from the two node records at its ends, in the plane's own coordinates, so the path the camera travels is the line you were shown. “Every transition carries direction, scale, and ownership — and the camera follows the edge's own path.”

For any route, compare RZ.chainD(chain) against the d attributes of that route's drawn veins: the same points, in the same order. Then pan the board and diff bounding boxes — every vein moved by the same delta as the cards it joins, because the layer is a child of the same transformed plane and paints beneath them.

Law 5 · C6

The veins make a graph, not a tree wearing one. Real gardens fold back on themselves: two problems share one treatment course, a journal entry is about the plant it photographed. Those cross-branch veins are why this surface is an atlas at all, and removing them to tidy the picture removes the reason for the picture.

Assert the edge list contains at least one vein joining two different branches — a journal entry to its plant, or two problems to one course — and that RZ.EDGES.length is greater than or equal to the node count. A list where every node has exactly one parent is a hierarchy, and this regime is the wrong one for it.

Law 6 · C23

A vein into a region you have not fetched yet is still a vein: drawn dashed, stated in words, and traversable. “On a partial graph, unloaded regions are drawn as explicitly unknown — distinguishable from ‘loaded and empty’ — with traversal into them allowed and never dead-ending.”

Find a vein carrying data-unknown="true" and assert RZ.go across it succeeds, that the node it lands on offers a real way to fetch what is missing, and that it reads differently from a node that is loaded and genuinely empty — with the difference stated in words, not only in the stroke.

Specimen

Five cards, six veins, one declaration. Click any card to travel to it: the veins out of where you stand brighten and thicken, the camera follows the line rather than cutting across the space, and Navigate to prints the same count the veins do — all from the list below the specimen. Drag the bare board to pan and watch the veins move with the cards, not under them.

Two things in here are deliberate. Journal · Jul 18 photo is joined both to Journal and to Office Fig — a cross-branch vein, so this is a graph. And the vein to the unloaded region is dashed: it is real, it is takeable, and we are honest that we have not fetched what is on the other side.

The whole specimen is mounted from the runtime. Nothing here draws a line by hand, and nothing here counts a connection by hand — which is exactly why the count beside Navigate to cannot drift from the picture above it.

RZ.mount({
  world: "#rz-world",
  plane: "#rz-plane",
  focus: "n-office",
  path: ["n-garden", "n-office"],
  edges: [
    ["n-garden", "n-office"],
    ["n-garden", "n-j1"],
    ["n-office", "n-problems"],
    ["n-office", "n-j1"],        /* cross-branch: the photo is about the plant */
    ["n-j1", "n-unknown"],       /* into a region we have not fetched */
    ["n-problems", "n-unknown"]
  ]
});

Reading a count back is the same shape of call, and it is how you check Law 1 without opening the markup:

RZ.degree("n-office");                 /* 3 */
RZ.adj["n-office"];                    /* ["n-garden", "n-problems", "n-j1"] */
RZ.route("n-garden", "n-unknown");     /* ["n-garden", "n-j1", "n-unknown"] */
document.querySelectorAll(".rz-vein").length === RZ.EDGES.length;  /* true */

Behaviour

At rest

Every vein in the list is on the plane from first paint, at its quiet weight, beneath the cards. A dot sits at the middle of each one — a small confirmation that the line is a thing and not a shadow. Veins take no pointer events at all: you travel a vein by taking one of its ends, never by clicking the line, because a line is far too thin a target to be honest about.

Around the focus

The veins touching where you stand brighten and thicken, and their midpoint dots come up to full. That is the answer to where can I go from here in the picture itself, and Navigate to prints the same set in words. Both are recomputed on every hop from the same adjacency, so they cannot disagree.

While travelling

The route is drawn as a trail along the real veins and the camera centre is driven along it by arc length, so the motion says which vein you took. A crossing of more than one vein lights each card as the camera reaches it and opens only the one you asked for. The timing — one duration for every hop from every trigger — belongs to traversal and motion; the vein's part is only to be the shape the camera follows. Under reduced motion the trail is still drawn along the real veins and held, so direction and distance survive.

When something moves

Dragging a card in arrange mode re-reads both ends from the records and regenerates the layer in one pass. Nothing is appended, so a dragged card cannot leave a stale line behind or double the one it had. The world view redraws from the same list at the same moment, which is why the small map and the big plane never tell two stories.

When the graph is incomplete

A vein with an unfetched node at one end is dashed and paler, and the node it leads to says in its own words that we have not been there yet. That is a different material from a node that is loaded and simply has nothing in it, and both must be legible without the reader knowing the stroke pattern by heart. The full set of states belongs to degradation.

What a theme fills

A theme chooses how a vein reads; it does not choose which veins exist, or whether a drawn one can be taken.

Vein tokens
TokenMeans
--rz-vein A vein at rest. Quiet enough to sit under the cards, present enough that the garden reads as joined up from across the room.
--rz-vein-live A vein touching where you stand. Paired with --rz-vein-width-live, so the difference is carried by weight as well as by colour.
--rz-vein-unknown A vein with an unfetched node at one end. Dashed as well as tinted — and the node at the far end says so in words.
--rz-vein-node The dot at a vein's midpoint. Half-strength at rest, full when the vein is live.
--rz-vein-width · --rz-vein-width-live The two weights. A theme may re-pitch both, but the live one stays visibly heavier: weight is the channel that survives a palette with little contrast in it.
--rz-vital-bright The trail: the route just travelled, drawn along the real veins and held under reduced motion.
--rz-z-vein The vein rung. Above the decorative field, beneath every card — the stacking is owned by elevation.
The attributes the runtime writes
AttributeOnMeans
data-a · data-b .rz-vein, .rz-vein-node The two node ids this vein joins, stamped from the list entry it was generated from. This is what makes Law 1 checkable from the DOM alone.
data-live .rz-vein, .rz-vein-node This vein touches the focus. Rewritten on every hop from the adjacency, never set by hand.
data-unknown .rz-vein One of its ends is a node we have not fetched. Set from the node record, so it cannot be claimed for a node that is actually loaded.
data-on .rz-trail A route is being shown. Held longer under reduced motion, which is the whole of how direction survives there.

Accessibility

The vein layer itself is hidden from assistive technology on purpose: a curve announces nothing useful, and reading out twenty-five of them would bury the one thing that matters. What matters is carried in words instead, from the same list — so nothing is lost by hiding the drawing.

Every vein out of where you stand is a real entry in Navigate to and a real answer to an arrow key, which means no destination exists that a keyboard user cannot reach. Arrow keys pick the neighbour that actually lies that way, computed from the two cards' real positions rather than from document order, so the direction you press and the direction the vein runs are the same direction. A direction with no vein is spoken by name — “no vein that way from Office Fig” — because silence reads as a broken key. Tab walks the veins out of the focus in turn and says which of how many.

After every hop one sentence names where you came from, anything you crossed without opening, and how many veins lead out of where you now are — the same number Navigate to prints and the same number the picture draws. The world view's label is rewritten in the same breath. Neither ever speaks a cell, a row or a coordinate; they speak your garden.

Colour is never the only signal a vein carries. Live is heavier as well as brighter, unknown is dashed as well as tinted, and the node at the far end of an unknown vein says in plain words that we have not fetched it — so a reader who cannot separate two strokes still gets the fact. The layer uses logical properties throughout, so nothing points the wrong way in RTL.

Usage guidance

Almost every way to get this wrong is the same way: making a line by hand, or making a number by hand. Both put a second truth on the plane, and the second one is always the one that goes stale.

Do

Add the relationship to the edge list and let the drawing, the count, the keyboard direction and the route all fall out of it. One entry, and the picture and the words agree by construction (Law 1).

Don’t

Place a rotated line between two cards to show a relationship the list does not have. It cannot be travelled, it will not move when a card moves, and it teaches your reader that the lines are decoration — after which none of them are believed (Law 1, C1).

Do

Print a degree from the adjacency, wherever it appears — the Navigate to header, a card's recap, a spoken announcement. Then the three of them are the same number because they are literally the same number (Law 1, C3).

Don’t

Write “6 veins” into the markup because that is how many there were when you built the page. The first time a relationship is added the interface starts lying, and it will keep lying quietly for as long as nobody counts (C3).

Do

Draw the vein into a region you have not fetched, dashed, and let the user take it. Being honest about the edge of what you know is what keeps the geography whole (Law 6, C23).

Don’t

Leave the vein out until the data arrives. The garden silently changes shape as things load, the user's map of it is invalidated by a network event, and a part of their own garden becomes unreachable for reasons they cannot see (C23).

One more, about restraint. Not every true relationship deserves a vein on the plane: a collection with many members contributes its two highest-ranked and one aggregate, and that ceiling is what keeps a garden of forty thousand plants drawable. Which members earn a vein is the density rule's decision, not this page's — see aggregate. The veins that survive it are all real, and all takeable.

Where else this appears

The plane a vein is drawn on, and the rule about which layers move with the camera, are world. The cards at its ends are node; the aggregate at the end of a collection's third vein, and the density rule that puts it there, are aggregate. The same list in words is navigate to, and the same list in miniature is minimap; the register both of those are dressed in is chrome. The journey along a vein, its one timing and its interruption policy are traversal and motion. Dashed veins and everything else the board does when the data is late, partial or gone are degradation. Keyboard direction-finding and the announcement are accessibility; the rule that a vein takes no pointer events is interaction and cursors.
