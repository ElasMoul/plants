Components

World

The world is the one place PlantPal happens: a single plane your garden stands on, a camera that moves over it, and a register of chrome around it that never moves at all. This page settles what belongs to which of those three, and shows them assembled.

What this is

Your garden is a place. Every plant you keep, every species behind them, every problem, treatment course and journal entry stands at its own spot on one plane, joined by veins. Opening a plant does not take you to a plant screen — the camera travels to it and the plane rescales around it, and the thing you came from is still standing where you left it, smaller. That is the whole of the world: .rz-world is the frame you look through, .rz-plane is the single subtree the camera moves, and everything the camera must not move sits beside the plane, in the chrome register.

There is one world per surface and it owns all of it. Never two worlds side by side, never a world nested in a panel, never a second one opened in a drawer — two worlds mean two answers to where am I, and your reader will trust neither. If a thing has no place on the plane — a sign-in form, a settings panel — it is not part of the world, and dressing it as one costs the plane its meaning.

This page owns the plane, its layers and its camera. The cards on it are node, the lines between them are edge, the furniture around them is chrome, and the journey between two cards is traversal. The density rule that decides how many cards a collection contributes is owned by aggregate and spacing; the lattice the cells are counted on is owned by spacing.

The laws

Law 1 · C4, C16

There is one world and one plane, and every navigation is a rescale of it. “Nothing is replaced, unmounted, or routed to — every navigation is a rescale.”

Record the set of .rz-n elements, hop ten times, and compare: they are the identical objects, nothing was added to or removed from the plane, and location.href never changed. Assert exactly one element carries data-focus="true".

Law 2 · C9, C20

What belongs to the world is a child of .rz-plane and is therefore scaled and translated with it. What belongs to the viewport is a sibling of the plane and never moves with the camera. Membership is the whole rule; nothing else decides it.

Pan the camera and diff every bounding box: the veins, the motes and the cards all moved by the same delta, and the bounding box of every .rz-chrome member is unchanged to the pixel. Then assert no element under .rz-plane carries a .rz-chrome class, and no chrome member is a descendant of the plane.

Law 3 · C17

The world pans; it never scrolls. “Nothing other than the focused node's body scrolls.”

With the specimen mounted, assert document.body.scrollHeight equals its clientHeight, and that of every element in the document only the focused card's .rz-n__body has scrollHeight > clientHeight. Drag the board a long way in one direction: no scrollbar appears anywhere.

Law 4 · C15, C16

The pointer has a precedence order and the world is last in it: the pan handler returns immediately if the pointer went down inside a card, and a card's own travel listener stands aside for its controls, its in-card hops and its links. Camera controls change neither data nor focus — looking around is not navigating.

Press inside a card and drag: the camera transform is identical afterwards. Press on bare board and drag: the transform changed and RZ.focus did not. Click every camera control and assert RZ.focus and every record field are unchanged.

Law 5 · C5, C19

The world is reviewed assembled, never in parts. A plane with no chrome around it cannot answer where you are or what you can do from here, so a specimen of the world always carries the top bar, Navigate to, Actions, the world view and the camera cluster together.

On the assembled specimen below, point at each of the four answers without hopping: what is this (the focused card's kind and name), what state is it in (its vitals), what can I do here (Actions), where can I go from here (Navigate to). Then zoom to both ends of the clamp and assert the world view's you-are-here marker is still inside its frame.

The assembled surface

Five cards, five veins, one camera, and the whole chrome register around them. Click a card to travel to it: the camera follows the vein rather than cutting across the space, every card's rank is recomputed from where you now are, and nothing on the page is replaced. Drag the bare board to pan. Hold Alt and use the wheel to zoom, so a bare wheel stays free to scroll the focused card's own body.

This is the same garden, at the same cells, as the one on the system's front page — a graph that shifts between two pages of one system teaches its reader that positions are decorative.

The specimen is mounted from the runtime, not re-implemented here. The edge list is declared once; the drawn veins, every card's rank, the Navigate-to entries, the degree beside them and the route of every hop are all generated from it — which is why a drawn-but-untraversable vein cannot exist on this page.

RZ.mount({
  world: "#rz-world",
  plane: "#rz-plane",
  focus: "n-office",
  path: ["n-garden", "n-office"],
  edges: [
    ["n-garden", "n-office"],
    ["n-garden", "n-studio"],
    ["n-garden", "n-garden-more"],
    ["n-office", "n-problems"],
    ["n-studio", "n-problems"]
  ],
  actions: {
    "n-office": ["Log a watering", "Move to another room", "Archive this plant"]
  }
});

One amendment was needed to build this page and is recorded as A1 in the constitution's register: chrome is fixed to a viewport edge by contract, and a documentation stage is not a viewport, so .doc-specimen__stage[data-stage="world"] now establishes a containing block so an assembled specimen can hold its own chrome. It moves nothing.

The parts, and who owns them

Membership is the design decision here — the column that matters is the last one. A layer inside the plane is part of the garden and moves with the camera; a layer beside it is part of the apparatus and does not.

Behaviour

Pointer

Dragging bare board pans. Pressing inside a card never pans — the pan handler returns the moment it sees a card under the pointer, which is why you can drag a long paragraph's selection inside the focused card without the world sliding out from under you. Clicking a card that is not the focus travels to it; clicking its own controls, chips, stakes or links does not, because the card's travel listener stands aside for them. There is no grab cursor anywhere inside a card: the board pans, the node reads. The full cursor table is on interaction and cursors.

Camera

Zoom, fit, recentre and pan are a third category of control: they change no data and they change no focus. Zoom is clamped at both ends and steps by a declared factor, so two people zooming the same world arrive at the same scales. Alt and the wheel zooms; a bare wheel is deliberately left alone so the focused card's body can scroll under the pointer. Recentre says so out loud — “you did not move” — because moving the camera back is the one camera action most easily mistaken for a hop.

Travel

Every hop, from every trigger, runs one code path at one timing, and the camera centre follows the polyline of the real veins by arc length rather than cutting across the space. A multi-hop crossing lights what it passes through as it reaches it and opens only the destination. A hop asked for while one is still in flight is refused out loud, never queued or blended. All of that is owned by traversal and motion; the world's part is only that it holds still while it happens.

Fit

The focused card is shown in full, top edge to bottom edge, at any zoom. If it does not fit the frame, the zoom gives way — not the card. The runtime measures the card against the live camera scale on every arrival and on every resize, so the rule survives a window being dragged smaller mid-read.

Arrange, states and modes

Arrange is entered from the button beside the world view and is a mode, not a layer: the camera fits every card, every card renders full, the whole card becomes the handle and everything else goes inert. Degradation is material on the cards themselves — never a global overlay, and the geography never degrades under it. Leaving for settings shrinks the whole world into a floating card rather than opening a dialog over it. Each of those has its own page: arrange mode, degradation, settings exit.

Accessibility

The world is a graph, and a graph has to be walkable without being seen. The frame itself is focusable and takes the arrow keys; each one proposes the neighbour that actually lies that way, computed from real positions rather than document order, and Enter travels there on exactly the path a click would take. A direction with no vein is spoken — “no vein that way from Office Fig” — rather than silently ignored, because silence reads as a broken key. Tab walks the veins out of the focus in turn and announces which of how many; Escape steps back one hop along the path you actually took.

Every focus change is announced in one sentence that names where you came from, what you crossed without opening, and how many veins lead out from here. The world view's accessible label is rewritten on the same hop, in lockstep with the visible you-are-here, so a screen-reader user and a sighted user are never told two different stories about where they are. Neither of those ever speaks a cell, a row, a column or a lattice coordinate: they speak the garden — “Plants · 12 plants”, “Problems · 3 active”.

Under reduced motion the camera stops animating but the direction survives: the trail is drawn along the real veins and held, and both the marker and its label move. Nothing is replaced by an abrupt swap, and the announcement is held longer. The decorative field stops drifting; it does not disappear, because it carries no meaning either way.

The vein layer is aria-hidden on purpose — drawn curves announce nothing useful. Every vein it draws is also a real entry in Navigate to, generated from the same list, so no destination exists that a keyboard user cannot reach. Colour is never the only signal: rank is carried by four visibly different card widths and by the length of the graduation on each card's head, so the hierarchy survives with the text blurred, and kind is carried by that graduation's colour and by the card's own kind line. Logical properties are used throughout, so nothing points the wrong way in RTL.

Usage guidance

The mistake to guard against is putting something in the plane because it is easier to position there. A layer's membership is a statement about whether it is part of your garden, and the camera will enforce that statement whether or not you meant it.

Do

Put the world view, Navigate to, Actions, the top bar and the camera cluster beside the plane, as siblings, flush to the edge they own. They are apparatus: they must stay put while the garden moves under them, and staying put is what makes them readable as apparatus at a glance (Law 2).

Don’t

Park a panel inside .rz-plane so it sits “near” the focused card. It will scale with the zoom, drift with the pan, and become a piece of the garden the user cannot travel to — apparatus wearing a plant's clothes (Law 2, C9).

Do

Let the camera move only for a reason the user can point at: a card they clicked, a key they pressed, a search result they chose, a camera control they used. A world you can predict is a world you can map (C16).

Don’t

Move the camera because something arrived — a watering logged, a health score landing, a notification. A world that drifts on its own is a world the user stops mapping, and a non-structural change is required to move nothing at all (C9).

Two more, both about restraint. Never re-place a card because something about it changed: only two things move a card, a drag you made and a genuinely new record taking a free cell. And keep the frame generous — air in this system is spent separating cards on the plane, not padding the insides of them. A crowded world reads as a list, and a list is the one thing the world is not.

Where else this appears

The cards on the plane are node; the veins between them and their single source of truth are edge; “+N more” and the density rule it serves are aggregate. The furniture around the frame is chrome, and its members have their own pages: navigate to, action rail, minimap, and the focused card's own answers in focus panel. The journey is traversal; the modes are arrange mode and settings exit; the states are degradation. The lattice is spacing, the stacking and the shadow rule are elevation, and the pointer table is interaction and cursors. The amendment path this page used is extending this system.
