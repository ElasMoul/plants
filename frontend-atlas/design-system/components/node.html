Components

Node

A node is one thing in your garden, standing at its own place on the plane. This page settles what a node is made of, what a theme may fill in, and what the card does when you travel to it or away from it.

What this is

Every plant you keep, every species behind them, every problem, treatment course, guide and journal entry is a node. It is the only body in this system: there is no “list item” version of a plant and no “detail page” version of it — there is one card, and it is smaller or larger depending on how far it is from where you are standing. When you open your Office Fig you are not going to a plant screen; the camera travels to the card that was already on the plane, and the card grows into a document you can read.

So the node is a contract, not a card. The system owns a set of attributes — kind, name, recap, cell, rank, focus, show — and the runtime fills the ones it computes. A theme decides what a node looks like at each rank; it never decides what rank a node is, where it stands, or whether it may be replaced.

The plane a node stands on is world; the veins between nodes are edge. How many cards a collection is allowed to contribute — and the “+N more” card that stands for the rest — is owned by aggregate and spacing. The four answers the focused card must give are laid out in focus panel; the instruments inside it are vitals. The journey between two cards is traversal, and the states a card can wear are degradation.

The laws

Law 1 · C2, C4

A node is never mounted and never unmounted. “Nothing is replaced, unmounted, or routed to — every navigation is a rescale.” Travelling away leaves the card you came from visible and shrinking in place: it does not vanish, does not slide to a rail, and is not swapped for a smaller substitute. Travelling to a card expands the card already on screen.

Record the set of .rz-n elements, call RZ.go ten times, and compare: the sets hold the identical objects, nothing was added to or removed from the plane, and location.href never changed. After each hop, assert the previous focus still has a rendered element carrying a data-rank attribute.

Law 2 · C3, C12

Rank is computed, never authored. It is graph distance from the focus — focus, near, far, fringe — recomputed on every hop, and it is spent on scale: “With the text blurred on a frozen frame, the hierarchy is still readable from scale alone, on the 100/75/55/30/18-25 ladder.”

For every card assert el.dataset.rank equals RZ.rankName(RZ.ranks(RZ.focus)[el.id]), and that no data-rank appears in the authored markup of a mounted world. Then apply a 6px blur to the plane and screenshot: four distinguishable card widths remain.

Law 3 · C12

Kind is a channel of its own, carried at a size where the name is unreadable. The graduation on the card's head does both jobs at once: its length is the rank and its colour is the kind.

Assert every .rz-n carries a data-kind-key from the eight domain kinds and renders a .rz-n__grad. Shrink the plane to the fringe scale and mask all text: each card's kind is still separable by the graduation's colour, and its rank by that graduation's length.

Law 4 · C17, C19

The focused node is shown in full, top edge to bottom edge, at any zoom — and if it does not fit, the zoom gives way, not the card. “Nothing other than the focused node's body scrolls.”

Zoom to zoom.max on a tall card and assert its top and bottom edges are both inside the frame, and that RZ.cam.k changed rather than the card's height. Then walk every element in the document: only the focused card's .rz-n__body has scrollHeight > clientHeight, and document.body has no scrollbar.

Law 5 · C9

A non-focused node is a recap derived from its own content, is clickable across its whole surface, and shows no controls. Its recap speaks your garden — “Plants · 12 plants”, “Problems · 3 active” — and never a cell, row, column or slot. “No non-structural data change (status, count, new attachment) moves anything.”

Assert no .rz-stake or .rz-chip is rendered inside a card whose data-focus is not "true". Rewrite a card's data-recap and its visible recap, then diff every card's computed left/top: nothing moved. Search the rendered text and every aria-label for a data-cell value: no match.

The contract

These are the attributes the system owns. You write the first group; the runtime writes the second on every hop. Writing one of the runtime's by hand is how a card ends up claiming a rank the graph does not agree with.

Node attributes
AttributeWritten byMeans
data-kind You The kind in the garden's own words, shown on the card: Plant, Species, Problem, Guide, Journal, Collection.
data-kind-key You The machine form of the same thing, one of the eight below. It keys the graduation's colour (Law 3).
data-name You What you call it. The runtime speaks this name in the live region and in the world view's label, so it is the name the user hears as well as reads.
data-recap You The one line this card is worth when it is not the focus, derived from its own content. Domain words only (Law 5).
data-cell · data-dx · data-dy You · the drag The node's identity on the lattice, and the offset a drag persisted against it. Position is origin + cell × pitch + offset; the formula is owned by spacing.
data-rank The runtime focus · near · far · fringe, from breadth-first distance to the focus. Recomputed every hop (Law 2).
data-focus The runtime "true" on exactly one card. It owns the camera, and it is the only card shown in full by default.
data-show The runtime recap, skel or full. The destination of a hop shows skel while the camera is still crossing, then its real content.
data-state Your data unknown, empty, error or archived — material the card wears, never a layer over the world. Owned by degradation.

Eight kinds, and no ninth without an amendment — kind is a channel before it is a colour, so adding one changes the vocabulary of the system and is recorded as such (see extending this system). This page added nothing: every class and token it uses already existed. The palette these eight are drawn from, and the fact that it re-keys live, belong to colors.

Behaviour

Arriving and leaving

A card you travel to expands where it stands. While the camera is still crossing, it shows a skeleton shaped like the content that is coming, at that content's real row heights; when the camera lands, the skeleton gives way to the card's own body and the runtime measures the card against the live zoom so it fits. The card you left shrinks in place to whatever rank it now holds. Neither card is mounted, unmounted, or moved a pixel from its cell.

Pointer

A non-focused card is one target across its whole surface: press anywhere on it and you travel. The focused card is a document instead — the ordinary cursor over its text, a pointer over its links and controls — and its travel listener stands aside for anything it contains, so the chip inside the Office Fig hops to Root rot without the card underneath also firing. There is no grab cursor anywhere inside a card: the board pans, the node reads. The full table is on interaction and cursors.

Scrolling

Only the focused card's .rz-n__body scrolls, and it does it under a bare wheel, because zoom is deliberately held behind Alt. Its height cap is written against the live camera scale the runtime publishes, so the card gives you the same amount of reading whether you are zoomed in or out.

Prose that travels

A card's interior is an ordinary document, and its prose may carry real links that traverse the garden. Write them as chips with data-rz-goto, and they run exactly the same hop, on exactly the same timing, as a click on the card itself — the timing is owned by traversal and motion.

Controls

Only the focused card shows controls, and there are two forms of them: a filled stake changes your data, an outlined chip with a caret travels. Nothing else appears inside a card. Which control belongs to which is settled by action rail and navigate to.

Accessibility

Each card is a group with an accessible label the runtime rewrites on every hop: its kind, its name, its recap, how many veins lead out of it, and “you are here” when it is the focus. The label is built from the attributes you wrote and the same adjacency the veins are drawn from, so a screen-reader user and a sighted user are never told two different numbers.

That label never speaks a cell, a row, a column or a slot. Those are the system's private words for where a card stands; the user hears the garden — “Plant · Office Fig · Fiddle-leaf Fig, overdue 2 days · 2 veins out”.

Every card is reachable by following the graph rather than the document: the arrow keys propose the neighbour that actually lies that way, computed from real positions, and Enter travels there. On arrival the runtime moves the browser's focus to the destination card without scrolling the page, so the keyboard user lands where the camera landed. That behaviour as a whole is owned by accessibility.

Colour is never the only signal on a card. Rank is four visibly different widths and four graduation lengths as well as four ink strengths; kind is the graduation's colour and the kind line written in words at the top of every card. A state is material — dashed, dotted, a rule down one side — before it is a hue. The card uses logical properties throughout, so its graduation, its section rules and its error bar all point the right way in RTL.

If you are unsure whether a recap will read out sensibly, read it aloud on its own. “Plants · 12 plants” does. “12 · 1 · 3” does not.

Usage guidance

Most mistakes on this component are the same mistake: treating the card as a view of a thing rather than the thing itself. A view can be replaced, re-ranked by hand, or opened somewhere else. A body standing on a plane cannot.

Do

Let the runtime rank the cards. Write the kind, the name, the recap and the cell, and let the graph decide who is near and who is fringe on every hop — it already knows the distances (Law 2).

Don’t

Author data-rank="near" on a card because it matters to you. The first hop away from it will disagree, and a card that looks one vein out while standing three lies about distance in the one channel a reader trusts at a glance (C3, C12).

Do

Keep the recap to what the card can honestly say from its own content — “Under treatment · root rot, step 2 of 4”. If a count is not known yet, say so in words; an honest gap reads better than a number the user cannot check.

Don’t

Put controls on a card that is not the focus. A stake on a fringe card offers a change to your garden at a size where its own label is unreadable, and it turns a whole-surface travel target into a place where a stray click waters something (Law 5, C14).

Do

Let the focused card grow as tall as its content needs and let the zoom give way to it. A plant with a long journal is allowed to be a long card (Law 4).

Don’t

Clip the focused card to a fixed height and let the page scroll behind it. Two scrolling surfaces is one too many, and the second one takes the geography with it (C17).

One more, about restraint: never re-place a card because something about it changed. A watering logged, a health score arriving, a photo attached — none of those is structural, and none of them moves anything. Only two things move a card: a drag you made, and a genuinely new record taking a free cell.

Where else this appears

The plane and the camera are world; the veins and their single source of truth are edge. The density rule that decides how many cards a collection contributes, and the “+N more” card that is itself a node, are aggregate. The four answers the focused card owes you are focus panel; the instruments inside it are vitals and treatment course. The two control forms are action rail and navigate to; the furniture that may never wear a card's material is chrome, and the map that marks where this card stands is minimap.

The hop itself is traversal, and an arrival from a search or a notification is arrivals. The states a card wears are degradation; the mode in which every card opens and the whole card becomes a handle is arrange mode; the card the whole application becomes is settings exit, and the first card in an empty garden is day zero. The rank ladder as ratios is typography, the kind palette is colors, the lattice and the placement formula are spacing, and the card's halo — and chrome's lack of one — is elevation. How the recap is worded is voice and copy.
