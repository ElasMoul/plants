Components

Vitals

The short block inside a card that answers what state is this thing in — how the plant is doing, and how sure we are about anything we worked out for you. This page settles where vitals live, what they are allowed to say, and why reading them never moves your garden.

What this is

A vitals block is the health readout of one plant, species, problem or treatment course, written as a few labelled rows in that thing's own card: how it is doing, when you last watered it, how confident we are that the species we identified is really the species you have. It is the second of the four questions the focused card has to answer on its own — what is this, what state is it in, what can I do here, where can I go from here (C19).

Vitals are a readout, not a control. Nothing in the block changes anything: the watering lives on the action rail as a stake, and the treatment steps live in the treatment course. If a row tempts you to make it clickable, what you actually want is a stake or a chip, and those two forms are settled on interaction and cursors.

Be honest here, in the app's own spirit. A vital we measured — when you last watered — is stated flatly. A vital we inferred — a species read from your photo, a diagnosis read from a leaf — carries its confidence beside it, and if we are not sure, the row says so in words as well as in the meter.

The laws

Law 1 · C9

A vital changing changes the vital and nothing else. No health, count, staleness or confidence value ever moves a card, resizes one, re-ranks one, or grows the world.

Record every .rz-n's computed left/top and RZ.extent(). Flip every data-health through thriving, watch, ailing and lost, and rewrite every recap count. Diff both recordings: identical.

Law 2 · C19

Health is said in words as well as in colour. The dot is a second reading of the row, never the only one.

Render the page with filter: grayscale(1) applied to the plane. Every .rz-health still states its condition — "Wants water", "New growth this month", "Losing lower leaves" — from its text alone, and no vitals row is left carrying meaning only in a swatch.

Law 3 · C19, C12

Vitals belong to the body, and the body belongs to the focused card. A card you have not travelled to says its state in one clause of its recap instead — "overdue 2 days", "3 active" — never as an instrument panel.

For every .rz-vitals in the document, assert its offsetParent is null unless its enclosing .rz-n carries data-focus="true". Hop with RZ.go(id) and re-run: exactly one vitals block is visible, and it is the new focus's.

Law 4 · C19

Anything we worked out rather than watched carries its confidence in the same row, and a low confidence says so in a sentence too.

For every inferred vital — identification, diagnosis, any AI-read value — assert its .rz-vital-row contains an .rz-conf whose data-conf is high, medium or low, and that every data-conf="low" row also carries a plain-language sentence saying we are not sure.

Staleness, slow reads and failed reads are states of the node, not of the vitals block, and they are settled once on degradation. A vitals block under those states keeps its rows and its row heights; it does not empty itself out and it does not reflow.

The health scale and the confidence meter

Five conditions, each with its own word. The word is the reading; the dot repeats it. Write the word in the plant's terms, not the system's: "Wants water" beats "Watch", and "We think this one is gone" beats "Lost".

<div class="rz-n__section rz-vitals">
  <div class="rz-vital-row">
    <span class="rz-vital-row__key">Health</span>
    <span class="rz-health" data-health="watch">Wants water</span>
  </div>
  <div class="rz-vital-row">
    <span class="rz-vital-row__key">Species</span>
    <span><span class="rz-binomial">Ficus lyrata</span>, read from your photo</span>
    <span class="rz-conf" data-conf="high">
      <span class="rz-conf__track"><span class="rz-conf__fill"></span></span>
      <span class="rz-label">Confident</span>
    </span>
  </div>
</div>

What a theme fills

Health
TokenMeans
--rz-thriving Growing well. Nothing is asked of you.
--rz-stable Holding steady. The default when no data-health is set.
--rz-watch Something is due or drifting — water, light, a check.
--rz-ailing Actually unwell. A problem or a treatment course is attached.
--rz-lost Gone. The node stays in your garden and stays traversable.
Confidence
TokenMeans
--rz-conf-high A full track. We would act on this ourselves.
--rz-conf-medium Most of the track. Worth a look before you act.
--rz-conf-low A short fill. Say it in words in the same row as well.
--rz-conf-track The unfilled remainder, so the fill reads as a proportion.

Accessibility

Vitals are read, not operated, so their whole accessibility burden is in being legible and in being announced when they change hands.

  • Colour is never the reading. Law 2 is the accessible-name rule in disguise: the dot on .rz-health is a CSS pseudo-element and is invisible to a screen reader by construction, so the condition has to be in the text or it does not exist. The same holds for .rz-conf — the meter is decoration over the .rz-label beside it.
  • The key/value pairing survives linearisation. Read in DOM order, a row is "Health — Wants water", which is the sentence you want. Keep the key first and keep it a real word, not an abbreviation.
  • A vitals block is not focusable. It holds no control, takes no tab stop, and never interrupts the keyboard walk of the graph — arrow keys with the world focused still follow the veins, and that walk is settled on accessibility.
  • Arriving is announced with the state. Travelling to a node hands the reader that node's recap through RZ.say's live region along with the origin and the new degree — so a screen-reader user learns the plant wants water at the same moment a sighted user sees the amber dot, without hunting for it.
  • Everything is written with logical properties (inline-size, border-inline-start), so the confidence meter fills from the reading edge in RTL and the rows do not point the wrong way.

Render the plane in greyscale with ::before suppressed, then read every .rz-vital-row's textContent: each is a complete statement of that reading, with no residual meaning left in the colour that is now missing.

Using it

Do

Say the condition in the plant's own words and let the dot agree with it — "Wants water", with the watch colour behind it. The sentence carries the meaning; the colour makes it findable at a glance.

Don’t

Ship a row whose only content is a coloured dot, or one labelled "Status: amber". A colour is not a reading, and the system's own state words are not the user's (C19).

Do

Put the confidence in the same row as the value it qualifies, and when it is low, write the doubt out: "We are not sure this is rot rather than underwatering."

Don’t

Print "87% confident". We cannot defend the second digit, and a number invites a decision the reading was never accurate enough to support. Three steps, and words for the low one.

Do

Let a health change repaint the row in place. The card keeps its width, its cell and its rank, and the world view does not redraw its extent (C9).

Don’t

Sort, promote or enlarge a card because it turned ailing. The moment a status can move a plant, you can no longer find your plant where you left it, and the whole geography stops being trustworthy.

Vitals belong to something that has a state. A guide has none — do not give it an empty block for the sake of consistency. If a node has no honest reading to offer, it has no vitals, and its recap does the whole job.
