Components

Action rail

Actions: everything you can do to the plant you are standing on, and nowhere you can go. This page settles what may appear in that panel, what form a mutation takes on the board and in chrome, and how a change is proved to have moved your garden without moving your camera.

What this is

You are standing at Office Fig. It is two days overdue, one of its lower leaves has gone, and there are four things you might do about it: water it, log what you noticed, start a treatment course, put it away for the winter. Those four are the Actions panel — the mutation member of the chrome register, flush to the start edge at the bottom of the viewport, scoped by name to the one focused card and rewritten on every hop.

Actions is one of the two panels that finish the focused card's four answers: Navigate to answers where you can go, and Actions answers what you can do. Nothing in it travels. A mutation and a traversal are told apart before you touch either — in a node by their two forms, in chrome by their leading mark — and this page owns that distinction on the doing side.

Chrome's material, position and voice are owned by chrome. The destination panel beside it is navigate to. What a change does to the card it lands on is owned by node and vitals, and a multi-step change of its own is treatment course. This page states only what a mutation itself decides.

The laws

Law 1 · C14

A mutation announces itself by form, not by label. On the board it is a filled stake and nothing else; in chrome it is an item carrying the filled-square mark (data-means="mutate") and nothing else. “A user can tell navigation from mutation before activating it, from form alone.”

Mask the text of every affordance on screen and screenshot it: each one is a filled stake, an outlined chip with a caret, or a camera button — no fourth form. Assert every .rz-chrome__item inside #rz-actions-body has data-means="mutate", and that no .rz-stake and no .rz-chip exists anywhere inside .rz-chrome--actions.

Law 2 · C15

A mutation moves your garden and never your camera, and it holds no traversal. “No mutating affordance moves the camera, no navigation affordance changes data, and no dialog contains a traversal.”

Read RZ.cam.x, RZ.cam.y, RZ.cam.k and RZ.focus; activate every stake and every item in #rz-actions-body; read all four again. They are identical. Assert no element inside .rz-chrome--actions calls RZ.go or carries data-rz-goto.

Law 3 · C19, C16

Actions belongs to exactly one card and says whose it is. Its header prints the focused node's own name, it is emptied and rewritten from that node's list on every hop, and it never offers a change to something you are not standing on.

Assert #rz-actions-scope reads RZ.nodes[RZ.focus].name, and that every item's text is a member of RZ.ACTIONS[RZ.focus]. Hop three times and assert both still hold, with no item surviving from the previous focus.

Law 4 · C9

The result lands where the thing is. A watering updates that plant's own vitals, recap and health mark on its own card; it does not reflow the board, retitle a panel, or raise a message somewhere else. “No non-structural data change (status, count, new attachment) moves anything.”

Record the computed left and top of every .rz-n and the value of RZ.extent(). Run every action in the panel. Read both again: nothing moved and the world-view extent is unchanged, while the target card's own text has.

Law 5 · C24

Offline, a mutation is refused or queued visibly, one stake at a time. The refusal is on the control you reached for, in that plant's own words — never a banner across the world, which would be a claim about every plant that is perfectly current.

Run RZ.setState("net", "offline"). Assert every .rz-stake computes cursor: not-allowed, that activating an action sets data-refused on that item and announces itself in the live region, and that no element spanning the viewport was added. Assert every hop still works.

Law 6 · C25

A change that fails says so inside the card it was aimed at: what happened, when, what became of what you typed, a retry in place and a second way through. It is never a message that floats free of the plant it concerns.

Fail one action and assert the fact, the time, the fate of the user's data, a retry and a second route are all inside that node's .rz-n__error, and that the camera transform is unchanged.

Actions, running

The same garden, at the same cells, as everywhere else in this system. The Actions panel at the lower start edge is not written into this page: RZ.redrawChrome() fills it from the list handed to RZ.mount, scoped to whichever card is focused. Activate one and watch what does not happen — the board holds still, the focus does not change, and the live region says what was recorded and on what.

Hop to Studio Fig and the panel's header changes to its name and its list to its own — a plant under treatment offers a step to mark done, not a course to begin. The list is the surface's to declare, one entry per thing that is genuinely possible on that node right now.

RZ.mount({
  world: "#rz-world",
  plane: "#rz-plane",
  focus: "n-office",
  path: ["n-garden", "n-office"],
  edges: [
    ["n-garden", "n-office"],
    ["n-garden", "n-studio"],
    ["n-office", "n-problems"],
    ["n-studio", "n-problems"]
  ],
  actions: {
    "n-office": ["Log watering", "Note what you saw", "Start a treatment course"],
    "n-studio": ["Mark step 2 done", "Note what you saw"],
    "n-garden": ["Add a plant"],
    "n-problems": ["Note what you saw"]
  }
});

The shell a page provides

A surface hands the panel three things: the chrome member, a header with the word Actions and a place to print the scope, and an empty body. The entries belong to the runtime. Hand-writing one forks the scope, and Law 3 stops being checkable.

<div class="rz-chrome rz-chrome--actions">
  <p class="rz-chrome__head">
    <span class="rz-chrome__title">Actions</span>
    <span id="rz-actions-scope"></span>
  </p>
  <div id="rz-actions-body"></div>
</div>

And this is the shape the runtime writes into it, one per action — shown so you can recognise it, not so you can author it:

<button type="button" class="rz-chrome__item" data-means="mutate">
  Log watering
</button>
The parts, and who fills them
PartWhere it comes fromWhat it means
.rz-chrome--actions The page, once. The panel: flush to the start edge at the bottom of the viewport, bleeding off both. Chrome material throughout — no shadow, one rung, tracked wide.
#rz-actions-scope RZ.nodes[RZ.focus].name, on every hop. Whose garden this is about to change. Names the plant, never a count and never a cell.
.rz-chrome__item RZ.redrawChrome(), one per action. One change. A button, not a link: nothing here is a place.
data-means="mutate" Set by the runtime on every entry. The leading filled square. Chrome's form of “this changes your garden”, as an arrow is chrome's form of “this travels”.
data-refused Set when the world is offline and the change is queued. The item inks back and takes the refused cursor. The words go to the live region, not to a banner.
.rz-stake The card's own markup, inside .rz-n__full. The same meaning in the node register: filled, squared, a heavy bottom rule. A stake in soil, and never in chrome.
.rz-stake[data-refused="true"], [aria-disabled="true"] The surface, when the change is genuinely impossible. Quieted fill, muted ink, not-allowed. Used for impossible, never for merely discouraged.

Behaviour

On a hop

The panel is emptied and rewritten from the new focus in one pass, alongside Navigate to and the world view, at the end of the hop — never patched by adding the new entries and removing the old, which is how an action belonging to the plant you left survives into the plant you arrived at. Its contents do not animate: the movement you can see during a hop is the camera's, and a list sliding at the same time gives you two things to follow.

A change that needs a value

Some changes need something from you first — how much water, what you noticed, which pot. That form opens inside the focused card's own body, under the stake that asked for it, and the card is already the only thing on screen that scrolls. It is never a dialog: a dialog would cover the world, break the you-are-here, and put a lid on the only surface a traversal can start from (C15). The panel's own items stay single-line, so a change that needs a value is offered in chrome and answered in the card.

Ordering and length

Actions are listed in the order the surface declares them, which is stable across loads, so the position of a change is something you can learn. Put the one you are most likely to want first and the hardest to undo last. Keep the list to what is genuinely possible on this plant right now: a list padded with changes that will be refused teaches you to read every line every time.

Archived, not deleted

Removing a plant is a change of state, not a removal: the card stays on its cell wearing the archived material, and the geography is undisturbed. That is why there is no confirmation step here — nothing in this panel is irreversible enough to earn one, and a confirmation is a dialog by another name.

When the world is offline

Travel keeps working; changing things does not. Each stake takes the refused cursor, each item you activate says for itself whether it was refused or queued, and the plant states its own staleness in its own words on its own card. The system-wide behaviour of every degraded state is owned by degradation, and you can walk them all from the probe rather than from a hand-written specimen.

Accessibility

  • The meaning is a shape first. Mutation is a filled square in chrome and a filled, squared stake in a card; traversal is an arrow and an outlined pill with a caret. The two survive a greyscale screenshot, so the register does not rest on colour (C14).
  • Real buttons, in order. Every action is a <button type="button">: reachable by Tab, activated by Enter or Space, announced as a button. Reading order matches visual order and the panel is never reordered in CSS.
  • Arrow keys are the graph's. The panel has no arrow-key traversal of its own, because arrow keys move you along the veins from wherever you are standing. Nothing in Actions is a direction (C18).
  • The scope is text. The plant's name is in the header as words, so a screen reader reads whose garden is about to change with the panel itself rather than inferring it from the camera.
  • Refusal is announced, not implied. A refused or queued change speaks into the live region in the plant's own words. Use aria-disabled="true" rather than a visual quieting alone, so the state is spoken; when the reason matters, put it on the card beside the vitals.
  • The result is read where it happened. A recorded change updates that card's own vitals and recap, so re-reading the focused card hears the new state. The announcement follows the data.
  • Direction is logical. The panel is placed with inset-inline-start and its marks lead with gap in the inline direction, so in a right-to-left reading the panel moves to the other side and the mark still leads.

The system-wide rules for focus, contrast and announcement live on accessibility.

Usage guidance

Do

Keep Actions to changes and let every destination be a vein. “Log watering”, “Note what you saw”, “Start a treatment course” — each one moves your garden and leaves you exactly where you are standing (C15).

Don’t

Slip a destination into the panel — “Open Problems”, “Back to my garden”. Both meanings then live in one list, the leading mark stops telling them apart, and the board never shows the distance being crossed (C14). Every destination belongs in navigate to.

Do

Land the result on the card: the health mark moves from Watch to Thriving, the recap re-reads “watered today”, and nothing else on the plane so much as shifts (C9).

Don’t

Recentre, re-fit or re-rank the board after a change “so the user can see it worked”. The camera belongs to the focus, and a change that moves it costs the user the place they had learned (C15, C16).

Do

Say what is happening when a change cannot run: “Offline — this watering is queued and will be recorded when you are back.” On that plant, in that plant's words (C24).

Don’t

Hide the action while it is impossible, or draw a banner across the whole world. The first makes you hunt for something you saw yesterday; the second says every plant is stale when most of them are current.

Writing the entries

  • Name the change, not the mechanism: “Log watering”, never “Submit”, “Save” or “OK”.
  • Start with a verb and stay under four words, so the panel is read at a glance in chrome's one rung.
  • Say what the garden gets, not what the app does: “Start a treatment course”, not “Create record”.
  • No ellipsis to hint at a form. The form opens in the card under your hand, and you will see it.
  • If a change is uncertain in its effect, say so on the card beside it — a button never hedges, and the honest sentence belongs in prose where it can be read.

Where else this appears

The register this panel belongs to and its material are chrome; its sibling panel of destinations is navigate to. The card a change lands on is node, its four answers are focus panel, and the instruments a change moves are vitals and treatment course. The board it must not disturb is world, and the picture of where you stand is minimap. The two forms and the cursors that go with them are interaction and cursors and iconography; refusal, queueing and failure are degradation; and the mode in which every stake goes inert is arrange mode.
