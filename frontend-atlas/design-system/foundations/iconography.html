Foundations

Iconography

Rhizome has no icon set. Meaning is drawn — a form, a rim, a fill, a rule — and the two-form grammar that tells travelling apart from changing your garden is geometry the stylesheet owns, not a picture anyone chooses.

What this is

Your garden is already a picture. Every plant, species, problem and journal entry is a body on one plane with veins between them, and a leaf glyph pinned to a plant or a droplet pinned to a watering control would put a second picture inside the first. So this system ships no icon font, no sprite sheet, no rz-icon class, and no place a component reserves for a glyph. What it ships instead is a small vocabulary of drawn marks: the filled stake, the outlined chip and its caret, the leading mark inside chrome, the graduation on a card's head, the health bead, the confidence track, the dashes on an unknown rim.

Every one of those comes out of assets/system.css, from tokens, with no image file and no network request. Which means a theme round changes what they look like by changing a token — and cannot change what they mean, because the meaning lives in the class, not in a picture someone picked.

This page settles one thing and defers the rest. It settles that the grammar of C14 — "A user can tell navigation from mutation before activating it, from form alone." — is carried by drawn form. What the two forms do, and what happens when you activate one, belongs to action-rail, navigate-to and interaction-and-cursors. What colour a mark takes belongs to colors. How long a mark's rule is at each rank belongs to node.

The laws

Law 1 · C14

There is no icon set. A mark in Rhizome is geometry a class draws, and it is named in words beside itself.

Load any page of the running system and search it: no icon font is loaded, no @font-face is declared, and the only <svg> in the document is .rz-veins (the graph) and .rz-map (the world view). Assert document.querySelectorAll('.rz-n img, .rz-n svg, .rz-stake img, .rz-chip img').length === 0 — no affordance contains an image, and no state is announced by a picture.

Law 2 · C14, C15

The two forms are drawn by the class, never by a glyph placed inside them. A stake is filled, squared and weighted at its foot; a chip is outlined, pilled, and its caret arrives from the stylesheet.

For every .rz-stake and .rz-chip assert el.querySelector('svg, img') is null, and that getComputedStyle(chip, '::after').content is the single caret character. Remove every text node from both and they are still two visibly different forms — the fill and the rim are the message, not the label.

Law 3 · C14

Neither form may enter chrome. Inside chrome the same two meanings become a leading mark on an item — a square in the mutation ink, an arrow in the traversal ink — and nothing else.

Assert document.querySelectorAll('.rz-chrome .rz-stake, .rz-chrome .rz-chip').length === 0, and that every .rz-chrome__item carries data-means="mutate" or data-means="travel". The blur test that chrome owns then still holds: no soft bright rectangle appears in chrome.

Law 4 · C12

A mark is never the only carrier of a meaning that colour alone distinguishes. Every mark that says something has a word next to it, and every mark that says nothing is inert.

Render the page in greyscale and read every mark: the two forms still differ (fill against rim), health still reads (its word is beside the bead), the chrome marks still differ (square against arrow). Then assert every .rz-chrome__item, .rz-health and .rz-conf has non-empty text in or beside it — a bead with no word is a defect.

Law 5 · C18

A page never types a direction. Marks are placed with logical properties, and the two directional characters the system uses are supplied by the stylesheet, so they are one place to change and not a hundred.

Search every page's markup for &rarr;, &raquo; and their literal characters: none appear. Set dir="rtl" on the document and assert every mark has swapped side — the chip's caret, the chrome item's leading mark, the graduation on a card's head — because all of them are placed with inline-start/inline-end, never left/right.

Being honest about a limit, in the app's own spirit. Law 5's placement mirrors today, but the two characters themselves do not: .rz-chip::after and .rz-chrome__item[data-means="travel"]::before hold a right-pointing character, and a right-to-left build needs those two declarations restated under [dir="rtl"]. That is a two-line change in assets/system.css, and the amendment path in extending this system is how it gets made. It has not been made yet, and no page should pretend otherwise.

The two forms, drawn

This is the whole grammar a user has to learn, and they learn it once. A filled stake changes your garden. An outlined chip with a caret takes you somewhere. Both specimens below are the real classes — no mock-up, no drawing of a button.

Mask the words and the row still sorts itself into two groups: two solid blocks with a heavy foot, two open pills with a caret. That is the test C14 asks for, and it is why no third form is allowed inside a node — a third shape would make the sort ambiguous, and there is nothing a third shape could mean that is not already one of these two.

The camera cluster is the one thing that changes neither your garden nor where you are. It gets its own quiet square, and it lives in chrome:

Plus and minus are the only two characters this system types into a control, and they earn it: they are arithmetic rather than pictures, they read the same in every language PlantPal ships in, they are symmetrical so they need no mirroring, and they belong to an instrument that moves the view rather than your garden. A character that fails any of those four tests does not get in.

Inside chrome, a form becomes a mark

Chrome is a different material from a card, so it cannot borrow a card's forms — see chrome for why. The two meanings survive the crossing as a leading mark: a small square in the mutation ink, an arrow in the traversal ink.

Those are the real items, shown out of their panel on purpose: a chrome member is fixed to a viewport edge and bleeds off it, so this page cannot stand one inside a figure without overriding the very thing that makes it chrome. The assembled Navigate-to panel is on navigate-to. In the app the items sit inside their member, and it is written like this:

<div class="rz-chrome rz-chrome--rail">
  <div class="rz-chrome__head">
    <span class="rz-chrome__title">Navigate to</span>
    <span>3 veins</span>
  </div>
  <button type="button" class="rz-chrome__item" data-means="travel">Fiddle-leaf Fig
    <small>Ficus lyrata &middot; 12 plants</small></button>
  <button type="button" class="rz-chrome__item" data-means="mutate">Log watering
    <small>Today, for this plant</small></button>
</div>

The rest of the mark vocabulary

These are all of them. If the mark you want is not here, this system does not have it, and the answer is a word in a .rz-label — not a new shape.

Every drawn mark
MarkDrawn bySays
The stake .rz-stake — fill plus a heavy foot rule Activating this changes your garden's data.
The chip and its caret .rz-chip — rim, pill, ::after Activating this travels a vein you can already see.
The leading mark .rz-chrome__item::before, keyed by data-means The same two meanings, in chrome's own material.
The graduation .rz-n__grad — length is rank, colour is kind How far this is from you, and what kind of thing it is, at a size where its name cannot be read. Owned by node.
The health bead .rz-health Thriving, stable, watch, ailing or lost — always with the word beside it.
The confidence track .rz-conf__fill How sure the assistant is. Graded HIGH, MEDIUM or LOW in text as well.
The step mark .rz-course__mark and the step's start rule Where you are in a treatment course, and whether a step is done or due.
The dashed rim .rz-n[data-state="unknown"] and [data-state="empty"] Not fetched yet, or fetched and genuinely nothing. Two different dashes for two different pieces of news — see degradation.
The you-are-here .rz-map__you, .rz-map__you-halo Where you are standing, at every zoom. Owned by minimap.
The focus ring --rz-focus on :focus-visible The keyboard is here. It belongs to focus and may never be borrowed.
The mote field .rz-motes Nothing at all. It is texture, it carries no state, and it must never be made to carry one.

Two of those are decoration and the rest are load-bearing. The mote field says nothing; the focus ring says exactly one thing. Everything else in the table is state, and state is always doubled in words.

What a theme fills for the marks
TokenMeans
--rz-mutate The stake's fill and the chrome mutation square. This ink appears nowhere else on screen.
--rz-vital The chip's ink and the chrome travel arrow — the traversal voice.
--rz-vital-edge The chip's rim. The rim is what makes a chip an outline instead of a fill, so a theme may re-key it and may never remove it.
--rz-corner-chip The chip's pill. Against --rz-corner-inner on the stake, this is half of the form difference.
--rz-strong The stake's foot rule and the graduation's weight — the mark that reads as pressed into soil.
--rz-focus The focus ring, drawn clear of the element's own edge. No mark may be placed where it would hide this.

The marks are tokens, so they follow the key

Because no mark is a picture, a live palette change re-keys the whole vocabulary — the stake's fill, the chip's rim, the caret's ink, the health bead — with nothing to re-export and nothing to redraw. Switch the key below and the forms hold their meaning while changing their colour. The buttons are wired by RZ.mount({}) at the foot of this page; nothing here is re-implemented.

That second half also shows Law 4 doing its work. The bead and the track carry colour; the words carry the claim. Take the colour away and you still know that one plant is being watched and that the assistant is not sure.

A refusal is drawn on the form itself rather than announced somewhere else. Take the world offline from the probe below and the stake goes visibly refused while the chip keeps travelling — being offline does not stop you looking around your own garden.

Accessibility

No mark in Rhizome is the only carrier of its meaning, and that is not a concession — it is PlantPal's own instruction to its assistant applied to geometry. If the system knows something about a plant, it says it. A coloured bead on its own is not saying it.

Colour is never the difference between two marks. The stake and the chip differ in fill and rim before they differ in ink. The chrome marks differ in shape — a square against an arrow. The unknown rim is dashed, not merely dimmer. Test any mark you use by asking what is left of it in greyscale; if the answer is "nothing", the mark is wrong.

Decorative marks are hidden from the accessibility tree and interactive ones are named. .rz-motes is a canvas with nothing to announce, and .rz-veins is presentational because the graph is announced instead: RZ.present() writes the focus, what you crossed and how many veins lead out of it into the live region. That is what C18 asks for, and accessibility owns it in full.

When a control's visible content is a character — the two camera buttons, and only those — the word goes beside it in .rz-sr. Never a title attribute: a keyboard user cannot reach one and a touch user cannot either.

<button type="button" class="rz-cam-btn">&minus;<span class="rz-sr">Zoom out</span></button>
<button type="button" class="rz-cam-btn">+<span class="rz-sr">Zoom in</span></button>

The focus ring is not one of the decorative marks and must never be treated as one. It is drawn with an offset, clear of the element's own edge, so it survives against a filled stake and an outlined chip alike — and it survives forced-colours modes, where a soft halo does not. No mark may be positioned so that it clips the ring.

Every mark is placed with logical properties, so the caret, the leading mark and the graduation all move to the other side under RTL on their own. If you find yourself nudging a mark with margin-left, the mark is right and the nudge is wrong. The limit noted under Law 5 is real and stands until that amendment is made.

Do / Don't

Do

Write the verb and the noun in full and let the form carry the grammar: Log watering on a stake, Fiddle-leaf Fig on a chip. Whoever reads either one knows what will happen before they touch it.

Don't

Put a droplet glyph on the watering control and a leaf glyph on the plant, then shorten both labels to fit them. There is now a symbol set to learn, and nobody learns one for an app they opened because a plant looks unwell (C14).

Do

Let a mark that means nothing keep meaning nothing. The mote field is texture: inert, seeded so it does not reshuffle between loads, and it moves no node.

Don't

Make a mark move to get attention. A pulsing bead is alarm, and this product does not raise its voice — an ailing plant states what is wrong inside its own card, with the time and a way forward (C25).

Three more rules follow and are worth stating plainly. Never put a picture inside a node — the world is the picture, and a glyph competes with the card's own name at exactly the sizes where names are already hard to read. Never make a mark a control: the bead, the graduation and the track are drawn, not clicked; anything clickable is a card, a stake, a chip or a chrome item. Never invent a shape — if the vocabulary has no mark for what you mean, either the meaning belongs in words, or you have found a genuine gap, and the amendment path in extending this system is how you close it. Do not close it locally in a page.
