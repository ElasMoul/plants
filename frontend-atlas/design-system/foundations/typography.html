Foundations

Typography

The prominence ladder is a set of ratios, not a set of sizes: a theme chooses the pitch, and the steps have to stay visibly stepped.

What this is

Your garden is one plane, and at any moment most of it is small. A card two hops out is ten rems wide; a card on the fringe is smaller still, and you will never read its name. Type in Rhizome therefore has two jobs at once — it has to be readable on the card you are standing in, and it has to keep saying this one matters more than that one when nothing on the card is legible any more. This page settles the ladder those two jobs share, the voices that speak on it, and what a theme round is allowed to move.

The ladder is named in rungs — --rz-rung-100 down to --rz-rung-12 — and every rung is a layer-3 property pointing at a layer-1 size. A component asks for a rung. Nothing in this system asks for a size.

The laws

Law 1 · C12

The ratios are the law; the pitch is a theme variable. A theme may drop --rz-rung-100 an octave and stay conformant, provided the steps stay visibly stepped. C12 reads: “With the text blurred on a frozen frame, the hierarchy is still readable from scale alone, on the 100/75/55/30/18-25 ladder.”

Read the rungs back with getComputedStyle(document.documentElement) and divide each by --rz-rung-30. The quotients are strictly decreasing and no two adjacent quotients are within 5% of each other. Then halve every --rz-size-* and re-run: the quotients are unchanged.

Law 2 · C12

A rung is asked for by name, never by value. No component and no page states a font size.

Search assets/system.css, assets/docs.css and every page for font-size:. Every declaration's value is var(--rz-rung-…), with the single exception of the inline-code 0.92em in docs.css, which is relative and claims no rung of its own.

Law 3 · C12

Each voice is bound to a meaning: the hand writes names and prose, the label writes machine facts in monospace, and the plate writes the botanical binomial in italic and writes nothing else.

For every element on a rendered board, assert its computed font-family resolves to --rz-face-name, --rz-face-body, --rz-face-label or --rz-face-latin, and that every element resolving to --rz-face-latin carries .rz-binomial. A family on screen that is none of these is a defect.

Law 4 · C3, C12

Rank steps the name down the ladder, and the recap sheds its second line before it sheds its first. A card never truncates its way down; it drops a whole rung and a whole line.

Render one node at each of the four ranks and read the computed font-size of .rz-n__name: four values, all distinct, all members of the rung set. At data-rank="fringe" assert .rz-n__recap-second computes to display: none while .rz-n__recap does not.

Law 5 · C17, C19

Prose is measured, not stretched. Reading text sits inside --rz-measure-prose however wide the focused card grows.

Open the focused card at the zoom maximum and at the widest viewport you have. Every paragraph inside .rz-n__body has a computed max-inline-size of --rz-measure-prose, and no line in it exceeds that measure.

A theme round moves the pitch and, if it wants, the families. It does not move the rung names, the order of the ratios, or the binding of a voice to a meaning. If a theme needs a rung the ladder does not have, that is an amendment — see extending this system. This page made none.

Accessibility

Type here has to survive being resized by someone other than us. Every rung is declared in rem, so the reader's own browser font-size scales the whole ladder together and the ratios — which are the law — hold at any base. Nothing is declared in px, and nothing is capped in a way that refuses a larger base.

  • The label voice is uppercase by styling (text-transform), never by the words themselves, so a screen reader is given “Last watered”, not the letters one at a time. Write the source in sentence case, always.
  • Text that exists only for the reader who cannot see the board — the you-are-here rewrite after a hop, a direction with no neighbour — goes in .rz-sr and carries no rung at all. It is announced, not read. Accessibility owns what gets said and when.
  • The recap's second line leaves the accessibility tree at the same moment it leaves the screen — display: none, not a faded opacity — so a fringe card is not quietly longer to a screen reader than it is to your eye.

Where a size and a colour together carry a meaning, the size is the part that survives. The kind channel is a colour and is never the only signal; colors owns that rule, and it is the graduation's length that carries rank at fringe scale, where no text is legible at all.

One limit, honestly: at the largest browser base sizes a name at rank fringe can meet its card's height cap and clip. We think that is the right trade — a fringe card is a landmark, not a label, and the focused card is never clipped at any zoom. If you find a case where it costs a real reader something, that is worth an amendment, not a local override.

Using it

Do

Give a card less type as it gets further out: a rung down for the name, and the second recap line gone. The card stays whole, and it still says what kind of thing it is.

Don't

Keep the same rung and clip the name with an ellipsis. An ellipsis is the same size as the text it ate, so the hierarchy stops being readable from scale alone (C12).

Do

Set the binomial in the plate voice and let it be the only italic on the screen: <span class="rz-binomial">Ficus lyrata</span>.

Don't

Reach for italic for emphasis somewhere else. The moment a second italic appears, the binomial stops being recognisable at a glance and becomes something you have to stop and read.

Do

Ask for var(--rz-rung-25) when you want a recap to read as a recap.

Don't

Add a rung between two existing ones because one card felt crowded. An extra step makes the ladder harder to read as a ladder, and the crowding is nearly always the density rule rather than the type — that rule is owned by aggregate.
