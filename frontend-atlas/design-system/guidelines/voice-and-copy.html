Guidelines

Voice and copy

Every word this system puts on screen. This page settles which register each surface speaks in, what a card says while it is waiting, what it says when it is stale, unknown, empty or broken, and what your garden says out loud when you arrive somewhere.

What this is

PlantPal talks to you the way a friend who knows plants would: warm, plain, specific, and honest when it is not sure. Its own instruction to its assistant — if unsure, say so; never guess about plant health — is the sentence this whole page grows from. Rhizome inherits that voice and gives it a second job, because a world you move a camera over asks things of copy that a stack of screens never does.

Three of those jobs are new here. Your cards are read at four different sizes, so a recap has to survive being cut to one line at the fringe. Your camera moves, and someone who cannot see it move still has to be told where they landed. And your garden degrades in pieces rather than all at once — one card slow, one card stale, one card broken, the rest of it perfectly fine — so almost every sentence about state belongs to a single card and is written in that card's own words.

Two registers, and they never borrow from each other. Chrome speaks in tracked uppercase labels: apparatus, naming itself. A card speaks in sentences: content, talking to you. The material difference is owned by chrome; what you find here is the wording that goes with it.

The laws

Law 1 · C14

The form already says whether a control changes your garden or moves you, so the label only has to name the thing. A stake is a verb phrase about your plants — "Log watering", "Mark step 2 done". A chip is the name of the place you would arrive at — "Problems", "Office Fig". Never "click here", never "go to", never a stake that begins with "view".

Mask the form of every affordance and read the labels alone. Each is either a verb phrase naming an act on your garden or a bare place name, and the two lists do not overlap — no label appears in both.

Law 2 · C19

A card speaks the domain and never the system's private vocabulary. The words cell, lattice, slot, ring, band, row, column, rank, node and edge list are how this system talks to itself. What you read is "12 plants · 1 overdue today", "Problems · 3 active", "Three veins lead out of this plant".

Read the rendered text of every card, exclude the chrome apparatus labels, and search it for that word list. It returns nothing, and every collapsed recap names a domain thing and a count.

Law 3 · C22

A wait is written inside the card that is waiting, and it says what is being waited for and roughly how long that usually takes. "Still identifying this photo — usually about 15 seconds." The word loading is not in this system's vocabulary, and neither is a bare ellipsis standing in for a sentence.

Set data-speed="slow" from the probe. Every card carrying data-pending="true" has a non-empty .rz-n__wait sentence naming the work, no card outside that set says anything new, and a case-insensitive search of the running app's own text for "loading" and "please wait" returns nothing.

Law 4 · C23, C24

Unknown, empty and stale are three different sentences and are never one banner. An unknown place says it has not been fetched and offers to fetch it. An empty place says what could go there. A stale card says, in its own words, when it was last true — and two cards last checked at different times say different things.

Set data-graph="partial": the unknown card's sentence offers a fetch and the empty card's sentence offers a beginning, and neither text could be swapped for the other. Then set data-net="offline": collect every visible .rz-n__stale string, assert at least two differ, and assert nothing outside the cards announces the disconnection.

Law 5 · C25

A failure sentence carries four things and is written inside the card that failed: the fact in plain words, the time it happened, what became of what you typed or photographed, and two ways forward — the retry in place and one other route. It never says "something went wrong".

Read the .rz-n__error text of a failed card and find all four: a fact, a time, an explicit statement about the user's own data, and two distinct controls. The camera transform is unchanged, and no text about that failure appears anywhere else in the document.

Law 6 · C18, C21

Every arrival is spoken in one sentence that names where you came from, anything you crossed without opening, and how many veins lead out of where you now are. An arrival you did not start by clicking a card — a search hit, a notification — says the distance first, before the news.

Hop and read #rz-live's text: it names the origin, the destination, any card passed through, and a vein count equal to RZ.degree(RZ.focus). Trigger a search arrival and assert the hop count is spoken before the news, from that same one sentence-writing path.

Specimens

What your garden says when you arrive

Click any card, or tab into the world and use the arrow keys. The bar along the bottom edge is the live region: it shows the same sentence a screen reader hears, which is how you proofread it. Law 6 is being written by RZ.go(), not by this page.

What a card says while it is waiting

Identifying a photo genuinely takes five to fifteen seconds here, so the wait is real and the sentence has to earn it. It names the work, it gives you a length to expect, it says what happened to your photo, and the skeleton under it is shaped like the write-up that is coming.

What a card says when it breaks

The fact, the time, the fate of your data, and two ways on. The camera does not move and the rest of your garden is untouched, so the sentence can afford to be calm.

Saying what you do not know

PlantPal identifies plants and reads problems from photographs, and it is wrong often enough that hedging is part of the product rather than a softener on the copy. Confidence is stated as a word and a level, and a low reading always names the thing that would raise it.

Three rules hold the hedge in place. Say the level in a word as well as in the bar, because a bar at a third of its track is not a sentence. Name the limitation rather than the doubt — "the photo is dark" is useful, "I am not certain" is not. And never hedge something you do know: the day you last watered a plant is not a guess and must not be dressed as one.

Accessibility

In a world with a camera in it, the copy is the accessible layer. Everything below is a wording requirement; the markup side of the same ground lives on accessibility.

  • The arrival sentence is the whole of the navigation for anyone who cannot see the travel. It names the origin, anything crossed, and the number of veins out, in that order, so a listener can rebuild the geography from the sentence alone.
  • Every colour carries a word. Health is written out — "Watch — the lower leaves are yellowing" — beside the dot that colours it. Confidence prints its level. Kind prints its name in .rz-n__kind above the coloured graduation. Turn the screen greyscale and nothing is lost.
  • A refusal is spoken, not implied. A stake that cannot run says why in its own words, and a direction with no vein that way says so out loud rather than doing nothing.
  • Copy never points. No sentence says "on the left", "in the panel on the right" or "the button below" — name the thing instead. The layout mirrors in RTL and the camera moves besides, so a direction written into a sentence is a sentence that will become wrong.
  • Punctuation carries nothing. The middle dot in a recap is a separator, not a meaning. Anything a reader has to understand is a word, because a screen reader may not read the dot at all.
  • The live region is polite and one sentence long. It is never used for a failure — a failure lives in the card that failed, where a keyboard user can reach the retry — and it is never interrupted by a second sentence about the same hop.

Do and don't

Do

Name the work and give a length you can keep: "Still identifying this photo — usually about 15 seconds. Your photo is saved either way." The card holds its place while it says it.

Don't

Write the word "loading", or leave an ellipsis to do the work of a sentence. Neither tells you what is happening or whether what you just took survived it (C22).

Do

Let each card date itself: "Last checked 40 minutes ago, before you went offline." The card beside it, checked at breakfast, says something different, and both are true.

Don't

Put one sentence about the connection across the top of the world. It is a claim about every card at once, and it is false for the ones that are current (C24).

Do

Hedge with the reason: "This looks like underwatering, but the photo is dark and the soil is not in frame." The reader learns what to photograph next.

Don't

State a diagnosis flat — "Your fig has root rot" — from a reading the system itself grades low. That is the one thing this app told its assistant never to do.

Do

Label by form: a stake reads Log watering, a chip reads Problems. Read as two lists, the verbs and the places never mix.

Don't

Write "View problems" on a chip or "Go to watering" on a stake. The form has already said which it is, so the extra verb only gives you a way to contradict it (C14).
