Guidelines

Interaction and cursors

The board pans and the node reads. This page settles which cursor every surface in your garden shows, which gesture does what, and which handler wins when a pointer goes down somewhere two of them could claim it.

What this is

Your garden is one plane you move a camera over, and on that plane sit cards you read. Those are two different things to do with a mouse, and the pointer has to say which one you are about to do before you commit to it. Drag the dark between the cards and the world slides under you. Put the pointer on a card's paragraph and it is a paragraph: an ordinary document, with an I-beam over its text, a pointer over its links, and a bare wheel that scrolls it rather than zooming the world out from under your hands.

That split is the whole of this page. It is the reason a plant's own write-up can carry a real link to the disease it mentions and still not fight the board it floats on, and the reason you can put your pointer down anywhere on a distant card — anywhere at all, not on a title you have to aim for — and travel there.

What a card is belongs to node; what the plane is belongs to world. The two forms a control may take, and the timing every hop runs on, are owned by action rail, navigate to and traversal. This page states only what the pointer does and who gets the event.

The laws

Law 1 · C16, C17

The board takes the grab cursor and the node takes the document cursors. grab on the world, grabbing while you are panning, and inside a card the ordinary ones: default over text, pointer over anything you can activate, not-allowed on a control that is genuinely refused. There is no grab cursor inside a card anywhere, at any rank, in any state.

Walk every element under .rz-n with getComputedStyle and assert none computes cursor: grab or grabbing. Assert .rz-world computes grab, and that it computes grabbing exactly while data-panning="true" is set.

Law 2 · C15, C16

Pointer precedence is declared, not discovered. Three rules, in order: the pan handler returns immediately if the pointer went down inside a card; a card's travel listener stands aside for the card's own controls, in-card hops, stakes and links; in arrange mode the card is the handle and everything else is inert.

Press and drag from a card's own body: the camera transform is unchanged and data-panning was never set. Click a .rz-chip, a .rz-stake and a link inside a non-focused card: the chip's own destination is reached, the stake changes data without a hop, and neither activates the card's own travel. Press and drag from the plane between cards: the camera moved and RZ.focus did not.

Law 3 · C17

A bare wheel belongs to the card. Alt plus wheel zooms the world; a wheel with no modifier is left alone, so it scrolls the focused card's own body — the one thing on screen that scrolls. “Nothing other than the focused node's body scrolls.”

Read RZ.cam.k, wheel over the world with no modifier, read it again: unchanged. Wheel with altKey and it has changed, clamped between zoom.min and zoom.max. Then assert that the only element in the document with scrollHeight > clientHeight is the focused node's .rz-n__body, and that document.body has no scrollbar.

Law 4 · C15, C16

Looking around is not navigating. Pan, zoom, fit and recentre are a third category of control: they move the view and change neither your data nor where you are standing. They are the only affordances on screen that are neither a filled stake nor an outlined chip.

Record RZ.focus and every node's data-rank. Pan, zoom both ways, fit, recentre. Read both again: identical. Assert every camera control is a .rz-cam-btn and that none of them calls RZ.go.

Law 5 · C15, C16

The whole surface of a non-focused card is the target, and the focused card is not a target at all. A card you are not standing on takes pointer across its entire face; the card you are standing on takes default, because it is a document you are reading and not somewhere to go.

Assert every .rz-n without data-focus="true" computes cursor: pointer on its own box, and that clicking its padding — not a heading, not a link — reaches that node. Assert the element carrying data-focus="true" computes cursor: default and that clicking its prose does nothing.

Law 6 · C15

Arrange mode takes move, deliberately not grab. Grab means the board is about to slide; move means this one card is about to be placed. Two different promises, so two different cursors — and in arrange the world itself drops to default, because the board is not what you are dragging.

Run RZ.setArrange(true). Assert every .rz-n computes cursor: move, that .rz-world computes default, and that every .rz-stake and .rz-chip computes pointer-events: none. Leave the mode and assert all three revert.

The laws, running

The same four plants, at the same cells, as everywhere else in this system — but read this one with your hands. Drag the dark between the cards and the world moves. Hold Alt and wheel, and it scales around your pointer. Put the pointer on Studio Fig anywhere and it is a target; put it on the paragraph inside Office Fig and it is text, with a real link in it that travels without the card's own travel firing underneath it. Nothing you do here with the camera changes which plant you are standing on.

The gesture line at the lower start edge is a chrome member like any other: it states the two gestures that are not discoverable by looking, in chrome's own voice, and it never says anything that a form on the board already says for itself.

RZ.mount({
  world: "#rz-world",
  plane: "#rz-plane",
  focus: "n-office",
  path: ["n-garden", "n-office"],
  edges: [
    ["n-garden", "n-office"],
    ["n-garden", "n-studio"],
    ["n-office", "n-problems"],
    ["n-studio", "n-problems"]
  ]
});

The cursor table

One row per surface. A theme may not add a row and may not re-key one: a cursor is a promise about what the next press will do, and a seventh promise is a seventh thing to learn.

Cursor law
SurfaceCursorThe promise it makes
.rz-world grab The board. Press here and the whole world slides with you.
.rz-world[data-panning="true"] grabbing You have it. Set by the runtime for exactly the length of the drag.
.rz-n, not focused pointer Somewhere you can go. The whole face of the card, not a target inside it.
.rz-n[data-focus="true"] default Where you already are. A document to read, not a place to travel to.
Links and buttons inside the focused card pointer Ordinary document behaviour, restored explicitly so the card's own default does not swallow it.
.rz-stake[data-refused="true"], [aria-disabled="true"] not-allowed This change is genuinely impossible right now — offline, or refused. Never used for merely discouraged.
.rz-chrome__item[data-refused="true"] not-allowed The same refusal, in chrome's register, on the item you reached for rather than on a banner.
.rz-n in arrange mode move This card is about to be placed. Deliberately not grab: the board is not what moves.
.rz-shell in overview mode zoom-in The whole application, shrunk to a card. Click it to dive back in.

The gesture map

What each gesture reaches
GestureHandlerWhat it does, and what it does not
Drag on the plane The world's pointerdown Pans. Changes no data and no focus. Refuses to begin if the press landed inside a card.
Alt + wheel The world's wheel, non-passive Zooms about the pointer, clamped to zoom.min…zoom.max by RZ.clampZoom. Without Alt the handler returns and the event is the card's.
Bare wheel The browser Scrolls the focused card's body, and only that. Nothing else in the document scrolls.
Click a card you are not on RZ.wireNode → RZ.go(id) Travels, on the one timing, along the real edges. Stands aside for the card's own controls.
Click your own card Nothing Returns at once. You cannot travel to where you are.
Camera cluster RZ.zoomBy, RZ.fitAll, RZ.recentre Moves the view. RZ.focus is untouched, and recentre says so out loud.
Arrow keys on the world RZ.neighbourInDirection Travels to the neighbour that actually lies that way, computed from real positions. A direction with no neighbour is spoken, not ignored.
Drag a card, arrange mode only RZ.beginDrag Stores an offset against that node's record. Changes no data and performs no traversal.

Precedence, in one line each

The pan handler's first act is to look up from the pointer's target for a card, and give up if it finds one. The card's travel listener's first act is to look for anything the user could have meant instead — this is the stand-aside list, and it is the reason a link in a plant's write-up works like a link:

var STAND_ASIDE = "[data-rz-goto], .rz-stake, .rz-chip, button, a, input, textarea, select";

/* pan: rule 1 */
if (ev.target.closest(".rz-n")) return;

/* travel: rule 2 */
if (ev.target.closest(STAND_ASIDE)) return;

Rule three needs no test of its own: arrange is a mode, and both handlers above return on RZ.arrange before they do anything else. What the mode is for belongs to arrange mode.

Accessibility

  • No gesture is the only way to anything. Panning has recentre and fit beside it in the camera cluster; zoom has two buttons; travelling has the arrow keys, the destination panel and the you-are-here trail. A pointer gesture is always the fast path and never the path.
  • The keyboard follows the graph, not the DOM. An arrow key proposes the neighbour that genuinely lies that way, from real positions. The behaviour and its announcement are owned by accessibility; what belongs here is that the pointer and the keyboard reach the same set of destinations, because both read the one edge list.
  • A direction with nothing in it is spoken. Silence reads as a broken key. The live region says there is no vein that way, and you are still where you were.
  • Every target is the whole card. A card you are not standing on is one large target, which is the easiest thing on the surface to hit with an unsteady hand or a head pointer. Nothing requires you to land on a word.
  • Refusal is never cursor-only. not-allowed is a hint for people who can see it, so every refused control also carries aria-disabled or data-refused and says its reason in the live region.
  • Focus outlines are never removed. The system's :focus-visible ring is drawn on the card, on every chrome item and on every camera button, and a card reached by travel takes focus at the end of the hop.
  • Logical properties throughout. Nothing in this page's rules names left or right, so a right-to-left garden pans, arranges and reads the same way round.

Using it

Do

Leave the bare wheel alone and put zoom behind Alt. The card is the thing with reading in it, so the unmodified gesture is the card's, and the world only scales when you ask it to.

Don’t

Bind a bare wheel to zoom. A user scrolling a plant's journal would pull the whole garden away under the sentence they were reading, and the card that must never be clipped would be the first thing to go (C17).

Do

Let the whole face of a distant card travel, and let its own stake and chip stand in front of it. One target, one exception list, both declared.

Don’t

Make only the card's name clickable and leave the rest of it inert. It shrinks the target to a word, and it teaches that some of a card is live and some is not — which stops being true the moment that card is focused and its prose carries links (C16).

Do

Show move when a card is about to be placed and grab when the board is about to slide. Two promises, two cursors.

Don’t

Put a grab cursor on a card, in arrange mode or out of it. It says the plane is coming with you, and then only the card moves — which is the one thing arrange mode must never be ambiguous about (C15).
