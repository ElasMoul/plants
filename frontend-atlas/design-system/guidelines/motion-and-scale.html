Guidelines

Motion and scale

Two channels, two questions, and no swapping them: motion answers how you got here, scale answers what you are looking at. This page settles what happens where the two meet.

What this is

You click the Fiddle-leaf Fig. The camera pulls across your garden along the vein that joins it to the Office Fig, and when it comes to rest the Fig is the big card in front of you and the Office Fig is a smaller one, one hop back, still on screen. Two things happened in that one crossing, and they are carrying two different facts. The movement said which way you went and how far. The size says which card you are now reading. A garden where those two agree is a place; a garden where they disagree is a slideshow.

This page owns the join, and nothing else. It does not set the clock — there is one navigation timing and it lives on motion. It does not choreograph the crossing — the route, the polyline and what lights on the way through are settled on traversal. It does not define rank or the widths rank is spent on — those belong to node and the ladder to typography. What is left over is the part every one of those pages assumes and none of them states: which channel is allowed to carry which fact, and what must be true of the two together at the end of a hop.

Reach for this page when something in a view feels wrong and no single component is at fault. Two cards competing to be the subject, a change that wants to be staged as a journey, a zoom that quietly reads as promotion, a size that grows to say look at this — those are all one fault seen from four sides. A signal was spent on something that did not happen.

If unsure which channel a thing belongs to, ask what a user would have to have done for it to be honest. If they would have had to travel, it is motion. If they would have had to arrive, it is scale. If they would have had to do nothing at all, it is neither, and the change says itself in words where it stands.

The laws

Law 1 · C9 · C10 · C15

Neither channel may stand in for the other. Motion is spent only when the focus changed, and scale changes only because the focus changed. A reading that arrives, a count that ticks, a treatment step you check off and a node that finishes fetching move nothing and resize nothing — they say themselves in place.

Sample the camera transform and the computed inline-size of every .rz-n, then change a node's data-recap, its health and its count, and check off a course step. Assert both sets are byte-identical afterwards, and that no element gained or lost data-rank. Then run a hop and assert both sets changed. Finally activate every stake on the focused card: the camera transform is unchanged after each.

Law 2 · C10 · C12 · C16

Where the camera stopped and what wears the focus size are the same card, always. Exactly one card is at focus size when a crossing lands, and it is the one the camera travelled to — never none, never two, and never a card the route did not end on.

After each of ten hops, assert exactly one element carries data-focus="true", that its id equals RZ.focus and the last entry of the route just travelled, and that its computed inline-size resolves to --rz-card-focus while no other card's does. Do it again after a hop that passes through two nodes: the passed-through cards are lit and neither is at focus size.

Law 3 · C4 · C10 · C12

Size changes only as the handover a hop causes, on the navigation timing, and never as emphasis. Nothing grows to be noticed, nothing pulses to be found, and no type size is ever transitioned — a rung is a fact about a card's role, not an animation.

Walk every rule in system.css and assert no transition-property anywhere includes font-size, and that every rule transitioning inline-size on .rz-n or .rz-n__grad uses var(--rz-dur-nav). At runtime, hover ten cards, let a slow node resolve, and idle sixty seconds: no card's computed inline-size changes in any of the three.

Law 4 · C12 · C15 · C16

Zoom is not rank. The camera scales every card by the same factor and promotes none of them: after any amount of zooming, panning, fitting or recentring, every card holds the rank it held before, and the focus is where it was. Looking closer at a neighbour is not arriving at it.

Record every card's data-rank and RZ.focus, then zoom to zoom.max, back to zoom.min, pan, fit and recentre. Assert both records are unchanged and that --rz-cam-k moved. Assert the clamp holds: no sequence of zoom steps drives RZ.cam.k outside [0.28, 1.9], so a fringe card can never be zoomed to focus size.

Law 5 · C12 · C17

When the focused card and the zoom disagree about the room available, the zoom gives way. The card you are reading is shown top edge to bottom edge at any camera scale; it is never cropped, never given a scrollbar of its own to hide behind, and never shrunk below its rank to fit.

Focus a long card in a short world and call RZ.fitFocus(). Assert the card's full height times RZ.cam.k is within the world's height, that its computed inline-size still resolves to --rz-card-focus, and that the only element in the document with scrollHeight > clientHeight is that card's own body.

Specimens

The handover, and the three things that must not cause one

A small garden with a real graph in it. Travel by clicking a card and watch the two channels hand over: the camera crosses the vein, and the card it stops at takes the focus size while the one you left drops to the size its new role earns. Then use the camera cluster — zoom in hard, pan the dark, recentre — and watch nothing at all change rank. The sizes on screen all grow together, which is exactly why zoom cannot say what rank says.

The page declares an edge list, a starting focus and the actions the Office Fig offers. It declares no size, no duration and no rank — every one of those is computed, and there is nowhere on a page to write them:

RZ.mount({
  edges: [
    ["n-garden", "n-study"],
    ["n-garden", "n-ficus"],
    ["n-study", "n-office-fig"],
    ["n-ficus", "n-office-fig"],
    ["n-office-fig", "n-leaf-spot"],
    ["n-office-fig", "n-journal"],
    ["n-journal", "n-ficus"]
  ],
  actions: { "n-office-fig": ["Log a watering", "Mark the leaf spot treated"] },
  focus: "n-garden"
});

Run Law 4's check against that garden in a console, while you zoom:

var before = Object.keys(RZ.nodes).map(function (id) {
  return id + ":" + RZ.nodes[id].el.dataset.rank;
}).join(" ");

RZ.zoomBy(RZ.PARAMS.zoom.step);   // and again, and again
RZ.recentre();

Object.keys(RZ.nodes).map(function (id) {
  return id + ":" + RZ.nodes[id].el.dataset.rank;
}).join(" ") === before;          // true — the camera promoted nobody
RZ.cam.k >= RZ.PARAMS.zoom.min && RZ.cam.k <= RZ.PARAMS.zoom.max;   // true

The blur test, walkable

Both channels are checked the same way: take the meaning out of the words and see what survives. Blur the garden above until no name is legible and you should still be able to point at the card you are reading, tell a neighbour from something two hops out, and see nothing moving that you did not set off. Press the buttons below to hold the garden still while you look, and to see what the crossing leaves behind for a reader who cannot be moved across a screen.

The last two buttons are here to be uneventful. Slowness and being offline are conditions the garden is in, not places you went and not roles anything took on — so they change what a card says and never how big it is or where the camera points. They are settled on degradation; what they prove here is that the two channels stayed out of it.

The two channels, and what a theme fills

Neither channel is this page's to define — the sizes belong to node and typography, the timings to motion. What follows is the join, so you can see at a glance which fact each token is carrying and which of them a theme may re-key.

Scale — what am I looking at
TokenCarriesJudgement
--rz-card-focus The one card you are reading Exactly one card wears it at rest (Law 2). A theme may set the width; it may not give it to a second card, and it may not withhold it from the card the camera stopped at.
--rz-card-near, --rz-card-far, --rz-card-fringe Graph distance from you, one step each Three widths for three distances, and they must stay visibly three under blur. A theme may compress the ladder; a theme that compresses it until two of them read the same has broken the test, not tightened the design.
--rz-cap-near, --rz-cap-far, --rz-cap-fringe How much a card at that distance may say Height is the second half of the same fact. Capping is what keeps a far card from out-shouting a near one by being longer.
--rz-grad-focus … --rz-grad-fringe Rank where no text is legible The signature channel: length is the rank, colour is the kind. This is what makes the blur test survivable at fringe size, so a theme may restyle it but may not remove it.
--rz-cam-k The live camera scale, published by the script Read it, never set it. It is the bridge that lets a card compensate for zoom in pure CSS, which is how Law 5 stays true at every camera scale.
Motion — how did I get here
TokenCarriesJudgement
--rz-dur-nav Distance crossed The one timing a hop may spend, pinned to the parameter nav_ms. Not a theme's to change, and not a component's to choose — see motion.
--rz-ease-travel Arriving rather than stopping The camera's curve, and the curve the rank handover rides, so the two read as one event and not as a move followed by a resize.
--rz-ease-rest A state settling, with nobody having moved Everything that is not a hop. If you are reaching for it on the plane's transform, or for the travel curve on a hover, the channels have crossed.

This page added no token and no class, so there is no amendment to record. If you find the join genuinely needs one — a channel that carries rank where the graduation cannot — take it through extending this system rather than opening a size or a duration on your page.

Accessibility

Neither channel may be the only carrier of a fact. Scale is invisible to a screen reader and motion is invisible to it twice over, so every hierarchy the widths draw is also stated in words: the focused card carries aria-current, every card's label names its kind, its recap and how many veins lead out of it, and the crossing itself is announced. Turn all motion off and shrink every card to the same size, and nothing that mattered has been deleted — that is the test.

Under reduced motion the handover still happens. The crossing collapses to a single step and the trail along the real veins is drawn and held instead; the card the camera arrived at still takes the focus size, and the you-are-here still moves. Scale is doing the work motion cannot do here, which is the whole reason the two are separate channels — the reduced-motion path is settled on motion and accessibility.

Zoom is a camera control and must never move keyboard focus. Zooming, panning, fitting and recentring leave your focus exactly where it was, so a keyboard reader who looked around is not silently relocated. A card only takes focus when a hop lands on it, and it takes it without scrolling the document.

Text scaling is a user's own zoom, and the ladder must survive it. Every rung and every card width is expressed in relative units, so a reader who scales their text up scales the whole hierarchy with it. Never pin a size in a page to make a card fit — Law 5 says the zoom gives way, not the card, and that includes giving way to somebody's own text settings.

Nothing animates without your asking. The camera never moves on a timer, on an incoming record, or on a status change, and no card ever changes size on one. The single thing that moves on its own is the drifting field behind the world, which is decoration, carries nothing, and stops entirely under reduced motion.

Do and don't

Do

Let a new reading arrive where the card already stands, in the card's own words, at the size it already had. The garden's shape did not change, so nothing about the view should say it did.

Don’t

Stage “needs water” as an arrival, with a crossing and a card that grows. The user reads a journey they never took and then goes hunting for a plant they think they moved to (C9).

Do

Give one view one subject: the card the camera stopped at, at focus size, with its neighbours below it at the size their distance earns.

Don’t

Put two cards at focus size because both matter. A view with two subjects cannot tell the user which one the camera actually brought them to, and the blur test fails before a word is read (C12, C16).

Do

Let zoom make everything bigger together, and leave rank alone. Looking closely at a neighbour is looking; going there is a hop, and only the hop changes what you are reading.

Don’t

Promote a card because the camera happens to be near it, or grow one on hover to advertise that it is clickable. Both spend the scale channel on something that did not happen (C12, C15).

Do

Believe the camera when the two channels disagree in review. Motion records what the user did; scale is something you chose, so scale is the one to fix.

Don’t

Shorten a crossing because it feels long on the fiftieth viewing, or crop the focused card to save a hop's worth of zoom. The first turns distance into a cut; the second hides the thing the user came to read (C10, C17).
