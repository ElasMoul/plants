Guidelines

Accessibility

Accessibility here is behaviour, not markup hygiene: this page settles how a keyboard walks your garden by its veins rather than by document order, and what the world says out loud each time you arrive somewhere.

What this is

Your garden is a world, and the whole point of a world is that you can tell where you are in it and what lies next to you. A pointer gets that for free: the veins are drawn, the neighbouring cards are on screen, and Office Fig is plainly two steps from the root-rot treatment course. None of it arrives on its own for someone travelling by keyboard or listening to a screen reader — and if it is rebuilt out of tab stops and headings, what they get is a list of cards in the order somebody happened to write them, which is not the shape of your garden at all.

So the same graph that draws the veins drives the keyboard. Press the right arrow and you are offered the neighbour that really lies to the right, because the system asked the edge list who the neighbours are and then asked where they sit. Press Enter and you travel that vein. Arrive, and the world tells you where you came from, what you crossed without opening, and how many veins lead out of where you now stand. Where the geography is the interface, the geography is what has to be spoken.

Traversal's timing, its interruption policy and the trail it draws belong to traversal and motion; the pointer's side of the same story belongs to interaction and cursors. This page owns the keyboard, the announcement and the accessible label — the parts of the world that only exist when nobody is looking at it.

The laws

Law 1 · C18

The keyboard follows the graph, not the document. An arrow key proposes the neighbour that really lies that way, chosen from where the neighbours actually sit — never the next element in source order.

With the focus on any node, call RZ.neighbourInDirection("ArrowRight") and assert the id it returns is in RZ.adj[RZ.focus] and has the greatest positive horizontal offset from the focus. Then reverse the order of the .rz-n elements in the plane and call it again: the answer is unchanged.

Law 2 · C18

A direction with no vein is spoken, not swallowed. Silence is indistinguishable from a broken key.

Focus a node with no neighbour above it and press ArrowUp: #rz-live gains text naming that node and the direction that has nothing in it, and the camera transform is unchanged.

Law 3 · C18

Every focus change is announced through one polite live region, and the announcement names four things: where you came from, where you are, what you crossed without opening it, and how many veins lead out from here.

Hop across two edges and read #rz-live: it contains the origin's name, the destination's name, the name of each node the route passed through with a statement that it did not open, and a count equal to RZ.adj[RZ.focus].length.

Law 4 · C5, C18

The world view's accessible label is rewritten on every hop, in lockstep with the visible you-are-here, and says the same thing in words — the node, its kind and the path travelled. Never a cell, never a coordinate.

After each of ten hops, compare the aria-label of #rz-map with the text of #rz-here: both name the current node, and both changed on the same hop. Assert the label matches no digit-comma-digit pattern.

Law 5 · C13

Under reduced motion nothing is replaced by an abrupt swap: the trail is still drawn along the real veins and held, the you-are-here still moves, and the announcement is held longer so it can be read at rest.

Set data-motion="reduced" and hop: #rz-trail carries data-on="true" along the route's own polyline, the world-view marker and its label have both moved, and #rz-live holds its text longer than it does with motion on.

Law 6 · C18

Direction is written in logical properties throughout, so a world read right-to-left points the way its reader expects and nothing has to be re-authored.

Search assets/system.css for left:, right:, margin-left, padding-right, border-left and text-align: left: there are none. Then set dir="rtl" on the document and assert every chrome member swaps to the edge its logical property names, and the caret on .rz-chip still leads away from the label.

Walk it with the keyboard

Four plants, the same four as everywhere else in this system, at the same cells. Click the dark once — or tab to it — and then put your mouse away. Arrow keys propose a neighbour and say what it is; Enter travels the vein; Tab cycles the neighbours in graph order, counting them out; Escape comes back the way you came, along a real edge rather than a history stack. Everything the world says appears in the live region below the specimen, which is heard and not seen in the product and is shown here on purpose, so you can read what a screen reader would be told.

That line above is the live region itself, shown here rather than hidden so you can read what is said. In the product it also wears rz-sr: heard, never seen.

RZ.mount({
  world: "#rz-world",
  plane: "#rz-plane",
  focus: "n-office",
  path: ["n-garden", "n-office"],
  edges: [
    ["n-garden", "n-office"],
    ["n-garden", "n-studio"],
    ["n-office", "n-problems"],
    ["n-studio", "n-problems"]
  ]
});

/* what an arrow key asks, and what an empty direction is answered with */
RZ.neighbourInDirection("ArrowRight");   /* an id from RZ.adj[RZ.focus], or null */
RZ.say("No vein that way from Office Fig.");

The key map

Six behaviours, and no seventh. Each is a promise about the graph, not about the page: a key that moved you through the document would be a seventh thing to learn and a different world from the drawn one.

Keyboard traversal
KeyWhat it doesWhat is said
Arrow keys Propose the nearest real neighbour that lies that way, computed from the neighbours' own positions. That neighbour's name and its recap, then that Enter will travel there.
Enter · Space Travel the proposed vein, through the same one code path a card press uses. Origin, destination, anything crossed and not opened, and the new degree.
Tab Cycle the focused node's neighbours in graph order. “Vein N of M”, then that neighbour's name.
Escape Return along the path you travelled — a real edge, never a history stack. The same arrival announcement as any other hop.
Escape in arrange mode Leave the mode. It is the only key the mode answers, so a keyboard user is never shut inside it. That positions were kept, and where you still stand.
A direction with no vein Nothing moves and the camera is untouched. That there is no vein that way, naming the node you are on.

What a theme fills, and what it may not

Almost nothing on this page is a theme's to choose. The focus ring is the exception, and it is a colour and a weight — not a decision about whether the ring exists.

Tokens and hooks
Token or hookMeans
--rz-focus The focus ring's colour. A theme keys it to its own palette and may not set it to the surface it sits on.
--rz-focus-width The ring's weight. It is drawn as an outline, so it never changes a card's size and nothing on the plane moves when focus lands.
--rz-focus-offset Its stand-off from the edge, so a ring on a card reads clear of the card's own membrane.
.rz-sr Heard, not seen. What the live region and any keyboard-only wording wears in the product.
.rz-live on #rz-live The single polite live region. One per surface: every announcement in the system goes through RZ.say and lands here, so two of them can never talk over each other.
aria-current on a node Set by the system on the focused card and cleared from the rest, so “where am I” is answerable without sight of the camera.
aria-label on #rz-map The world view in words, rewritten every hop. A theme writes the wording; it may not leave it standing still.

Do and don't

Do

Ask the edge list which neighbours exist, and their positions which one lies that way. The keyboard then walks the same world the eye does, and a card moved in arrange mode is reachable by the arrow that now points at it.

Don't

Hand the keyboard to tab order and call it done. Source order is the order somebody typed the cards in; it is not a direction, and following it makes the spoken world a different shape from the drawn one (C18).

Do

Say the whole arrival: “Travelled My garden to Problems, past Office Fig, which stayed shut. Three veins lead out from here.” Where you came from and what you crossed are the two things a listener cannot recover from the screen.

Don't

Announce the destination's name alone. It is the one fact the new card already carries, and it leaves distance, direction and degree unsaid — which is the whole of the geography (C18).
