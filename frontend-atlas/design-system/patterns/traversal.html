Patterns

Traversal

Traversal is how you get from one part of your garden to another: the camera picks up and carries you along a vein you could already see, at one timing, on one code path, and the place you left is still standing behind you. This page settles that timing, that path, and what happens when you ask for a second hop before the first has landed.

What this is

You are looking at your Office Fig and you want the treatment course it is on. In PlantPal that is not a link to a treatment screen. The vein from the fig to its problem, and from that problem to the course, is already drawn on the plane in front of you; when you ask to go there, the camera runs along those veins, lights each thing it passes without opening it, and comes to rest with the course open. You saw the distance you crossed, so you know how to come back.

Every trigger in the product runs that same move: a card you click, a link inside a card's prose, an entry in Navigate to, a step in you-are-here, an arrow key, a search result, a notification. There is no faster kind of hop for a nearby thing and no different kind for a far one — one path, one timing, and a component that picks its own duration is non-conformant, not a variation.

This page owns the journey: the timing, the arc-length path, the multi-hop rule, the interruption policy and reduced motion. The plane it happens on is world, the cards are node, the veins and their single source of truth are edge, and the panel that lists where you can go is navigate to. Landing somewhere you did not walk to is arrivals. The prohibition against a per-component duration is restated nowhere else: it is stated here and carried by motion.

The laws

Law 1 · C10

One timing, one code path, every hop. parameters.nav_ms is 300ms and every navigation on the screen — from any trigger, over any distance — goes through RZ.travel. “Every focus change travels — nothing fades, pops, or teleports.”

Wrap RZ.travel and fire every navigation affordance on this page — a card, an in-card chip, a Navigate-to entry, a you-are-here step, Enter after an arrow key. Every call receives RZ.PARAMS.nav_ms and no other duration appears. Then sample the camera transform each frame during one hop: more than two distinct positions are sampled, and the first equals the pre-hop camera.

Law 2 · C11

The camera centre is driven along the polyline of the route's real veins, by arc length — never straight across the space. “Every transition carries direction, scale, and ownership — and the camera follows the edge's own path.”

Sample the camera centre each frame during a multi-hop and assert every sample lies on RZ.chainD(chain)'s polyline within one pixel. Drag a card in arrange mode so the vein bends, then hop again: the samples follow the new bend, because the path is read from the veins and not from a remembered curve.

Law 3 · C1, C4

Every hop follows a vein that was visible before it was asked for, and arrives by rescale. “Nothing is replaced, unmounted, or routed to — every navigation is a rescale.” If RZ.route finds no way through, nothing happens: the interface never invents a path.

For every control that calls RZ.go(target), assert each consecutive pair of RZ.route(RZ.focus, target) is in RZ.EDGES and has a drawn .rz-vein in the DOM at first paint. Record the .rz-n element set before and after ten hops: identical objects, nothing added or removed, and location.href unchanged.

Law 4 · C2

A multi-hop crossing lights each node as the camera reaches it and opens only the destination — and the place you came from stays on screen: “After any hop, the origin is still on screen or exactly one visible hop back — with no reliance on a history stack or browser back.”

Hop across three veins and watch: each intermediate card takes data-passing="true" in turn and none of them takes data-focus. Afterwards, assert the previous focus still has a rendered element carrying a rank attribute and appears in Navigate to or is joined to the new focus by one drawn vein, and that history.length is unchanged.

Law 5 · C16

A second hop asked for while one is in flight is refused out loud — never queued, never blended. The hop in flight always completes, so you never land somewhere you did not ask for. “There is exactly one focal point, it owns the camera, and the camera never moves without a user-attributable reason.”

Call RZ.go() twice inside one frame: the second returns immediately while RZ.travelling is true, the live region reads “Still travelling…”, and after nav_ms the focus is the first target and not the second. Assert exactly one element carries data-focus="true" throughout.

Law 6 · C13

Under reduced motion the trail is still drawn along the real veins and held, and the you-are-here has already moved: “Under prefers-reduced-motion, direction is still shown and the you-are-here still updated — with no transition replaced by an abrupt swap.” Direction and distance survive; only the sweep is spent.

Set data-motion="reduced" on the root and hop: #rz-trail carries data-on="true" with a d equal to RZ.chainD(chain), and both the world-view marker and its accessible label have moved. Assert the announcement is held longer, not skipped.

Travel it

Four veins, five cards, and you start at your garden. Click Treatment course — the far card on the right — and watch the camera take all four veins in turn rather than cutting across: Office Fig, Problems and Underwatering each light as it is reached, none of them opens, and only the course does. Then click a near card, and use Enter after an arrow key. All of it is the same 300ms.

Try interrupting: click one card and immediately click another. The second is refused and says so, and the first hop lands.

Nothing on this page re-implements the journey. The specimen is mounted from the runtime, the route is resolved from the same edge list the veins are drawn from, and the timing comes from the declared parameter rather than a number written here.

RZ.mount({
  world: "#rz-world",
  plane: "#rz-plane",
  focus: "n-garden",
  path: ["n-garden"],
  edges: [
    ["n-garden", "n-office"],
    ["n-office", "n-problems"],
    ["n-problems", "n-underwater"],
    ["n-underwater", "n-treatment"]
  ]
});

// one hop, from anywhere, by any trigger:
RZ.go("n-treatment");

// what it resolves to, before it moves:
RZ.route("n-garden", "n-treatment");
// -> ["n-garden", "n-office", "n-problems", "n-underwater", "n-treatment"]

Behaviour

One hop, in order

Ask for a destination and the runtime does five things, always these and always in this order: it refuses if a hop is already in flight; it resolves the route over the adjacency; it hands the focus to the destination and recomputes every card's rank from the new position; it drives the camera along the route's polyline for nav_ms, lighting each node as it arrives at it; and it rewrites you-are-here, Navigate to and the world-view marker together, then says out loud where you are.

Zoom, fit, recentre, pan Camera cluster — a third category RZ.zoomBy, RZ.fitAll, RZ.recentre. These change the view and never the focus. Looking around is not navigating (C15).

Accessibility

A journey has to be walkable without being seen, and the keyboard follows the graph rather than the document. The world frame is focusable; each arrow key proposes the neighbour that actually lies that way, computed from real positions, and announces it with its recap. Enter or Space travels there on exactly the path a click would take. Tab walks the veins out of the focus in turn, saying which of how many. Escape steps back one hop along the path you actually took. A direction with no vein is spoken by name — “no vein that way from Office Fig” — never silently ignored, because silence reads as a broken key.

Every arrival is announced once, in one sentence, through the polite live region: where you came from, what you crossed without opening, and how many veins lead out from where you now are. The world view's accessible label is rewritten on the same hop, in lockstep with the visible you-are-here, so a screen-reader user and a sighted user are never told two different stories. Neither ever speaks a cell, a row or a column: they speak your garden.

A refused hop is announced too. Silence after a click reads as a dead control, so the interruption of Law 5 says “still travelling” rather than doing nothing, and the hop in flight lands where you asked.

Under reduced motion the sweep is spent but the journey is not: the trail is drawn along the real veins and held, the marker and its label have already moved, and the announcement is held longer rather than dropped. Direction is never carried by motion alone, so nothing is lost when the motion goes. Logical properties are used throughout, so nothing points the wrong way in RTL — including which neighbour ArrowLeft finds.

Usage guidance

Almost every mistake here is the same mistake: treating one hop as special. A hop that is short, or frequent, or “only a panel” is still a hop, and the moment one of them picks its own duration the reader stops being able to feel distance at all.

Do

Send every trigger through RZ.go and let it take nav_ms, whether the destination is one vein away or four. One timing is what makes the sweep legible as distance (Law 1, C10).

Don’t

Give a neighbouring card a quicker hop because it is close, or a far one a slower one because it is far. A scale of durations reads as permission to choose per component, and then no duration means anything (C10).

Do

Light each node the crossing passes as the camera reaches it, and open only the one you asked for. That is what tells you the route was real and which way it went (Law 4, C2).

Don’t

Cut the camera straight to a far card because the route is long. A straight line across empty space says no vein was taken, and the reader loses both the direction and the way back (C11).

Do

Refuse a second hop while one is in flight, out loud, and let the first land. One camera, one owner, one movement (Law 5, C16).

Don’t

Queue the second hop or blend the two camera paths. Queuing lands the user somewhere they have stopped asking for; blending puts the camera on a curve that matches no vein on the plane (C11, C16).

Two more, stated as rules. Never travel on the system's own initiative — a reminder firing or a health score landing must not move the camera; if something arrived, that is an arrival and the user still chooses to cross. And never offer a destination the plane did not draw: if there is no vein, there is no hop, and inventing one would make a place the reader has no way back from.
