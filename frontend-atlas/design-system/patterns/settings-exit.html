Settings exit

Settings is the one place in PlantPal that is not on the plane. This page settles how you get there without losing the garden: the camera pulls all the way out, your whole application shrinks into a floating card, and settings stands beside it. No dialog, no blur, nothing closed.

What this is

You are somewhere in your garden — say on Office Fig, two hops from the collection — and you want to change the palette. In most apps that means a dialog dropped over the top of what you were doing, and the moment it closes you have to find your way back by memory. Here it means the camera keeps pulling out past the last zoom stop until the entire application is a card floating in front of you, still laid out exactly as you left it, with Office Fig still focused inside it. The settings panel sits next to that card, on the same plane, at the same depth. You click the card to dive back in.

That is the whole idea, and it costs nothing to explain because you watched it happen: you can see where you were the entire time you are away. Settings is content — a place, with its own material — and the small bar beside it that says Move, Select, Zoom and Recentre is apparatus, in the chrome register. Nothing about your garden is hidden while you are out here, and nothing about it moves.

This page owns the exit choreography and the app card. The material the card borrows is owned by node and elevation; which layers ride the camera and which belong to the viewport is owned by world and elevation; the live palette switch you use once you are out here is owned by colors. This page cites them; it does not restate them.

The laws

Law 1 · C20

Leaving for settings is a camera move, not a layer: “Leaving to settings runs the exit choreography — camera out, app shrinks to a floating card, panel beside it — rather than a centred modal over a blur.” The shell transform is scale(0.42) translateX(-25%), used verbatim.

Call RZ.setMode("overview") and read the computed transform of .rz-shell: it is that matrix and no other. Then assert document.querySelectorAll('[role="dialog"]').length is 0, that no element in the document has a computed backdrop-filter other than none, and that no ancestor of the app card carries a filter.

Law 2 · C20

The app card takes the card's own material — its corner, its halo, its membrane hairline — because it is a card now, and the reader should be able to tell that without being told.

In overview, read the computed border-radius, box-shadow and border-color of .rz-shell and compare them with the same three on any .rz-n: they resolve from --rz-corner-card, --rz-halo-lit and --rz-membrane-lit. Then blur the frame: the app card and the settings panel are two bright soft rectangles, and the bar beside them is not one.

Law 3 · C7, C9, C16

Going out and coming back changes nothing about the garden. The same node is focused, every card is at the same place, the camera is where you left it, and no record was touched — the exit is a scale applied to the whole shell, never a re-layout of what is inside it.

Record RZ.focus, the camera transform, and the computed left/top of every .rz-n. Run RZ.setMode("overview") then RZ.setMode("app") and diff all three: identical. Assert the node element set is the identical objects — nothing was unmounted — and that history.length is unchanged.

Law 4 · C20

The app's own background field goes with the app. In overview it covers the card and nothing beyond it: the decoration belongs to the surface it decorates, and the surface is now a card. The space around the card is bare ground.

In overview, assert the mote canvas the runtime is painting is #rz-motes-app (inside the shell) and that its bounding box is contained by the app card's bounding box to the pixel. Assert the viewport field #rz-motes is painting nothing. Return to app mode and assert the roles swap back.

Law 5 · C14, C17

Settings is content and takes the node register; the bar beside it is chrome and takes the chrome register. The panel may hold real controls in the two forms; the bar holds camera controls and marks, never a stake and never a chip.

Assert .rz-settings resolves a non-none box-shadow and a card corner, while every .rz-chrome member resolves box-shadow: none. Assert .rz-overview-bar .rz-stake, .rz-overview-bar .rz-chip matches nothing. Assert only the settings panel's own body scrolls and document.body has no scrollbar.

Law 6 · C20, C15

The way back is the card itself. There is no close button, no dismiss, no escape hatch that means something different from clicking the thing you came out of — and the exit moves the camera without changing the focus, so it is a camera action, not a hop.

Click the app card and assert the mode returned to app and RZ.focus never changed. Assert no element inside the settings panel or the overview bar calls RZ.go. Assert the exit control is a .rz-cam-btn — the camera category — and not a stake or a chip.

The exit, running

Press Settings in the top bar. Watch what happens to Office Fig: it stays focused, stays in place, and simply gets smaller along with everything around it. Click the card to come back. The mode buttons below the specimen drive exactly the same call the top bar does — RZ.setMode — so there is one code path in and one out.

Two ways in and out, both the same call: pulls out, dives back in. In the product there is no second button: the card is the way back. Both are camera controls, in the camera category — they change no data and no focus.

<div class="rz-shell" data-rz-mode="app">
  <div class="rz-world">…the whole application, unchanged…</div>
</div>
<div class="rz-settings">…</div>
<div class="rz-chrome rz-chrome--camera rz-overview-bar">…</div>

RZ.setMode("overview");   // camera out — the shell becomes a card
RZ.setMode("app");        // dive back in

The specimen runs from the runtime. Nothing on this page re-implements the transform, the field or the palette switch, and the exit control in the top bar is wired by RZ.mount from its data-rz-mode attribute alone. The overview bar borrows the camera cluster's edge, because in overview that edge is where the Move / Select / Zoom / Recentre controls live; no new class was needed and no amendment was made for this page.

What the exit is made of

Four parts, and the column that decides everything is the last one: a part is either content — and takes the node's material — or apparatus, and takes chrome's.

The parts and their register (Law 5)
PartWhat it isRegister
.rz-shell The whole application, scaled down in place. It keeps its own chrome inside it, now absolutely positioned within the card rather than fixed to the viewport. Content, in overview. It takes the card's corner, halo and hairline, and the zoom-in cursor that says it can be dived into.
.rz-settings The panel beside the card, on the same plane at the same depth. Real controls in the two forms; its own body is the only thing here that scrolls. Content. Sheet fill, card corner, the same halo as the app card — a place, not a dialog.
.rz-overview-bar Move · Select · Zoom · Recentre. What your pointer does out here, stated rather than guessed at. Chrome. Monospace, uppercase, one rung, no shadow at all.
.rz-shell__caption “PlantPal Application · Click to return to the app.” It names the card and says what clicking it does. Chrome. It is apparatus describing a piece of content.
#rz-motes-app The application's own field, now covering the card and nothing beyond it (Law 4). It belongs to whichever surface it decorates — here, the card.
What a theme may and may not fill
ValueMeans
--rz-param-shell-transform scale(0.42) translateX(-25%). Hand-tuned, held in constitution.json and checked by RZ.verifyParameters(). It is the one literal in the system a page may write verbatim, and it is not to be re-derived, rounded, or replaced by a token of your own.
--rz-corner-card The card's corner. The app card takes it, so the shrunken application reads as a card and not a screenshot.
--rz-halo-lit The card's own halo, on both the app card and the settings panel. Chrome takes --rz-chrome-lift, which is none, always.
--rz-membrane-lit The hairline around the app card — the same membrane a focused card carries.
--rz-dur-3 · --rz-ease-travel The pull-out and the dive-in run on the system's travel easing. The timing rung is owned by motion; this page only asks that both directions use the same one.
--rz-sheet The settings panel's fill: content material, so the panel cannot be mistaken for the bar beside it.

Behaviour

Going out

One call, RZ.setMode("overview"), from the top bar's exit control. The shell scales and slides on the travel easing; nothing inside it re-lays out, because the whole thing is one transform. The app's field is re-sized onto the card and the viewport's field stops. Focus is untouched — you are still on the plant you were reading.

Coming back

Click the card. It carries the zoom-in cursor for exactly that reason, and it is the only way back that the product ships: a close button would be a second story about what the card is.

While you are out

The garden holds still. The pointer does what the bar says — move, select, zoom, recentre — and none of those four change data or focus. Palette changes apply as you click them, live, with no save step; the card re-keys under you, world and all, because every colour on screen is a token reference. That mechanism is owned by colors.

Travel and the exit

They do not overlap. The exit is a camera move; a hop is a change of focus along a vein. If a hop is in flight the exit waits for it rather than blending with it, and no control inside settings or the overview bar traverses the graph — a traversal out here would leave you looking at a card whose insides changed while you were not in them. Hops are owned by traversal.

Reduced motion

The scale still happens, on the same path; what goes is the drift in the field. The point of the choreography is that you can see where your garden went, and that survives a shorter transition — it would not survive a cut.

Accessibility

The exit is announced in the app's own words. Going out: “You are in overview mode. This is the entire PlantPal application. Click the app card to dive back in.” Coming back names where you are returning to, by node name, so a screen-reader user gets the same reassurance a sighted user gets from watching the card grow — that nothing was lost while they were away.

Because nothing is a dialog, nothing traps focus, and nothing needs to be dismissed. There is no role="dialog", no aria-modal, and no hidden background: the application is still in the document, still labelled, still reachable by keyboard. That is a real accessibility property of the pattern, not a side effect — a modal's focus trap is a thing users have to learn to escape, and this pattern never asks them to.

The app card is a real control and says what it does: it carries an accessible name that matches its visible caption, so “click to return to the app” is available to a reader who cannot see the caption. Its keyboard behaviour is the ordinary one — reach it by Tab, activate it with Enter or Space. The exit control in the top bar carries its own label rather than relying on its mark, since a mark alone names nothing.

The overview bar's four words are its own accessible content, not a tooltip, and it is not a live region — it does not interrupt. Colour is never the only signal that you are out here: the whole application has visibly changed size and its caption is present in text. Logical properties are used throughout, so the panel sits on the correct side in RTL, and the settings panel's own body is the only thing in the document that scrolls.

If you are unsure whether a control belongs in the panel or in the bar, ask whether a screen reader should call it a place or an instrument. Places go in the panel.

Usage guidance

The failure this pattern exists to prevent is the disappearing world: any treatment where the garden stops being visible and the user has to rebuild their sense of place afterwards. Blur is that failure wearing a nice coat — it says “this is still here” while making it unreadable.

Do

Shrink the whole application into a card, laid out identically, and put settings beside it at the same depth. The user can see where they were the entire time, and the way back is the thing they came out of (C20, Law 1).

Don’t

Centre a settings dialog over the world and blur what is behind it. It breaks the geography and the you-are-here at once, traps focus, and needs a dismiss control that means nothing in a world where nothing was ever opened (C20).

Do

Keep the app card inert while you are out. It is a picture of where you were, and clicking anywhere on it means one thing: dive back in, exactly where you left off (Law 3, Law 6).

Don’t

Let a card inside the shrunken app stay clickable as a hop. Then the user leaves overview and arrives somewhere they did not choose, and two different things happened for one click (C15, C16).

Do

Use this pattern for the small number of surfaces that genuinely are not in the garden: settings, your account, sign-out. They have no cell, no veins and no neighbours, and pretending otherwise would cost the plane its meaning.

Don’t

Reach for the exit to show something that does live in the garden — a plant's history, a treatment course, the full list behind “+N more”. Those are nodes; travel to them. The exit is not a general-purpose way to show a panel.

One more, about the number. The transform is hand-tuned to leave the app card readable and the panel unobstructed at the same time; if a theme finds it wrong, that is an owner ruling, not a token edit. Change the palette, the type voice and the density around it — see extending this system for the amendment path — and leave the choreography alone.

Where else this appears

The application that shrinks is world; the material the app card borrows is node and the shadow rule it obeys is elevation, which also owns the layer-ownership rule Law 4 depends on. The register the bar and caption take is chrome. The live palette switch you use out here is colors; the timing is motion and motion and scale. The two forms inside the panel are interaction and cursors; the announcements are accessibility. The other two things that are modes rather than layers are arrange mode and, for the states you can walk from the probe, degradation. Hops are traversal and arrivals.
