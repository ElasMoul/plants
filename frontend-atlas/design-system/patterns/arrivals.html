Patterns

Arrivals

An arrival is how you reach something you did not walk to: you searched for it, or it started needing you. This page settles what an arrival is allowed to be — an offer that states the distance first and then crosses it on the veins, never a jump that drops you somewhere with no account of how you got there.

What this is

You are standing at your garden and you type “fig”. Your Office Fig is four veins away, through the fig itself, its problems and the underwatering it is on. The search hit says so before you touch it, and taking it runs exactly the crossing a click on the card would have run: the camera picks you up at your garden, takes those four veins in turn, lights what it passes without opening it, and comes to rest with the fig open. The same is true of a reminder: the fig needing water today does not move you, it offers you a trip and tells you how far it is.

That is the whole pattern. An arrival chooses the destination; it never gets a different way of getting there. It is the one place in PlantPal where you may be offered something that is not next door, and it pays for that by writing the distance out in front of you.

This page owns the arrival: what it may offer, what it must state first, and the rule that nothing arrives by moving you. The crossing itself — the timing, the arc-length path, the multi-hop rule, the interruption policy and reduced motion — is owned by traversal and is cited here, never restated. An arrival into something not yet fetched is degradation; the very first arrival a new gardener ever sees is day zero.

The laws

Law 1 · C21

An arrival states the distance before it is taken and then crosses it on the one code path. “Search and notification arrivals show the distance being crossed rather than cutting to the destination.” The hop count is on the offer, in words, before anything is activated.

Instrument RZ.travel. Take a search arrival and a reminder arrival: both call it with the same chain RZ.route(RZ.focus, target) returns for a card click, and both receive RZ.PARAMS.nav_ms — one code path, no second duration. Then read each offer with the world hidden: the number of veins it costs is legible from the offer alone.

Law 2 · C1, C2

An arrival may only offer somewhere the plane already drew. “Every navigation follows an edge that was visible before the click.” If RZ.route finds no way through, the arrival is not offered — a result the world cannot reach is a defect in the graph, not a licence to invent a hop.

For every arrival offer on the page, assert RZ.route(RZ.focus, id).length >= 2 and that each consecutive pair is in RZ.EDGES with a drawn .rz-vein in the DOM at first paint. After landing, assert the node you came from still has a rendered element carrying a rank attribute, and that history.length is unchanged.

Law 3 · C15, C16

Nothing arrives by moving you. A reminder firing, a health score landing or an identification finishing may offer a trip; only your own activation takes it. “There is exactly one focal point, it owns the camera, and the camera never moves without a user-attributable reason.”

Raise every arrival this page can raise and then idle 60s: the camera transform is byte-identical, exactly one element carries data-focus="true", and it is the same element as before. Diff every node's computed left/top across the same window: an arrival is not a structural change, so nothing moved.

Law 4 · C14

An arrival wears the traversal form and no other. “A user can tell navigation from mutation before activating it, from form alone.” Inside a card an offer is an outlined chip with a caret; inside chrome it is the leading arrow mark. An arrival is never a filled stake, because taking one changes nothing in your garden.

Mask the text on every arrival offer and screenshot it: each is a chip or a .rz-chrome__item[data-means="travel"], and none is a .rz-stake. Activate every offer and assert no record field changed — only the focus and the camera did.

Law 5 · C13, C18

An arrival is heard as well as seen, and survives reduced motion. The distance is spoken before the crossing and the landing is spoken after it, and “under prefers-reduced-motion, direction is still shown and the you-are-here still updated — with no transition replaced by an abrupt swap.”

With a screen reader, take an arrival: the polite live region reads the hop count first and then names the origin, what was crossed without opening, and the new degree. Set data-motion="reduced" and take it again: #rz-trail carries data-on="true" with a d equal to RZ.chainD(chain), and both the world-view marker and its accessible label have moved.

Arrive at something

You are at your garden. Two things have arrived: a search you ran for “fig”, and a reminder that the Office Fig wants water today. Both are offers sitting in the card you are already reading, both say how far they are, and neither of them has moved you. Take one and watch the camera take the veins in turn — the same crossing the cards themselves would have run, at the same timing.

The veins-away count on each offer is written by the runtime from the real route, not typed into this page. Drag nothing, change nothing: come back with My garden in you-are-here and take the other one.

Nothing about the crossing is written on this page. The offers are ordinary in-card hops; the route, the lighting, the timing and the announcement come from the runtime, and the veins-away figure is read back from the same route the camera will take.

RZ.mount({
  world: "#rz-world",
  plane: "#rz-plane",
  focus: "n-garden",
  path: ["n-garden"],
  edges: [
    ["n-garden", "n-office"],
    ["n-office", "n-fig"],
    ["n-fig", "n-problems"],
    ["n-problems", "n-underwater"],
    ["n-underwater", "n-treatment"]
  ]
});

// what an arrival must be able to say, BEFORE it moves anything:
RZ.route("n-garden", "n-treatment").length - 1;   // -> 5 veins away

// and how it crosses: the same call a card click makes.
RZ.go("n-treatment");

Behaviour

One arrival, in order

Four beats, always these and always this way round. It is offered — the offer appears where you are already looking, in the traversal form, with its distance written on it, and the world does not stir. You take it — nothing at all happens until you do; an offer you never read has changed nothing. The distance is spoken — the live region says how far before the camera starts, so the count is not something only a sighted reader gets. The crossing runs — and from that point on this is not an arrival any more, it is traversal, on its path and its timing, with the landing announced the way every landing is.

An arrival has no furniture of its own

There is no arrivals dock, no toast layer and no notification tray in this system, and adding one would be a new chrome member. An arrival borrows a form that already exists: inside a card it is a chip in the prose, and where it belongs to the fixed furniture it is a chrome item with the travel mark, in the panel that already owns destinations — navigate to. The register those two forms belong to is chrome. Nothing floats over the world, because a thing that floats over the world hides part of it and the geography is the one thing that never degrades.

What an arrival may and may not do

The offer's own contract
Attribute or callFormMeans
data-rz-goto="<id>" Outlined chip with a caret An offer inside a card. Calls RZ.go(id), so the crossing is the card click's crossing.
data-means="travel" Chrome item, leading arrow mark The same offer where it belongs to the fixed furniture. Chrome admits no chips and no stakes.
RZ.route(RZ.focus, id) Read, not drawn The distance the offer prints, minus one. An offer that cannot resolve a route is not shown at all.
RZ.say(text) Polite live region The distance, spoken before the crossing starts.
RZ.go(id) No form of its own The single door. An arrival never calls RZ.travel directly and never sets the focus itself.
RZ.zoomBy, RZ.recentre, RZ.fitAll Camera cluster Never touched by an arrival. Looking around is not navigating, and an arrival is not allowed to look around for you.

What a theme may set

An arrival's values
Token or parameterMeans
--rz-vital The offer's ink and rim: this will take you somewhere, and it will not change anything.
--rz-vital-quiet The offer under the pointer. A hover state, never a second form.
--rz-vital-bright The trail the crossing leaves, and the whole of what reduced motion keeps: the distance, drawn on the real veins.
--rz-mutate Deliberately absent from an arrival. It appears here only so a theme can check its offers never wear it.
--rz-param-nav-ms The crossing's one timing, read from the declared parameter. An arrival does not get its own, near or far — the prohibition is carried by motion.

Accessibility

The distance has to be heard, not only read. Before the camera moves, the polite live region says how many veins the crossing is; when it lands, the ordinary landing announcement names where you came from, what you crossed without opening, and how many veins lead out from where you now are. A sighted reader and a screen-reader reader are told the same two things in the same order, so nobody is asked to infer the distance from a sweep they cannot see.

An arrival never takes the caret. It appears in the page in reading order, in the card you are already in, and waits to be tabbed to — because a control that grabs focus has moved you without asking, which is the exact failure Law 3 exists to prevent. When you do take it, the runtime hands focus to the destination card, so you are reading where you are standing.

Nothing is timed out of reach. An offer does not expire, cannot be the only route to a place, and never has to be caught: the plant it is about is still sitting in the world, still reachable by walking the veins. Time pressure is not a way to tell somebody about their garden.

An offer is told apart from a mutation by form, not by colour — an outline and a caret against a filled stake — so it survives without colour vision and in either theme. Under reduced motion the crossing's trail is drawn along the real veins and held, and the you-are-here has already moved, so distance and direction survive when the sweep is spent. Logical properties throughout, so the caret and the arrow mark point the right way in RTL.

Usage guidance

Every mistake here is a version of the same one: letting the system decide the reader is somewhere else now. An arrival is an offer. If the user did not activate it, nothing may have happened.

Do

Print the real distance on the offer — “five veins away”, read from RZ.route — and speak it before the crossing starts. The count is what turns a result into a place you can put on your own map (Law 1, C21).

Don’t

Cut straight to the destination because it was searched for rather than walked to. A search hit that teleports leaves the reader somewhere new with no account of the distance, and no idea how to get back (C21, C11).

Do

Let a reminder sit in the card as an offer and leave the world exactly where the reader put it. The fig has needed water since this morning; it can wait for the reader's own hand (Law 3, C16).

Don’t

Move the camera, or re-rank the cards, because something arrived. A notice that repositions the world takes the reader's place away to tell them about a plant, and no non-structural change may move anything (C9, C16).

Do

Draw an offer as an outlined chip with a caret, so the reader can see it will take them somewhere and change nothing before they touch it (Law 4, C14).

Don’t

Draw it as a filled stake, or fold a mutation into it — “water it and go there”. The two meanings have two forms precisely so a reader never has to guess which one they are about to do (C14, C15).

Three more, stated as rules. Never raise an arrival about the card the reader is already looking at — a watering they just logged or a count going up updates in place, silently, where it happened; an offer to travel nowhere teaches people to ignore the offers that matter. One offer at a time per plant. Three notices about the same fig turn a plant that wants a drink into a wall of alarm, and this is an app about relief from guilt. Say so when you are unsure. If a match is a guess, write that it is a guess in plain words rather than ranking it quietly in among the certain ones.

Where else this appears

The crossing an arrival hands over to is traversal, which owns the timing, the arc-length path and the interruption policy; the prohibition on a per-component duration is carried by motion. The routes are resolved over the single edge list at edge, on the plane at world; the cards an arrival offers and lands on are node. The panel that already owns destinations is navigate to, and the register it belongs to is chrome; the filled stakes an arrival must never wear are action rail. The marker that answers where you landed is minimap. An arrival into something not yet fetched, or refused because you are offline, is degradation; the first arrival in a new garden is day zero. The announcement contract in full is accessibility, and the two forms and their pointer behaviour are interaction and cursors.
