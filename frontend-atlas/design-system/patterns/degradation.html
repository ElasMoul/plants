Patterns

Degradation

When something is slow, missing, absent or broken, it is the card that says so — never the screen. This page settles the six states your garden can be in when the world is not whole, the material each one wears, and the one thing that never degrades: the geography.

What this is

You photograph a leaf and ask PlantPal what it is. That answer takes five to fifteen seconds, honestly — it is a real request to a real identification service, and sometimes that service is having a bad afternoon. Meanwhile your care guides are cached from last week, the part of your garden you have not opened since Tuesday has not been fetched at all, one plant you archived in spring is still there, and you are on a train with no signal.

All of that is one screen at once, and none of it is an interruption. Your garden keeps its shape: every card stays in the cell it holds, the veins stay drawn and stay traversable, the world view still says where you are, and the camera stays where you left it. What changes is the ink inside the cards that are waiting, unknown, empty, stale or broken — each in its own body, in its own words.

This page owns all six states and the probe that walks them. The source it is written from is degradation-spec.json, which is the record; where this page and that file could differ, the file wins.

What this page does not own: the register that makes the probe panel furniture rather than content is chrome; the material of the cards themselves is node; the fact that “+N more” is a density decision and never a gap is aggregate; the 300ms a hop takes, including a hop into something unfetched, is traversal; and the rule that a node keeps its cell when the graph grows is spacing.

The laws

Law 1 · C22, C9

The geography never degrades. Whatever state the world is in, no card moves, no card changes rank and no card changes cell: “At >=10s the geography holds, with per-node loading in place and no repositioning as nodes resolve.” The cells come from the record list, which is small and arrives first, so every card is placed before any of its contents resolve.

Snapshot the computed left/top of every .rz-n, toggle each probe control in turn, and snapshot again. The diff is empty in every state. Assert the camera transform and the world-view extent are unchanged too.

Law 2 · C22

Slowness is per card, never global. Past parameters.slow_threshold_ms — 10 seconds — a card whose payload has not resolved shows a skeleton shaped like the content it stands in for, at that content's real row heights, and says in its own words what it is waiting for. There is no spinner, no word “loading”, and no blocking layer over the world anywhere in this system.

Set data-speed="slow" from the probe. Assert no element covers the plane with a blocking layer and that document.querySelector("[role=dialog]") is null. Grep the built artifact for “spinner”, for the standalone word “Loading” and for any infinite rotation keyframe: all absent. Assert each unresolved card's skeleton rows match the row heights of the content that replaces them.

Law 3 · C23

Unknown is not empty, and both are demonstrable side by side. A region that has not been fetched is a card in its own right, dashed, marked unknown, stating what it believes is inside as a belief and printing no count it cannot vouch for. A card that is loaded and genuinely has nothing is a designed plot with an invitation: it knows there is nothing. “On a partial graph, unloaded regions are drawn as explicitly unknown — distinguishable from "loaded and empty" — with traversal into them allowed and never dead-ending.”

In the specimen below, assert an unknown card and an empty card are both present and materially different (dashed rim in --rz-unknown against dashed rim in --rz-empty), that the unknown card carries a real fetch control, that its veins are drawn with data-unknown="true", and that RZ.go("n-region") succeeds.

Law 4 · C24, C15

Offline, the last-known world stays navigable, staleness is stated per card in that card's own words, and every mutation is visibly refused or visibly queued. A single global banner would be a lie about the parts that are genuinely current: “Offline, the last-known world is navigable, staleness is honest per node, mutations are explicitly refused or queued, and geography stays put.”

Set data-net="offline" from the probe. Every hop, every camera control and keyboard traversal still work. Assert at least two cards carry data-stale values that are not equal. Assert every .rz-stake is either refused — data-refused="true", cursor not-allowed — or names itself queued in words. Assert nothing was accepted silently and no card moved.

Law 5 · C25, C16

A failure is a state inside the card that failed, and the camera is undisturbed: “On failure the error is a state inside the node, with retry in place and the camera undisturbed.” It states the fact, the time, and what happened to the user's own data, and it offers a retry in place plus a second, different way through. One card failing while its neighbours are healthy is the expected shape. There is no error dialog in this system.

Put a card into data-state="error": the fact, the time, the fate of the user's data, the retry and the second way through are all inside that card's .rz-n__error, and the camera transform before and after is identical. Assert no other card changed and nothing was unmounted.

Law 6 · C22, C23, C24, C25

A reviewer must be able to walk every state from a probe, without editing markup. The states are document attributes — data-speed, data-net, data-graph, data-motion, data-limit — plus per-card data-state, data-pending and data-stale, all set through one entry point, RZ.setState. The probe panel is chrome: it carries marks, never a stake or a chip, and it holds no traversal.

Assert every state named in degradation-spec.json → states is reachable by activating one probe control on this page, with no markup edit and no reload. Assert the probe contains no .rz-stake, no .rz-chip, and no element that calls RZ.go.

Walk the states

One garden, six cards, five probe controls. Toggle Slow and watch nothing move: the identification card fills with a skeleton at the real height of the answer it is waiting for, and every other card carries on. Toggle Offline and read the staleness lines — they differ, because the truth differs — then try to water the fig and watch the stake refuse itself rather than fail silently. Toggle Partial and compare the two dashed cards: Balcony, which is unknown and can be travelled into, next to Journal, which is loaded and simply has nothing in it yet.

Then travel while all of it is true. Every hop still works in every state, because degradation is ink and the geography is structure.

Nothing on this page draws a state by hand. The probe buttons are wired by the runtime through the one entry point, and the skeleton is the runtime's own show-mode rather than a second implementation written here.

RZ.mount({
  world: "#rz-world",
  plane: "#rz-plane",
  focus: "n-garden",
  path: ["n-garden"],
  edges: [
    ["n-garden", "n-ident"],
    ["n-garden", "n-guides"],
    ["n-garden", "n-journal"],
    ["n-ident",  "n-region"],
    ["n-ident",  "n-scan"],
    ["n-guides", "n-journal"]
  ]
});

// one entry point, one state at a time, no markup edit:
RZ.setState("net", "offline");
RZ.setState("speed", "slow");
RZ.setState("graph", "partial");

// the skeleton is a show-mode of the card, not a widget:
RZ.present(RZ.focus, "n-ident");   // -> that card renders data-show="skel"

The six states

What each one wears

State, attribute, material
StateAttributeMaterial, and what it means
Loading data-pending="true" The recap is replaced in place by .rz-skel rows at the real row heights of the content they stand in for, and the card says what it is waiting for in its own words. Never the word “loading”, never a spinner.
Slow data-speed="slow" Past 10 seconds. Everything structural holds: cells, veins, degree counts, world view, camera. Stated per card, so you can see that your garden is still arriving while the guides are already whole.
Partial graph data-graph="partial", data-state="unknown" Dashed rim in --rz-unknown, no count, a belief stated as a belief, a real fetch control, and dashed veins that are traversable — travelling in is what fetches it.
Offline data-net="offline", data-stale The last-known world stays navigable. Each card reveals its own staleness line, and they differ. Stakes are refused with not-allowed, or queued and said so in words.
Failure data-state="error" .rz-n__error inside the card that failed: the fact, the time, what happened to your data, a retry in place and a second, different way through. The camera does not move.
Empty data-state="empty" A designed plot: dashed rim in --rz-empty, room around it, an invitation. It states that it knows there is nothing, which is what tells it from unknown.
Archived data-state="archived" PlantPal's deletes are soft, so archived is a state a card wears — dotted rim, quieter ink — not a removal. There is no delete confirmation anywhere in this system.
AI limit reached data-limit="reached" PlantPal's AI endpoints are rate-limited per user, so this is a first-class state of the card that asked: what the limit is, when it resets, and a way through that does not need the model.

What holds in every state

Six things never degrade, and a build that loses any of them has a defect rather than a state: the position of every card; the you-are-here marker in the world view; the drawn veins and their traversability; the ability to look around with the camera; keyboard traversal along the graph; and the difference between a stake and a chip. Under reduced motion the sweep is spent, but the trail is still drawn along the real veins and held and the you-are-here still moves, so direction and distance survive — carried by traversal.

Accessibility

A degraded state that only shows is not a state; it has to be said. Each card's accessible name carries its own condition — “Region · Balcony · not fetched yet · 1 vein out” — so a screen-reader user meets the same distinction between unknown and empty that a sighted user sees in the two dashed rims. A skeleton is marked aria-hidden: it is the shape of an answer, not the answer, and reading three grey bars aloud tells nobody anything. The waiting sentence beside it is what gets read.

Entering a state announces itself once, in the polite live region, in the same voice the cards use: going offline says what is still yours to walk, not that something broke. A refused mutation says why it was refused at the moment you try it — silence after a click reads as a dead control, which is worse than a refusal. A queued one says it is queued and when it will run.

Nothing here removes a route. Every card is still reachable by keyboard along the graph in every state, including the unknown region, and no state disables the camera, the world view or arrow-key traversal. Colour never carries a state alone: unknown and empty differ by dash pattern, fill and words as well as hue; failure carries its sentence; staleness is a line of text, not a tint. Logical properties throughout, so nothing points the wrong way in RTL. The full keyboard and announcement contract is accessibility.

Usage guidance

Every mistake in this area is the same mistake wearing a different hat: letting a local truth speak for the whole world. One request is slow, so the screen is covered. One region is unfetched, so the garden is redrawn. One connection is gone, so a banner declares everything stale. Keep the fact where it happened.

Do

Draw the failure inside the card that failed, with the time, the fate of the user's data, a retry in place and a second way through. The camera stays where it was (Law 5, C25).

Don’t

Announce it over the world. A layer across the plane breaks the geography and the you-are-here at once, and it tells the user their whole garden is broken when one request is (C25, C16).

Do

Give each card its own staleness sentence offline, in its own words, and let them differ — “Cached Jul 20 — guides change rarely” beside “Never fetched” (Law 4, C24).

Don’t

Put one banner across the top saying the data may be out of date. It is a lie about every card that is genuinely current, and the user learns to disbelieve all of it (C24).

Do

Let a card that is waiting keep its cell, its rank and its veins, and fill in place as it resolves. The screen at ten seconds and at ten seconds plus one is the same geography with more ink in it (Law 1, C22).

Don’t

Hold cards back until their contents arrive and lay them out on arrival. Anything that places by arrival order re-lays-out the world on every refresh, and the reader loses the map they had learned (C7, C22).

Do

Mark an unfetched region unknown, with no count, a belief stated as a belief, and a vein you can actually travel to fetch it (Law 3, C23).

Don’t

Draw it as empty, or leave it off the plane. Empty says we looked and there is nothing; absent says it does not exist. Both are claims we have not earned, and both dead-end the reader (C23).

Three more, stated as rules. Never ship a spinner or the word “loading”, not even as the wrong half of a Do/Don't — an illustration gets copied. Never let a state change rank: a card that fails is still exactly as far from the focus as it was, and demoting it would tell the reader the graph changed shape when only its contents did (C9). And never confuse an aggregate with a gap: “+N more” is loaded, complete and counted — solid rim, real count — and it is a density decision owned by aggregate, not a state of not-knowing.

Where else this appears

The card material every state is drawn onto is node; the dashed vein into an unfetched region is edge; the plane that keeps its shape through all of it is world. The probe panel's register — why it is furniture and never content — is chrome, and the marker that keeps saying where you are is minimap. The “+N more” card that is complete rather than partial is aggregate; the stakes that are refused or queued offline are action rail; the health and confidence instruments a failed identification leaves unfilled are vitals. Hops into an unknown region, and reduced motion, are carried by traversal; a state landing while you are elsewhere is arrivals; a garden that is empty because it is new rather than because it is unfetched is day zero. The rule that a card keeps its cell as the graph fills in is spacing, the state colours sit in colors, and the wording of every sentence a state speaks is voice and copy.
