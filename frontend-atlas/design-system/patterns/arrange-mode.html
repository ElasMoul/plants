Arrange mode

Arrange is where your garden becomes yours to lay out by hand. This page settles that it is a mode and not a toggle laid over normal behaviour, what a drag stores, and why every other instrument on the board reads that one stored thing.

What this is

The lattice puts every body in your garden somewhere sensible without being asked, and most of the time that is enough. But it is your garden. You know that the two figs by the window belong beside each other, that the problems you are actually treating belong up near the top, and that the seedlings you check every morning belong where your eye lands first. Arrange is the mode that lets you say so.

Press Arrange beside the world view and the camera pulls back until the whole garden is in frame, every card opens to its full content, and the whole surface of each card becomes its handle. Drag a card and it stays where you put it. Nothing else on the board responds while you are in here — you cannot travel by accident, you cannot log a watering by accident, and no record changes. Press Esc or the same button again and you are back exactly where you were standing, at the zoom you were standing at, with your placements kept.

The formula a position is derived from, the cell semantics and the insertion rule belong to spacing; the cursor grammar and the pointer-precedence rules belong to interaction and cursors; the map that grows to hold an arranged body belongs to minimap. This page settles the mode itself and the offset it writes.

The laws

Law 1 · C15, C16

Arrange is a mode. While it is on, every other interaction on the board is inert: no travel, no mutation, no pan, no wheel-zoom. It is not a drag behaviour added on top of the normal board — it is a different board with one thing to do on it.

Call RZ.setArrange(true), then click every card, every .rz-stake and every .rz-chip on the plane. Assert RZ.focus is unchanged, the camera transform is unchanged by any of those clicks, and no record field changed. Assert getComputedStyle(stake).pointerEvents === "none". Then drag the bare board and assert RZ.cam.x/RZ.cam.y did not move.

Law 2 · C16

Entering puts the whole garden in frame and opens every card in full, because you cannot place what you cannot see and you cannot judge a placement from a recap. Rank stops being spent on size for as long as the mode is on.

Enter Arrange and assert every .rz-n carries data-show="full" and computes the same inline-size as var(--rz-card-near), that RZ.cam.k equals the value RZ.fitAll() computes from RZ.extent(), and that exactly one element still carries data-focus="true" — the mode moved the camera, not your place in the garden.

Law 3 · C7, C9

A drag stores an offset against the record. The cell stays the body's identity; the offset is your preference laid on top of it. “Two consecutive loads of the same graph lay out identically, with positions derived deterministically and persisted — never from a live simulation or render order”, and an arranged position is part of what is persisted.

Drag a card. Assert its data-cell is unchanged and that data-dx/data-dy now carry the offset. Reload with those offsets restored and diff the computed left/top of every .rz-n against the pre-reload values: equal. Reverse the DOM order of the cards and reload again: still equal.

Law 4 · C3, C9

Every consumer of position reads that one record — the veins, the world view's extent, Fit, and keyboard direction-finding — so none of them can disagree with where the card visibly is. A drag redraws them all from the edge list rather than appending to what is already drawn.

Drag a card well past the others and assert: every .rz-vein touching it ends at its new centre; the count of drawn veins is unchanged, so nothing was duplicated on release; RZ.extent().w has grown to include it; RZ.neighbourInDirection("ArrowRight") now proposes it if it lies that way. Then assert no other card's computed left/top moved at all.

Law 5 · C15, C16

Leaving gives you back the camera you arrived with. Arrange borrows the view to do its work and returns it; it changes no data and performs no traversal, in or out.

Record RZ.cam and RZ.focus, enter Arrange, drag two cards, press Esc. Assert RZ.cam.x, RZ.cam.y and RZ.cam.k equal the recorded values, RZ.focus is the same body, history.length is unchanged, and the two offsets survived.

Arrange, running

Press Arrange in the world-view corner. Watch the camera fit the whole garden and every card open. Drag Office Fig out to the right, past where the lattice would ever have put it, and watch the veins follow it and the map grow to hold it — its dot marks itself as one you placed. Press Esc: you are back at the fig you were standing in front of, at the zoom you left, and the placement stayed.

The mode has one entry point and one exit, both the same call. A page never sets data-arrange by hand, because entering is four things happening together — the flag, the presentation, the fit, and the announcement — and a hand-set attribute would get one of them:

RZ.setArrange(true);   /* fits the world, opens every card, goes inert */
RZ.setArrange(false);  /* restores the camera you arrived with */
RZ.arrange;            /* true while the mode is on — the guard every
                          handler in system.js reads first */

What the mode owns

Arrange is carried by one document attribute and three node-level ones. A theme fills the material; it does not add a fifth.

The attributes of the mode
AttributeWhereMeans
data-arrange="on" the document root The mode is on. Every rule below hangs off this one attribute, so the mode cannot be half-entered.
data-dragging="true" the card being moved This card is in your hand right now. It lifts to the lit halo and rides above its neighbours until you let go.
data-dx / data-dy the card Your offset, in garden units, written on release. This is the whole of what a drag stores — the cell underneath is untouched.
data-cell the card Unchanged by arranging, ever. It is the body's identity on the lattice, and it is what insertion and the free-cell search still reason about.
aria-pressed #rz-arrange Whether the mode is on, spoken. The runtime writes it; the page authors the button and its name only.
What the mode changes, and what it refuses to
SurfaceIn ArrangeWhy
the card cursor: move, one width, full opacity, full content The whole card is the handle, and move rather than grab because you are moving a body, not hauling the board (interaction and cursors).
the board cursor: default, no pan, no wheel-zoom The board is not the thing you are handling here. Leaving pan live would mean every missed grab slid the world out from under your placement.
stakes and chips inert and dimmed Nothing on this board changes data or travels while you are laying it out. A drag that starts on a button must not fire the button (C15).
rank not spent on size All four widths would make the far bodies unreadable at the fit-everything zoom, and you are placing them, not reading them.
the camera fitted on entry, restored on exit Borrowed, not taken. Arrange is the only thing in the system that moves the camera without you asking, and it gives it back.
the world view extent grows; scale still ignores the camera A body placed past the lattice edge is still on the map, in the place you put it, marked as yours (minimap).
your records untouched Arrange writes an offset and nothing else. No health, no count, no journal entry, no archive.

Accessibility

Entering and leaving are announced, in full sentences. Entering says what changed and what to do: “Arrange mode. Every card is open and the whole world is in view. Drag any card to place it; nothing else responds until you leave.” Leaving says what was kept and where you are: “Left arrange mode. Positions kept. You are still at Office Fig.” Each release says so too — “Office Fig moved. Its position is kept.” — so a reader who cannot see the card land still hears that the placement took.

The mode has a name and a state, not just a look. The entry control is an ordinary button carrying aria-pressed, so its on-ness is readable without seeing the fitted camera. There is no third state and no hidden one.

Esc always leaves. One key, from anywhere on the board, with no confirmation to dismiss and nothing lost by pressing it — every offset is already stored the moment you let go of a card. That is the whole keyboard contract of the mode, and it is the same key that steps back a hop outside it, so it never means “discard” in either place.

The arrow keys are given back on exit. Inside the mode they do not travel, because nothing travels in here. Outside it they follow the veins as they always do, computed from the neighbours' real positions — which now include the ones you moved (accessibility).

Dragging is not the only way to have a garden you can read. Be honest about this: pointer drag is currently the only way to set an offset, and a reader who cannot drag depends on the lattice's own placement being good. The lattice is deterministic and complete, so nothing is unreachable — but if you are asking whether that is enough, the honest answer is that it is a gap, and a keyboard nudge belongs in a later amendment rather than in a claim here.

No coordinate is ever spoken. Not on entry, not on release, not on the map's label. The cell, the row and the column are the system's private vocabulary; what you hear is the body's name and that its position is kept.

Using it

Arrange earns its place by being small and total: one button, one thing to do, one key out.

Do

Make the whole mode inert except the drag. When the only thing that responds is the thing you came in to do, you can move fast and sloppily across the board and nothing will fire behind your back (C15).

Don’t

Leave travel and stakes live and hang dragging off a modifier key. Then every placement is a gamble on whether the pointer moved far enough to count as a drag, and a missed one waters a plant or hops you to another body.

Do

Store the drag as an offset against the record and let the veins, the map, Fit and the arrow keys all read that one record. They then cannot disagree about where a body is, and a reload puts it back exactly (C7, C9).

Don’t

Write the drag straight onto the card's own transform, or give it a new cell. The map, the veins and the keyboard then each keep their own idea of where it is, and the first reload throws the user's work away.

Do

Give the camera back on exit, at the zoom and the position the user arrived with. Arranging is something you step out to do; it should not cost you your place.

Don’t

Leave the reader parked at the fit-everything zoom afterwards. They are now somewhere they did not choose to be, and the mode has quietly done a second thing it never said it would do (C16).
