Sheet

The sheet is Herbarium's mounting card: a bordered surface that holds one subject — a plant, a scan, a place in your garden — with its title above a rule and its actions below one. It is the surface almost every other component sits on.

Stable v1.0.0

Overview

A sheet is paper, not a panel. It carries one subject and says what that subject is before you read a word of the body: the header names it, the hairline rule under the header separates the name from the evidence, and the footer holds what you can do about it.

Use a sheet when the content on it belongs to a single named thing you could point at in your garden. Use it for a plant's detail surface, a recent scan, a bed, or a grouped set of facts you want read together.

Do not use a sheet as a general box. A page section does not need one, a list of neighbours belongs to Edges, and a nursery-stake summary of one plant belongs to Plant tag. Nesting a sheet inside a sheet is always wrong — if the inner content is a separate subject, it needs its own place in the world, not a card inside a card.

Rule of thumb

One sheet, one subject, one title. If you cannot write the header without an "and", you have two sheets.

Anatomy

A sheet is four parts, and only the body is required to have content. The header carries the subject's name and, on the end, one piece of status. The footer carries actions and never carries prose.

Parts

ClassRequiredWhat it holds
.hbm-sheet Yes The mounting surface itself — background, hairline border, sheet radius and the one lift this system gives a resting surface.
.hbm-sheet__header No The subject's name at the start, one status at the end, closed by a hairline rule. Omit it only when the sheet's subject is already named by the heading above it.
.hbm-sheet__title With the header The subject's name. Put it on the heading element that is correct for the page's outline, never on a <div>.
.hbm-sheet__body Yes The evidence: prose, a plate, facts, a log. Its last child's bottom margin is removed for you.
.hbm-sheet__footer No Actions and edges, in a row, above a hairline rule. Never explanatory text — that belongs in the body.

Variants

The sheet has one modifier. .hbm-sheet--mounted draws the ruled mount margin of a real herbarium sheet down the leading edge, in the accent border. Use it to mark the sheet that is the subject of the current view — the plant you opened, the scan you are reading. Everything else on the page stays plain, so the mount margin keeps meaning something.

Sheets in a bed

Several sheets of the same kind lay out in .hbm-bed, which fills the row and wraps. Keep the sheets in a bed structurally identical — same header shape, same footer shape — so the eye can compare them down a column instead of re-reading each one.

Behaviour

The sheet is a surface, not a control. It has no hover state, no pressed state and no selected state, because nothing about it is activatable — the things you can activate sit on it. If a whole sheet appears to need to be clickable, what you want is an edge row; see Edges.

States

A sheet's states are the states of its contents, and they are drawn in place. When a fact is still loading, wrap that fact in .hbm-growing and leave the sheet where it is; the sheet does not collapse, shrink or get replaced by a placeholder card. When the subject fails to load, the sheet keeps its header and its body carries .hbm-node-error with the retry inside it, so the failure stays attached to the thing that failed.

Keyboard

The sheet takes no focus of its own and adds nothing to the tab order. Focus moves through its contents in reading order: header status is text, then the body's controls, then the footer's action and its edge. Because the footer is last in the markup as well as last on screen, that order is already correct — do not reorder the parts visually.

Reduced motion

The sheet does not animate. It does not fade in, and it never moves when its contents change: a new count, a new health leaf or a new photo updates in place. Under prefers-reduced-motion nothing about a sheet changes, because there was nothing to reduce.

Accessibility

Contrast. The sheet's background and text both come from semantic roles, so body text on a sheet meets contrast in both the paper and dusk themes. The hairline border is decorative and is never the only thing carrying meaning — the mount margin on --mounted marks the current subject, but the subject is also named in the header.

Semantics. A sheet is a <div> by default and needs no role. Give it role="region" with aria-labelledby pointing at the title only when the sheet is a landmark a user would want to jump to, such as the plant you opened. Do not add role="region" to every sheet in a bed; a dozen regions makes the landmark list useless.

<div class="hbm-sheet hbm-sheet--mounted" role="region" aria-labelledby="sheet-fern">
  <div class="hbm-sheet__header">
    <h3 class="hbm-sheet__title" id="sheet-fern">Fern</h3>
  </div>
</div>

Heading level. .hbm-sheet__title styles a heading; it does not choose one. Pick the level that continues the page's outline and never skip a level to get a smaller title.

Screen-reader announcement. A status in the header is read after the name, so write it as a phrase that stands alone — "Needs attention", not "attention". Where a status is drawn only as a shape, give it a text label with .hbm-visually-hidden, so the leaf colour is never the sole carrier of meaning.

Live updates. When a fact inside a sheet changes because of something the user just did, announce it once through a toast rather than marking the sheet as a live region; the sheet is quiet paper and should stay that way.

Usage guidance

Keep one subject per sheet, keep the footer to actions, and keep the mount margin rare.
