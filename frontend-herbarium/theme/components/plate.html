Components

Plate

A plate is one photograph of your plant with the affected region ringed by hand and a numbered caption underneath. It is how PlantPal shows you what it is looking at, so that you can disagree with it.

Stable v1.0.0

Overview

Use a plate wherever a photograph carries a claim: the result of a scan, a diagnosis on a leaf, a before-and-after in the plant's record, a recent scan in your garden. The ring is the point — it says this patch, not the whole plant, and it lets you check the system's reading against what you can see in your hand.

Do not use a plate for decoration. A photograph with nothing to say about it is just an image and belongs in the body of a sheet; the plant's name and standing facts belong on a plant tag; what was done and when belongs in the care log.

A plate never appears alone when it carries a diagnosis. Every diagnostic plate is accompanied by a confidence reading from Health & confidence, and a low reading is spoken plainly with .hbm-hedge rather than softened away — the full rule is on Uncertainty.

About the previews on this page

This documentation site ships no photographs, so the frames below are empty. In the product, .hbm-plate__image is the first child of the frame and fills it; the annotation and the caption sit over and under it exactly as shown. The The photograph section gives the markup with the image in place.

Specimen

Anatomy

A plate has four parts. They appear in this order, and nothing else belongs inside the frame.

PartClassRule
Plate .hbm-plate The container, normally a figure. It sets no margin of its own — space it with the layout around it.
Frame .hbm-plate__frame Holds the photograph and every annotation over it. Its ratio is fixed at 4:3 so a row of plates lines up whatever the source photographs were, and it clips its own overflow.
Image .hbm-plate__image The photograph. Required in the product, and always carries real alt text — see Accessibility. It fills the frame and is cropped by it; never scale or letterbox it yourself.
Region .hbm-plate__region The hand-drawn ring around the patch under discussion. Optional — omit it when the system is looking at the whole plant. At most one region per plate.
Region label .hbm-plate__region-label A short noun phrase naming the ringed patch, placed inside the region. Three to five words; it names, it does not diagnose.
Caption .hbm-plate__caption The plate number in .hbm-plate-no followed by one sentence. Required whenever the plate carries a claim.

The photograph

In the product the image is the first child of the frame, and the annotation follows it so that the ring draws over the photograph. The alt text describes what the camera saw, not what the system concluded — the conclusion is in the caption, where you can read it and disagree.

Where the ring goes

The region reads its position and size from four custom properties on the element, as percentages of the frame. Percentages, not lengths — the frame is responsive, and the patch has to stay over the same leaf at every width. Their defaults ring the upper start corner of the frame, which is where the previews on this page sit.

PropertyDefaultMeaning
--hbm-region-x 20% Distance from the frame's start edge. Logical, so a mirrored layout mirrors the ring with the photograph.
--hbm-region-y 20% Distance from the frame's top edge.
--hbm-region-w 30% Width of the ring.
--hbm-region-h 30% Height of the ring.

These four values are stored with the scan, exactly like a node's coordinates. Compute them once when the region is detected and persist them; two loads of the same plate must ring the same patch. Never recompute the ring at render time, and never animate it into place.

</div>

Variants

Severe

Add .hbm-plate__region--severe when the finding is harm rather than something to keep an eye on: rot, infestation, tissue already lost. It changes the ring's colour and nothing else — the plate does not grow, flash or move up the view. Reserve it for findings a gardener must act on today, or it stops meaning anything.

Unringed

Omit the region when the photograph is a record rather than a finding — a plant's portrait, a growth comparison, a recent scan that came back clear. The caption still names what you are looking at and when it was taken.

Behaviour

States

A plate is a still object. It has no hover state, no selected state and no state of its own that a photograph would not have; everything below is about the data arriving.

StateHow it is drawnRule
Loading .hbm-growing on the frame The frame keeps its 4:3 ratio while the photograph arrives, so nothing around the plate shifts when it lands.
Not loaded .hbm-unknown-region in place of the plate A scan that has not come back is unknown, not absent. Say so in the hatched region rather than showing a plate with no caption.
Failed .hbm-node-error with its retry The failure and the retry stay where the plate was. Never a page-level banner, and never a silent gap.
Stale .hbm-stale-note beside the caption An old scan is still useful. Date it and say it is old rather than hiding it.

Keyboard

A plate is not interactive, so it takes no focus and needs no key. When a plate leads somewhere — to the plant it belongs to, or to a fuller diagnosis — put an edge under the caption and let that edge take the focus. Do not make the frame clickable; an image that quietly navigates is the confusion the whole edge vocabulary exists to prevent.

Reduced motion

Nothing on a plate animates. The ring is drawn with the photograph and stays there, and the loading shimmer stops under prefers-reduced-motion while the frame keeps its shape. Add no transition of your own.

Accessibility

The ring is not information. It is a pointer, and a pointer no screen reader can follow. Everything the ring means has to be in text as well: name the patch in .hbm-plate__region-label and state the finding in the caption. A plate whose meaning is only in its geometry is unfinished.

Alt text is required and is never the diagnosis. Describe the photograph — the plant, the framing, what is visibly different. The system's reading belongs in the caption, so that someone listening hears the evidence and the claim as two separate things, in that order.

Contrast. The ring is drawn in the caution role, or the danger role when severe, over a photograph of unknown brightness, and a hairline over a photograph is never a reliable contrast pairing. That is why the label and caption carry the meaning; do not thin the ring, tint it, or drop it to a lighter role.

Announcement. The figure and figcaption pairing is what associates the caption with the image, so keep the caption inside the figure. Announce a plate as image then caption: the photograph's description, the plate number, the sentence. When a confidence reading sits beside the plate, keep it adjacent in the markup as well as on screen, so it is heard with the claim rather than several elements later.

A new plate is not an alert. A scan finishing puts the plate in place; it does not interrupt. If a finding is severe enough to need saying out loud, say it in a polite live region alongside the plate, never with role="alert" — the pattern is on Uncertainty. Where a plate is announced with no visible text, hide the redundant copy with .hbm-visually-hidden rather than a clip style of your own.

Usage guidance

Do
Low confidence This might be spider mite. A closer photograph of the underside of a leaf would help.

Say how sure the system is next to the plate, so a weak reading is something you can weigh rather than something you have to catch.

Don't
Spider mite

State a finding as a settled fact when the system is guessing — a bare verdict hides the doubt that would have made you look again.

Do
Plate 014 Open Fern

Put the way onward under the caption as an edge, so the photograph stays a photograph and the destination is visible before it is taken.

Don't

Turn the plate into a button — a filled action promises to change your garden, and this one only moves you.

Two more rules, both non-obvious. One ring per plate. If a scan finds two patches, show two plates with two numbers; two rings in one frame leaves you unable to tell which one the caption is about. Never re-crop to make the ring look tidy. The frame crops the photograph the same way every time, and moving the crop to centre a finding changes what the evidence appears to show.
