Components

Health & confidence

Two separate readings, always shown together: how the plant is doing, and how sure PlantPal is about what it just told you. One is about the plant; the other is about the system.

Stable v1.0.0

Overview

A health reading is a claim about your plant: thriving, stable, watch, ailing, lost. A confidence reading is a claim about the claim: how much evidence PlantPal had when it made it. Keep them apart. A confident diagnosis of an ailing plant and an uncertain guess at a healthy one look nothing alike, and the person holding the phone needs to tell them apart before deciding whether to act.

Use .hbm-health wherever a plant's condition appears — on a plant tag, in a node, at the head of a sheet. Use .hbm-confidence on every identification or diagnosis the system produced, without exception. When confidence is low, add .hbm-hedge and say what you are unsure about in words.

Do not use either for progress, for a rating, or for anything the person entered themselves. What someone typed is not a claim PlantPal needs to hedge, and a health leaf next to their own note reads as the system doubting them.

Health levels

Five levels, no more. Each is a leaf mark plus its word — the mark alone is never enough, because colour is not readable by everyone and "watch" and "ailing" sit close together in hue.

ModifierTokenSayUse when
.hbm-health--thriving --hbm-health-thriving Thriving New growth since the last check, and no care overdue.
.hbm-health--stable --hbm-health-stable Stable Nothing has changed and nothing is due. The resting state of most plants in my garden.
.hbm-health--watch --hbm-health-watch Needs attention Something is drifting — care overdue, or a sign worth a second look. Not an emergency.
.hbm-health--ailing --hbm-health-ailing Ailing A diagnosed problem is spreading and the plant will get worse without care.
.hbm-health--lost --hbm-health-lost Lost The person recorded that the plant died. Kept in the garden, never deleted quietly.

Health is recorded, not guessed

A level only changes from a scan, a logged care event, or something the person told PlantPal. Never derive a level from how long the app has been open or how long since a photo — an absence of information is not a decline, and drawing it as one teaches people to distrust the leaf.

Confidence rungs

Three rungs, drawn as a small ladder with its word beside it. Three rung elements are always present; the modifier fills one, two or three of them, so the unfilled rungs show how much certainty is missing rather than hiding it.

ModifierTokenSayUse when
.hbm-confidence--high --hbm-confidence-high High confidence One clear match and no close rival. Safe to name the plant plainly.
.hbm-confidence--medium --hbm-confidence-medium Fairly sure A leading match with others behind it. Name it, and offer the alternatives.
.hbm-confidence--low --hbm-confidence-low Not sure Several matches, or a poor photo. Pair with .hbm-hedge and ask for what would help.
— --hbm-confidence-track — The unfilled rung. Present at every level, so the ladder's height never changes.

The hedge

The hedge is the herbarium label's cf. — the collector's mark for "compare with, but I could not confirm". It carries its own mark, so you never write "cf." into the text yourself. Use it whenever confidence is low, and say in the same breath what would raise it.

Both readings in place

On a real sheet the two sit side by side under the name: the plant's condition first, the system's certainty second. Read left to right in English, they say "here is how it is doing, and here is how much to trust me".

Behaviour

Neither reading is interactive. They are labels, not controls: nothing here is focusable, nothing responds to hover, and neither is ever the thing a person clicks. If you want the person to act on a reading, put an .hbm-act or an .hbm-nav next to it and let that carry the interaction.

A change of level is a non-structural change, so it updates in place. The leaf changes colour and the word changes with it; the reading does not move, the sheet does not re-order, and nothing around it reflows. The same holds for a rung count: the ladder keeps its height and its position, and only the fill changes.

Under prefers-reduced-motion nothing on this page behaves differently, because neither component animates at any setting. Both are drawn states, not transitions — a plant that started ailing overnight simply is ailing when you open the app.

When a reading has not loaded yet, do not draw a health level or a confidence level at all. Use the honesty states instead: an unloaded region is unknown, not thriving, and a stale reading is marked stale rather than silently shown as current.

Accessibility

Both components carry their own text, and that text is the accessible name. Never ship a bare leaf or a bare ladder: a mark on its own is unreadable to a screen reader, to anyone who cannot separate the greens and yellows, and to anyone reading in bright sun beside a plant.

Colour is a second channel here, never the only one. The leaf and the rungs repeat what the word already says, so a person who sees no colour difference between "needs attention" and "ailing" still reads them correctly. The words carry text colour at body or caption weight and meet contrast against the sheet in both the paper and dusk themes; the leaf and rung marks are decoration and are exempt.

Neither component is focusable and neither is in the tab order, which is correct — there is nothing to operate. The keyboard path runs through the actions and edges beside them. Do not add tabindex to make a reading reachable; if it needs reaching, it needs an action.

When a level changes while the page is open, announce it once through the region that owns the sheet, in the same words shown on screen: "Kitchen Fern, needs attention". Do not announce the confidence rungs as a number out of three — say "fairly sure", the same phrase a sighted person reads.

Where the reading is drawn inside a graphic rather than as text — a leaf on a node in the world view — pair it with .hbm-visually-hidden text carrying the same word, so the node's name and its condition are announced together.

Usage guidance

Show confidence on every claim PlantPal makes, including the confident ones. A rung ladder that only appears when the system is unsure teaches people that its presence means trouble, and they stop reading it.

Do
Ailing Not sure

Show the condition and the certainty together, so a serious-sounding reading arrives with the caveat that PlantPal is guessing.

Don't
Ailing

Show a diagnosis alone — the person reads a guess as a verdict and repots a plant that was fine.

Do
Possibly leaf spot. A close photo of one marked leaf would help.

Say what is uncertain and what would resolve it, so the next step belongs to the person and is easy to take.

Don't
Leaf spot 62%

Hand over a raw percentage in an alarm-coloured badge — it reads as urgent, and a beginner cannot act on 62%.

Keep the wording fixed. "Needs attention", "fairly sure" and "not sure" are the system's words for these levels everywhere in PlantPal; rewriting them per screen makes the same reading look like two different things. The full rules for phrasing an uncertain claim live on Uncertainty, and the voice these phrases come from is set out on Voice & copy.
