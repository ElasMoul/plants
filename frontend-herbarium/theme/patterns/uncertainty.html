Uncertainty

PlantPal often knows less than it could pretend to. This pattern is how it says so: a named plant carries how sure it is, a region that has not loaded is drawn as unknown rather than left blank, and a failure stays in the place it happened, with its retry.

Stable v1.0.0

Overview

A photo of a leaf does not always name a plant, a network does not always answer, and a garden on its first day is genuinely empty. Each of those is a different kind of not-knowing, and the user can act on each one differently — so the system draws them differently and never rounds any of them to a blank space.

Use this pattern wherever the app states something it inferred rather than something you told it: identification from a scan, a diagnosis from a photo, data that arrived a while ago, data that has not arrived at all, a request that failed, and the empty garden you start with. Every one of them belongs to a specific place on the page — the plant, the region, the node — and stays there.

Do not use it to soften a fact the system is certain of. A watering you logged yourself is not uncertain, and dressing it with a hedge teaches the user to ignore the hedge when it matters. The parts this pattern assembles are documented in Health & confidence and Plate; the words to put in them are on Voice & copy.

The rule this page exists for

Say when unsure. A claim the system inferred is shown with its confidence attached, in the same glance, or it is not shown at all.

The three rungs of confidence

Confidence has three rungs and no percentage. A number invites the user to do arithmetic they have no basis for; three rungs answer the only question they actually have, which is whether to act on this now or look closer first. The rungs are drawn, so they survive a screenshot, a colour-blind reader and a bright balcony.

Low confidence is honest, not alarming. It uses the calm sill colour, never the danger colour — the system being unsure about your fern is not bad news about your fern.

RungClassWords to useWhat the user should do
Three .hbm-confidence--high Sure Act on it. The name is offered as the plant's nickname without asking again.
Two .hbm-confidence--medium Fairly sure Confirm it. The name is offered, with a way to pick a different one.
One .hbm-confidence--low Not sure Look again. Lead with a hedge and ask for a second photo before naming anything.

Identification

A scan produces a candidate, not a verdict. Show the candidate with its confidence in the same sheet, in the same glance, and keep the way to disagree next to it — the user is standing in front of the plant and knows things the photo does not.

A candidate you should not act on yet

At one rung, the hedge leads and the name follows it. The primary action becomes taking another photo, because that is the thing that will actually settle it; naming the plant drops to a quiet action so it is still available to a user who already knows.

Diagnosis

A diagnosis says something is wrong with a plant someone cares about, so it carries two separate things the user must not confuse: where on the plant the system is looking, and how sure it is about what it sees. The ring shows the first; the confidence shows the second. Never let one stand in for the other — a firmly drawn ring around a region does not make an unsure diagnosis sure.

Diagnosis copy names what was seen before what it might mean, and never prescribes a treatment at one rung. This documentation site ships no photographs, so the frame below is empty; in the product the image is the first child of the frame.

One severe ring at a time

Use .hbm-plate__region--severe only for something that will cost the plant if it is left this week. Everything else is a caution ring, so that the one time it matters, the user has not stopped noticing.

Unknown is not empty

A place that has not loaded is not a place with nothing in it, and the two must never look alike: one asks the user to wait or tap, the other asks them to plant something. Unknown is drawn hatched and dashed, keeps its name, and stays exactly where it will be once it loads, so nothing jumps when it does.

Both nodes sit in the world at their own persisted coordinates, beside the one focal node, exactly as they do in World & nodes — an unloaded neighbour keeps its place on the map rather than being left out of it. While a region resolves, the geography holds still and only the text that is arriving is drawn as growing — the map never rearranges itself around a load. Moving between regions is Traversal.

Offline and failure

Offline is a state of the whole app and belongs at the top of it, stated once and calmly: what still works, and what will happen to what you do now. A failure is different — it belongs to the one thing that failed, and it carries its own retry so the user does not have to find their way back afterwards.

An error message says what did not happen and what is still safe, in that order, and never asks the user to guess at a cause they cannot check. Do not put a failure in a toast: a toast leaves, and the user is then holding a phone that shows no sign anything went wrong.

Day zero

An empty garden is the one honest emptiness in the app — nothing failed and nothing is missing, there is simply room. It reads as an invitation with a single thing to do, and it never apologises for being empty or shows a picture of someone else's plants.

Accessibility

Uncertainty is information, so it has to survive every way a person reads the screen. None of these states may be carried by colour alone: confidence has three drawn rungs and a word, unknown regions carry a hatch and a dashed border, stale data carries its age in text, and the diagnostic ring carries a label.

What a screen reader hears

Give every confidence indicator a text label in the same element — the words Sure, Fairly sure and Not sure above are read out, and are the only thing a non-visual reader has. If a layout has no room for the word, put it back with .hbm-visually-hidden rather than dropping it.

The rungs are decoration once the word is present, so hide them from the reader with aria-hidden="true" and let the word carry the meaning. A hedge is prose and needs nothing added; it is read in place, before the name it qualifies, which is the same order a sighted user reads it in.

Announcing a state change

When a result arrives, a region loads, or a retry fails, announce it in the region it belongs to with aria-live="polite", and never with assertive — nothing here is urgent enough to interrupt someone mid-sentence. Announce the outcome and the confidence together in one message, so they cannot be heard apart.

Keyboard and focus

Every retry, every unknown region that can be fetched, and the day-zero action are reachable in document order and operable with Enter and Space. A failed action keeps focus where it was; a retry that succeeds moves focus to the content that arrived, so the next Tab continues from the new material rather than the top of the page.

Contrast and motion

The unknown, stale and offline colours are set to hold their contrast against both paper and dusk themes, and are always paired with a border or a dot so the state is legible without them. The growing placeholder is the only looping motion in this pattern, and under prefers-reduced-motion it stops moving and holds at reduced opacity while the content resolves.

Usage guidance

Two rules cover most of the mistakes: show confidence with the claim, and let each kind of not-knowing keep its own shape.

Do
Possibly a peace lily Not sure

Put the confidence next to the claim it qualifies, so the user reads both before deciding anything.

Don't
Peace lily Low match

State a guess as a name and push the doubt into an alarming badge — the user acts on the name and learns to distrust the app.

Do
Balcony — not loaded yet

Draw a region that has not loaded as unknown, keeping its name, so the user knows something is there and can ask for it.

Don't

No plants here yet

Show a place that simply has not loaded as an empty one — the user believes their balcony is gone.

The full reasoning behind these sits in the sixth principle, Say when unsure. The single-hop parts this pattern arranges are on Edges and Health & confidence.
