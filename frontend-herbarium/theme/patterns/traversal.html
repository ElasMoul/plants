Traversal

Traversal is a whole journey through your garden: the hop you take, the way back you can still see, and the world that holds still around you. Edges are the parts; this page is how they are put together into a walk you never get lost on.

Stable v1.0.0

Overview

There is one world in PlantPal and you are always somewhere in it. Moving is a hop from where you stand to a neighbour you could already see, along an edge that was drawn before you took it. The world does not close and reopen — the camera travels, your marker travels with it, and everything else stays exactly where it was.

Use this pattern for every move between places: a bed to a plant, a plant to its care log, a scan to the plant it identified. Use it for the way back, for asking permission to enter somewhere that needs it, and for stepping out to settings.

Do not use it for anything that changes your garden — a change is an action, and it never moves the camera. The single-hop pieces live in Edges, the map they are drawn on lives in World & nodes, and what to do when a destination has not loaded lives in Uncertainty. This page assumes all three and describes the walk.

The test for a hop

Before the tap, the user can name where they are going. After it, they can name where they came from and see it. If either half fails, the journey is wrong.

The shape of a hop

A hop has four moving parts and they resolve together: the edge lights as the one being travelled, the camera pans to centre the destination, the you-are-here marker moves to the new node, and the minimap keeps the whole garden in view at the size it always was. Nothing is unmounted, so nothing has to be rebuilt when you come back.

Take a hop below — click a node, or reach one with the keyboard and press Enter. The garden underneath is the same graph, with the same coordinates, as the one on World & nodes: two loads of my garden lay out identically, and so do two pages.

A hop is one step at a time. Never chain two moves onto one tap, and never move the camera on the user's behalf because something finished elsewhere — that arrives as a toast naming its origin, and the user decides whether to travel to it.

The way back is a place

Every hop leaves a way back, and it is named. An origin edge points at the place you came from, so returning is a step to somewhere rather than a guess about what the browser remembers. Draw it at the head of the destination, before its content, and keep it there for as long as you are standing in that place.

One origin, one hop back. If a place is three hops deep and the way back is a chain of three names, the world has been built too deep — flatten it so any place in the garden is reachable from the world view directly.

Keyboard traversal follows the graph

On the keyboard, arrow keys walk the real neighbours of the focused node, in the order they are declared — not the order the markup happens to be in. Tab reaches the focused node and its neighbours; nodes that are not adjacent are out of the tab ring, because they are not one hop away. Enter or Space takes the hop.

This is why neighbours are declared rather than derived. data-hbm-neighbours is the list the keyboard walks, the list the degree count reports, and the list the drawn edges match. When those three disagree, the keyboard is the one that betrays it first.

<span class="hbm-node" data-hbm-id="balcony" data-hbm-x="46%" data-hbm-y="70%"
      data-hbm-neighbours="garden,shelf,hallway">…</span>

Do not add your own arrow-key handling on top. The system already walks the graph, and a second handler turns one press into two hops.

The resident's permission ladder

Some places in your garden need something from the person before they can be entered: the camera to take a scan, your location to know the season and the light, notifications to tell you a plant is due when you are not looking. PlantPal asks for each of these at the edge that needs it, in the moment it is needed, and never as a gate at the front door.

The ladder has four rungs, and a resident climbs only as far as the places they actually visit. Nothing above a rung is hidden while you stand below it — you can see the destination, you simply have not been let in yet, and the interface says which of those two it is.

RungAsked atIf declined
Walking Nothing is asked. My garden, beds, plants, care logs and scans already taken are all open. Not applicable — this rung is never gated.
Camera The first time the resident takes the edge to a new scan. The scan edge stays drawn and explains what it needs. Every plant already identified stays readable.
Location The first time a care plan would use daylight and season, on the plant it concerns. Care advice continues without it and says plainly that it is general rather than local.
Notifications After the resident's first plant becomes due today — never before there is anything to be told. Due today keeps working as a place you visit. Nothing is lost, only unannounced.

An ask is drawn as a note beside the edge it belongs to, with the reason first and the grant as an action. It says what PlantPal will do with the thing it is asking for, in one sentence, and it offers a way onward that does not require saying yes.

A declined rung is a state of the place, not an error. Draw the destination as unknown-until-permitted rather than removing it, so the garden the resident can see stays the garden that exists.

Ask once per rung. A resident who says no is asked again only when they take that edge again themselves, and never by a prompt that arrives on its own.

Leaving the world

Settings is not a place in your garden, so going there is an exit rather than a hop: the world pulls back, the app shrinks to a card that stays visible, and the panel opens beside it. You can see the garden you stepped out of the whole time, which is what makes stepping back in obvious.

Nothing inside the drawer navigates. A destination reached from a panel you have already stepped out to leaves you unable to say where you are standing, so the drawer holds settings and its own way back, and nothing else.

Traversal tokens

A journey is drawn from the same semantic roles as its parts, plus the two motion roles that separate a settle from a distance crossed. Set none of these on a page; they are what the components already read.

TokenRoleWhere it lands in a journey
--hbm-edge-active The one edge being travelled. Marked on the edge taken, and only that one, for the length of the hop.
--hbm-node-focus The single focal point of the world. The border of the node you arrived at.
--hbm-marker You are here. The marker on the canvas and its twin on the minimap.
--hbm-marker-halo The marker's ring, so it is findable at any zoom. Around the marker and the focused node.
--hbm-unknown A place that exists but has not loaded, or has not been permitted. Unknown nodes, unknown edges, and the region a declined rung leaves behind.
--hbm-motion-travel The distance crossed. Camera pan, marker travel, the app card pulling back on an exit.
--hbm-motion-state A thing settling in place, having crossed nothing. Edge and node colour on hover and focus.

The two motion roles are not interchangeable. Travel duration on a hover makes the interface feel slow; state duration on a hop hides the journey, which is the one thing the pattern exists to show. Motion holds the scale itself.

Behaviour

Taking a hop. The edge marks active, the camera pans so the destination centres, the marker and minimap dot travel to the new coordinates, and the neighbour set is recomputed from the real degree. Every other node keeps its position exactly.

Zooming. Zoom rescales the world you are already in. It never routes anywhere, never changes the focus, and never replaces the view with a different screen — the minimap stays, so the whole garden is still answerable at any scale.

Arriving from elsewhere. Something that finishes in a place you are not standing in arrives as a toast naming its origin. The camera does not move; the user chooses whether to travel.

A destination that will not load. The failure is a state inside the node with its retry in place. The camera stays where it is and nothing is re-laid-out, so a retry that succeeds does not move the garden under the user's hand.

Escaping an exit. Escape closes the drawer, the app card comes forward, and focus returns to the control that opened it.

Reduced motion. Under prefers-reduced-motion the durations collapse to near-zero, so a hop resolves at once. Nothing is replaced by a fade or a cut: the marker still moves to the new node, the active edge is still marked, and the focus ring still lands. Direction is information in this system, so it is never what gets removed.

Accessibility

Contrast. Node names are primary text and degrees are muted text on the world's own background, and both hold their contrast in paper and dusk. Focus is carried by border weight and the halo ring as well as by colour, so the focal point survives with colour removed entirely.

Semantics. A node is a link — it is given role="link" and taking it moves you. The current node carries aria-current="true", so "where am I?" is answerable without sight of the marker. An origin edge is an ordinary <a> naming its destination.

Keyboard path. Tab reaches the focused node and its declared neighbours only; arrow keys walk the neighbour list in both directions and wrap; Enter or Space takes the hop. Nothing traps focus. Because the tab ring follows adjacency, the number of keys between a user and their next place is the number of hops, not the number of elements on the page.

Announcement. Name a node with its place and its degree together — "Balcony, 3 linked" is a complete offer. On arrival, focus lands on the new focal node so its name is read; the change of place is never announced only by the marker, which is silent to a screen reader.

Permission asks. An ask is content in the flow, not an alert that seizes focus. Give the grant action and the way past adjacent focus stops, and never make the way past harder to reach than the grant. A declined rung must be readable as text — the hatched unknown region carries its sentence inside it for exactly this reason.

The exit. The trigger carries aria-expanded, the drawer takes focus when it opens, Escape closes it, and focus returns to the trigger. The app card behind is not hidden from assistive technology, because it has not gone anywhere.

Direction. Every part of a journey is drawn with logical properties, so the arrow, the origin edge and the drawer all flip for a right-to-left reader with no extra markup. See RTL & locale.

Usage guidance

Move one hop at a time, along an edge the user could already see, and leave the way back named on screen. Ask for permission at the edge that needs it, and let the user past without it.

Do
Balcony Care log

Keep the named origin and the onward edge on screen together, so the user can see both where they came from and where they can go.

Don't
Care log

Offer only the way onward and leave returning to the browser — the origin is then a memory rather than a place.

Ask at the edge that needs it, give the reason first, and keep a way onward for a resident who says no.

Don't

Before you start

Allow the camera, your location and notifications to use PlantPal.

Gather every permission at the front door, before the resident has a garden or a reason — there is nothing here for them to judge the ask against.

Before you ship a journey

Walk it on the keyboard with the sound on, then walk it again with motion reduced. If either walk leaves you unable to say where you are, the journey is not finished. The six principles decide anything this page does not.
