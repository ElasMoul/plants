Foundations

Motion

Motion in PlantPal is growth: slow, continuous, and always travelling from somewhere to somewhere. Four durations, two easings, three roles — you compose from the roles, never from a number you chose yourself.

Stable v1.0.0

Overview

Every transition in this system answers one question: what moved, and from where. A plant that was on the left is still on the left after you look at it. A count that changed is still in the same line of the same sheet. When the camera does travel — you tapped an edge, you opened a plate — the movement shows you the distance you crossed, so you never have to rebuild the map in your head.

Use motion when something changes place: the camera moving between nodes, a menu unfurling from its trigger, a toast arriving from the view it belongs to. Do not use motion for a value that merely changed: a new watering count, a health leaf turning from stable to watch, a photo finishing its upload. Those update where they already are, at once. This is the sixth principle in the principles, and it is enforced by the components — see Traversal for how the world moves and Edges for how arrivals are announced.

You will not write a duration or a curve on a component. The components already carry theirs. Your job on a page is to know which role a change belongs to, and to leave the rest alone.

Duration scale

Four steps, and nothing between them. Each one is a kind of change, not a speed: reach for the one that describes what is happening, not the one that feels right.

TokenValueUse
--hbm-dur-1 120ms A control answering your finger — hover, press, focus, a switch settling.
--hbm-dur-2 240ms A small element appearing or leaving in place, such as a hint under a field.
--hbm-dur-3 420ms Something unfurling from its own origin — a menu, a toast, a drawer.
--hbm-dur-4 700ms The camera crossing the world, and the you-are-here marker travelling with it.

Easing curves

Two curves. Both start gently and end without a bounce, because a plant does not snap back. There is no linear easing in this system and no overshoot: an element that overshoots reads as a pop, and a pop is the one thing motion here must never be.

TokenValueUse
--hbm-ease-unfurl cubic-bezier(0.22, 0.61, 0.20, 1) Something growing into the view: it leaves fast and arrives slowly, so the end of the movement is the part you read.
--hbm-ease-settle cubic-bezier(0.33, 0, 0.15, 1) Something already on screen changing state. Symmetrical and quiet — it draws no attention to itself.

Motion roles

These three are the layer you use. Each pairs a duration with the curve that belongs to it, so a state change and a camera move can never be given each other's timing by accident. Write the role into a transition shorthand; the property list is yours, the timing is not.

TokenValueUse
--hbm-motion-state --hbm-dur-1 + --hbm-ease-settle Colour, border and background changes on a control that stays where it is.
--hbm-motion-unfurl --hbm-dur-3 + --hbm-ease-unfurl Menus, toasts and anything else that grows out of a point you can see.
--hbm-motion-travel --hbm-dur-4 + --hbm-ease-unfurl The world canvas, the you-are-here marker, and the app shrinking as a drawer opens. 
.my-page-demo {
  transition: border-color var(--hbm-motion-state),
              background var(--hbm-motion-state);
}

One role per element

Never mix two roles in one transition list. If part of an element settles while another part travels, the element is doing two jobs and should be two elements.

State motion in place

The commonest motion in PlantPal is the smallest: a control acknowledging you without moving. Press the action below. It changes colour, becomes its own reversal for a few seconds, and returns — the button never leaves its position, and nothing around it reflows.

Hovering an edge shows the same principle from the other side: the rule under it takes the accent, and its arrow steps one space along the direction it will take you. That step is the shortest promise the system makes, and it is made before you commit.

Unfurling from an origin

Anything that appears over the page grows from the point that opened it, at --hbm-motion-unfurl. It does not fade in from nowhere, and it does not slide in from an edge of the screen it has no relationship with. Open the menu below and watch where it starts.

Travel motion — the world canvas panning, the marker following it — belongs to --hbm-motion-travel and is demonstrated where it lives, on Traversal and World & nodes. Do not rebuild a travelling camera on any other page.

Reduced motion

When the reader has asked their device for reduced motion, all four durations collapse to a single millisecond at the token layer. Nothing else changes: transitions still run, so no property is left mid-way; direction arrows are still drawn; the you-are-here marker is still redrawn at its new position; the focus ring still appears. The reader arrives at exactly the same picture, without the journey.

One thing is switched off rather than shortened — the looping shimmer on .hbm-growing, which would otherwise repeat forever at a millisecond. It becomes a still, dimmed block that still says this value has not arrived yet.

@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  :root,
  [data-theme="light"] {
    --hbm-dur-1: 1ms;
    --hbm-dur-2: 1ms;
    --hbm-dur-3: 1ms;
    --hbm-dur-4: 1ms;
  }
}

Because the collapse happens in tokens.css, you get it by using a role. A duration written by hand on a page would survive the query and keep moving for a reader who asked it not to — which is the whole reason hand-written durations are not allowed here.

Checking your work

Read herbarium.reducedMotion() if a behaviour genuinely needs to know. Never use it to substitute a different animation — the answer is always less motion, never other motion.

Accessibility

Reduced motion is honoured everywhere. The durations collapse system-wide, and no component reintroduces one. If you add a page-scoped diagram, give it a motion role or no transition at all.

Nothing moves for longer than three quarters of a second. The longest role is --hbm-motion-travel at 700ms, well inside the five seconds after which movement must be pausable. Nothing in the system moves on a loop except the loading shimmer, which stops under reduced motion.

Motion never carries meaning alone. The arrow on an edge, the halo on the focal node and the accent on an active edge are all drawn statically as well as animated, so a reader who sees no movement still sees direction, focus and state. Colour is never the only signal either — see Colors.

Focus is never animated away. The focus ring appears at full strength immediately and follows the keyboard, not the camera. When the world travels, focus stays on the element the reader chose.

Arrivals are announced, not just shown. The toast rail is a polite live region, so a change that travelled into view is also read aloud with the view it came from. Motion is the visual half of that announcement, never the whole of it.

Usage guidance

Confine motion to the thing that is actually changing. A single value still loading is a single quiet block; a whole sheet set shimmering tells the reader the page is broken when only one number is late.
