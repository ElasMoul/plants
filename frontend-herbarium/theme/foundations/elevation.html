Foundations

Elevation

Herbarium has three lifts and no more. A lift says one thing: this surface is sitting a millimetre off the mount, not printed on it.

Stable v1.0.0

What elevation means here

Think of a herbarium sheet on a mounting board. The sheet is real paper, so it casts a shadow — a short, soft one, close to the paper. That shadow is the whole of this system's elevation vocabulary. It tells you a surface is separate from the ground behind it, and nothing else.

Use a lift when a surface genuinely sits above another surface: a mounted sheet on the page ground, a menu over the sheet, a drawer over the app. Do not use a lift to say something is important, urgent or new. Importance is carried by the type scale on Typography and by colour role on Colors; a shadow that means “look here” would compete with both and lose.

Every lift is paired with a stacking layer. A surface that lifts must also be placed on the right layer, or it will draw above or below its own shadow's promise.

The lift scale

Three steps, plus --hbm-lift-0 for surfaces that are printed flat on the mount. Use the scale directly; there is no fourth step, and a shadow you compose yourself is not allowed.

PrimitiveReads asUsed for
--hbm-lift-0 Flat on the mount Anything ruled or filled rather than raised — tags, callouts, log entries, inputs.
--hbm-lift-1 A sheet on a board The specimen sheet and the world frame. The default for a card you can see the page behind.
--hbm-lift-2 Held just above Menus and toasts — short-lived surfaces that will close again.
--hbm-lift-3 Lifted clear The drawer, and the shrunk app card it opens beside. The deepest lift the system has.

Semantic roles

Components reference the role, never the primitive step. If you are choosing between two roles, you are choosing which layer your surface belongs to — answer that first and the lift follows.

RoleResolves toStacking layerApplied by
--hbm-lift-sheet --hbm-lift-1 --hbm-z-world .hbm-sheet
--hbm-lift-raised --hbm-lift-2 --hbm-z-menu, --hbm-z-toast .hbm-menu, .hbm-toast
--hbm-lift-drawer --hbm-lift-3 --hbm-z-drawer .hbm-drawer, .hbm-exit__app

The paper and dusk themes carry different shadow colours: paper casts a warm, softer shadow than dusk does, because a shadow on bone-coloured card is not the same shadow as one in a potting shed at evening. You never write either — you write the role, and the theme resolves it.

Lift one — the sheet

This is the lift you will use most. A sheet is raised enough that its edge is legible against the mount, and no more.

Everything about the sheet's anatomy lives on Sheet. What matters here is that you do not raise it further to make it stand out.

Lift two — held above

A menu or a toast is a surface you are holding above the page for a moment. It lifts more than a sheet because it will go away again, and it must not be mistaken for part of the page underneath.

Lift three — the drawer

The deepest lift is reserved for leaving the app: the app shrinks to a card and the drawer opens beside it. Both surfaces carry the drawer lift, because both are now off the mount together.

Never put a traversal inside a drawer. A drawer is a place you step aside to, not a place you travel from — see Traversal.

Applying a lift

Reference the role from your own rule. Do not compose a shadow, and do not add a second one on hover — hover changes border and background, never height.

.my-page-demo {
  background: var(--hbm-bg-raised);
  border-radius: var(--hbm-radius-sheet);
  box-shadow: var(--hbm-lift-raised);
  z-index: var(--hbm-z-menu);
}

If a surface needs a lift the scale does not have, the surface is on the wrong layer. Reopen that question rather than inventing a fourth step.

Accessibility

A shadow is not information. Nobody using a screen reader, a high-contrast mode or a forced-colours mode can perceive elevation, and low-vision users on a bright screen often cannot either. So elevation never carries meaning alone.

Three rules follow, and they are not optional:

  • Every lifted surface also carries a hairline border, so its boundary survives when the shadow does not render. .hbm-sheet, .hbm-menu, .hbm-toast and .hbm-drawer all do this already.
  • Contrast is measured against the surface behind, not against the shadow. A lift adds no contrast; check text on --hbm-bg-raised exactly as you would on --hbm-bg-sheet.
  • Layer order must match reading order. A drawer that draws above the app must also come after it in the document, so the keyboard path and the screen-reader path agree with what the eye sees.

Focus is never suppressed by elevation. The focus ring uses --hbm-focus-width and --hbm-focus-offset, and it is drawn outside the lifted surface's edge at every layer. Announce a lifted surface by its role and its name — a drawer titled “Settings” announces as Settings, not as a raised panel. The lift itself is never spoken.

Under prefers-reduced-motion the lifts are unchanged. Elevation is static in this system: a surface does not rise on hover, and nothing animates its shadow, so there is nothing to reduce. Motion is handled on Motion.

Usage guidance

Use one lift per stack. Two raised surfaces on top of each other read as a pile, and a pile has no top.
