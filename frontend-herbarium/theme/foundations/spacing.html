Spacing

Herbarium leaves room around things the way a mounting sheet does. Every gap, pad and inset in the system comes from one eleven-step scale, and nothing is spaced by eye.

Stable v1.0.0

Overview

Space is geometry, so it uses the scale directly. Write var(--hbm-space-5) wherever you need a gap; there is no second layer of role names on top of the steps, and there is no semantic space token to look for.

Use the scale for every gap, padding, inset and offset. Do not use it for type size, radius, rule width or duration — those are their own scales, on Typography, Elevation and Motion.

A value that is not on the scale is not allowed, including a calc() that lands between two steps. If a layout seems to need a step that does not exist, the layout is wrong — pick the nearer step and note the gap in your report.

The scale

Eleven steps on a 4px base. The first four steps are close together because they space things inside a component; from step 5 upward the steps open out, because they separate one thing from another.

TokenValueUse
--hbm-space-0 0 Collapsing a margin the browser gave you. The only zero you should write.
--hbm-space-1 0.25rem A hairline gap — a label and the value under it, the nudge an edge arrow makes on hover.
--hbm-space-2 0.5rem Icon to text inside an action or badge; the punched hole on a plant tag; a focus halo.
--hbm-space-3 0.75rem The default gap between siblings in a row, and the vertical padding of an action.
--hbm-space-4 1rem Padding inside a tag or callout; the standing gap between stacked blocks.
--hbm-space-5 1.5rem Padding inside a sheet or drawer; the gap between two cards in a bed.
--hbm-space-6 2rem Between one documented block and the next; the indent of a care-log entry.
--hbm-space-7 3rem The mount margin down the start edge of a sheet, and the travel distance of an arriving toast.
--hbm-space-8 4rem Between top-level sections of a view.
--hbm-space-9 6rem The breathing room around a day-zero world that has nothing in it yet.
--hbm-space-10 8rem Trailing space at the foot of a long view, so the last entry is not against the edge.

Rhythm in a stack

Vertical rhythm comes from .hbm-stack, which puts --hbm-space-4 between its children. Use it for anything read top to bottom: a garden of sheets, the facts on a tag, a run of callouts. Never set a margin on the children yourself — one owner of the gap means the rhythm survives when an item is added or removed.

When the items belong to one another closely enough to read as a single object — a name and its binomial, a count and its label — add .hbm-stack--tight, which drops the gap to --hbm-space-2.

Rhythm in a row

Horizontal groups use .hbm-row, which sets --hbm-space-3 and wraps. That gap is close enough to read as one cluster and wide enough that two actions are never mistaken for one control.

Beds and mount margins

A grid of plants is a .hbm-bed: columns fill themselves from the available width and the gap is --hbm-space-5, so a garden of three tags and a garden of thirty are spaced identically.

The mount margin

.hbm-margin indents content by --hbm-space-7 on the start edge — the blank strip a herbarium sheet keeps for its annotations. Use it when annotated material sits beside a body of text; on the sheet itself the ruled version, .hbm-sheet--mounted, draws the same margin as a rule. See Sheet.

Writing space in page CSS

Reference the step, never the value it resolves to, and use logical properties so a right-to-left garden mirrors without a second rule. See RTL & locale.

.my-page-demo {
  padding-inline-start: var(--hbm-space-7);
  margin-block-end: var(--hbm-space-6);
  gap: var(--hbm-space-3);
}

Not on the scale

padding: 18px, margin-left: var(--hbm-space-5) and gap: calc(var(--hbm-space-4) + 2px) are all rejected: the first is off the scale, the second breaks under RTL, the third invents a step.

Accessibility

Every step is expressed in rem, so the whole system grows with the reader's text size. Never convert a step to pixels to hold a layout still — a garden at 200% text should get taller, not tighter.

Interactive controls carry their own padding from the scale, which is what gives them a comfortable touch target: .hbm-act pads at --hbm-space-3 by --hbm-space-5, and .hbm-act--icon is a square target. Do not shrink an action below .hbm-act--sm, and never pack two actions closer than the --hbm-space-3 a row gives them — adjacent targets that touch are mis-tapped by someone standing at a windowsill holding a watering can.

Focus needs room to be seen. The focus ring is drawn outside the element at --hbm-focus-offset, so a control clipped by a parent with no padding loses part of its ring. Give any scrolling or overflow-hidden container at least --hbm-space-2 of padding.

Space is not an accessible label. A gap can group things visually, but a screen reader hears only the markup — keep related controls inside one landmark or list, and give a group its own heading or aria-label rather than relying on the space around it.

Usage guidance

Do

Let one container own the gap, so every row in my garden is spaced the same and stays that way when a button is added.

Don't

Leave siblings to fall wherever the browser puts them, which spaces one screen differently from the next.

Hold a label and its value a full step apart, which reads as two separate facts.

Space carries meaning here: proximity is the only thing telling a reader which label belongs to which value, so spend the scale on grouping before you spend it on emphasis. The principle behind this is stated in full in the six principles.
