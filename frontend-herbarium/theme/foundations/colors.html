Foundations

Colors

Herbarium's colour is a pressed-plant palette — paper, ink, leaf, terracotta — held in two layers. You reference the semantic layer and never the primitives beneath it.

Stable v1.0.0

Overview

Colour in PlantPal has one job: to say what something is, not to decorate it. A leaf mark tells you how a plant is doing. A terracotta fill tells you an action will change your garden. A dashed, hatched region tells you the app has not loaded that part of the world yet — which is not the same as it being empty.

Use colour when the meaning is already in the domain: health, confidence, tone, traversal, honesty. Do not use colour to rank two things that are equally important, to brand a screen, or to carry a meaning that has no other cue. Every colour in this system is paired with shape, text or position, so a person who cannot separate two hues still reads the page correctly.

Colour is the strictest part of the token contract. The six principles that set that strictness are in PRINCIPLES.md.

Two layers, and only one you may use

Layer 1 is primitives: raw families and steps such as --hbm-leaf-400. They exist so a theme can be re-pointed in one place. Never name one in a component, a page, or a demo.

Layer 2 is semantics: what a primitive means in this product — --hbm-accent, --hbm-health-watch, --hbm-text-secondary. This is the only layer you reference. The reason is the second theme: paper and dusk re-declare every semantic token and none of the primitives, so a page written against a primitive keeps its daylight colour in the evening and quietly breaks.

/* Correct — the role, which both themes redeclare. */
.my-page-demo { color: var(--hbm-text-secondary); }

/* Wrong — a primitive. This does not follow the theme. */
.my-page-demo { color: var(--hbm-paper-300); }

The one exception is a swatch in the tables below, whose whole purpose is to show a primitive.

Surfaces and ink

Surfaces stack from the world behind everything to the wells cut into it. Ink sits on top in three weights, plus one reserved for Latin binomials, which are always secondary because a beginner is never required to read them.

Accent, growth and borders

Terracotta is the resident's own voice: the fill on a primary action, the edge you are currently travelling, the focal node. Use one accent fill per view. Chlorophyll green is the affirmative — something grew, something was logged, something is fine.

--hbm-accent-ink paper-50 Text drawn on an accent fill. Never pair accent with body ink.

--hbm-focus terra-500 The focus ring. It is never removed, at any scale.

Health, confidence and tone

These roles come straight from PlantPal's domain, so you never choose them by eye — you map the value the app already holds. Health has five steps from thriving to lost. Confidence has three rungs, and low confidence is drawn in cool sill blue rather than red, because being unsure is honest, not an error. The full behaviour of both lives on Health & confidence.

TokenValue (paper)Use
--hbm-health-thriving leaf-500 Growing well since the last scan.
--hbm-health-stable leaf-400 Nothing has changed, and nothing needs to.
--hbm-health-watch chlorosis-500 Needs attention — the yellowing that asks you to look.
--hbm-health-ailing necrosis-400 Something is wrong now.
--hbm-health-lost ink-200 The plant did not survive. Grey, never red.
--hbm-confidence-high leaf-500 Three rungs — the identification is firm.
--hbm-confidence-medium chlorosis-500 Two rungs — likely, worth a second photo.
--hbm-confidence-low sill-500 One rung — say so plainly and pair it with a hedge.
--hbm-positive leaf-500 Something succeeded.
--hbm-caution chlorosis-500 Worth knowing before you act; also the annotated region on a plate.
--hbm-danger necrosis-500 Harm, or a destructive action. Reserved — it loses meaning if spent.
--hbm-info sill-500 Windowsill blue — the hedge, and the offline bar.

World, traversal and honesty

The atlas layer has its own colours. An edge is a drawn rule; the edge you are travelling turns accent. You-are-here is a single marker with a halo. Unknown is hatched and dashed in a muted paper tone — never a blank surface, because a blank surface reads as "you have no plants" when the truth is "we have not loaded this yet." How these states behave is on Uncertainty.

TokenValue (paper)Use
--hbm-edge ink at 22% A traversable edge, drawn before it is taken.
--hbm-edge-active terra-500 The edge being travelled right now.
--hbm-edge-dim ink at 10% An edge that exists but is out of reach from here.
--hbm-node-focus terra-500 The one focal node. There is never a second.
--hbm-marker terra-500 You-are-here, in the world and in the minimap.
--hbm-unknown paper-600 Not loaded yet. Always paired with a dashed border and hatch.
--hbm-empty ink-100 Loaded, and genuinely nothing here.
--hbm-stale chlorosis-600 Shown from an old scan, with its age in words.
--hbm-offline sill-500 Your garden is readable; changes will send later.

Themes

There are two: paper, the herbarium sheet under windowsill light, and dusk, a potting shed at evening. Pages ship as paper via data-theme="light" on the html element; the sidebar toggle switches them.

Both themes redeclare every semantic token, so a role means the same thing in either one — --hbm-caution is the colour that asks you to look, whichever light you are reading in. Check any new colour work in both before you finish; that is the fastest way to catch a primitive that slipped into a page.

Accessibility

Body ink on any surface, and every semantic text role except --hbm-text-muted, meets at least 4.5:1 in both themes. --hbm-text-muted is used only at label and caption weight for supporting text that repeats information available elsewhere; never put a sentence a person must read in it.

Colour never carries a meaning alone. Health is a leaf mark plus its name. Confidence is three drawn rungs plus a word. Unknown is a hatch plus a dashed border plus a sentence. A destructive action is danger-toned and says what it will remove. If you remove the text from any of these and the state becomes ambiguous, the composition is wrong.

The focus ring is --hbm-focus at --hbm-focus-width with --hbm-focus-offset, and it clears both the sheet and the mount in both themes. Never set outline: none, and never re-tint the ring to match a surface.

Announce state in text, not in colour, for screen readers. A leaf mark is decorative; the word beside it is the announcement. Where a mark stands alone, add the state with .hbm-visually-hidden rather than relying on a title attribute.

<span class="hbm-health hbm-health--watch">
  <span class="hbm-health__leaf"></span>
  <span class="hbm-visually-hidden">Needs attention</span>
</span>

Usage guidance

Use one accent fill per view: the single thing the person came to do. A second primary action makes both look optional. Reserve danger for harm and destruction — a plant that did not survive is grey, not red, because losing a plant is sad, not an emergency.

Do
Ailing Lost

Name the state beside its colour, and keep a lost plant grey so that danger still means something when a real harm appears.

Don't
Ailing Lost

Flatten two different states into one alarm colour — the person cannot tell what still needs their care.

Do

Spend the accent once, so the primary action is found without reading either label.

Don't

Fill two actions with the accent — neither one reads as the thing to do next.
